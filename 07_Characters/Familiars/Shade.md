## 1. Summary

Jason's shadow familiar, a formal, dry astral being who is one of the Reaper's shadows. He first appears as the keeper of the Reaper trials, becomes Jason's closest and most constant companion, and acts as his butler, driver, spy and sounding board. He says himself that he is a shepherd of death, and that he has changed more than the others of his kind.

## 2. Facts

- **The invigilator**: he appears as the shadowy figure who runs the trials of the City of Fallen Echoes and offers to explain their practical dangers or their origins (Book 2, Ch44 L65). He hands out vials and medallions with his shadowy hands, which are cold to the touch (Book 2, Ch44 L119).
- **A familiar before**: he tells the competitors that the grandmaster who built the place was an outworlder with the dark essence like Jason, "I was his summoned familiar once" (Book 2, Ch44 L93).
- **Summoned by Jason**: when Jason chooses to summon a familiar after the trials, he greets Shade with "I was hoping it would be you" (Book 2, Ch72 L28).
- **Bodies**: he appears as three bodies that become horses for a ride (Book 3, Ch5 L80) or large lizards for rough terrain (Book 3, Ch60 L8), and his bodies move among monsters as elusive shadowy figures (Book 3, Ch49 L45).
- **The Reaper's child**: the Reaper says "Of all my children, Shade has ever followed his own path" (Book 3, Ch69 L306).

## 3. Later developments, Books 4 to 9

<!-- level:2 -->
- **Manner**: formal, dry and discreet. He objects to "a decidedly indiscreet practice in the middle of the day" (Book 4, Ch9 L21), is told by Jason to "please show our guests out" (Book 4, Ch45 L81), and is described by Jason as "very nice" and "a good friend, even if he does occasionally keep things from me" (Book 4, Ch32 L21).
- **The Reaper's view**: he tells Jason, "You have a habit of not staying dead. This is not something the Reaper likes. You have its gratitude, however" (Book 4, Ch3 L28).
- **Vehicles**: he drives as a sports car and then a sleek four-door sedan (Book 4, Ch5 L33), and drives a motorhome onto the water (Book 4, Ch24 L33). Jason calls him "the single greatest thing on this planet" when he arrives as the car (Book 4, Ch3 L83).
- **A voice**: his voice can come from Jason's shadow, as when he speaks for Jason in a meeting (Book 4, Ch46 L78), and Jason can channel his sight and voice through him (Book 4, Ch18 L40). Jason compares talking through him to a better phone (Book 4, Ch50 L76).
- **Silver rank**: his bodies can teleport between each other (Book 5, Ch16 L103-108).
- **Brother**: Umber trapped him in an astral space for centuries (Book 8, Ch55 L85-86); see `../Umber.md`.

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Nature**: Shade tells Jason that astral beings are things of spirit with defined natures, and that Colin was born an avatar of hunger, Gordon a herald of annihilation and he himself a shepherd of death (Book 13, Ch981 L37).
- **Familiars develop personalities**: he says astral beings are uniform until physical reality gives them idiosyncrasies, "that is the gift that mortals give us", and that Jason has shaped Colin and Gordon "like a parent" (Book 13, Ch981 L51).
- **A falling out**: his previous summoner severed their bond and bound him to the place where he and Jason met, with Umber's interference (Book 13, Ch981 L33-35). Shade explains that Umber fixed on the idea that changing from the original state means falling from grace (Book 13, Ch981 L62).
- **Sanctioning himself**: Shade spends authority accumulated over tens of thousands of years, becomes a Shadow of the Hegemon, and accepts avatar status, which makes the familiar contract permanent (Book 11, Ch68 L2205-2218). The Reaper warns "Sanctioning can so easily go wrong" (Book 11, Ch68 L2220).
- **Vehicles in the astral kingdom**: his black skimmer is free of colour, and letting Colin and Gordon influence it would add defensive properties (Book 12, Ch9 L7).
- **Size**: Jason's gold-rank sheet lists Shadow of the Hegemon as three bodies at iron, seven at bronze, thirty-one at silver and two hundred and eleven at gold (Book 12, Ch67 L229-232).

## 5. Using this in a story

Shade is the straight man to Jason's jokes and the one who says the sensible thing in the plainest words. He is loyal but not transparent; he keeps secrets for reasons he considers good, and he is not afraid to let Jason be wrong. His speech is formal and unhurried. He can carry Jason, his luggage, his voice and his mana-draining attacks, so a story that removes him leaves Jason without a driver, a go-between and an informant at once.
