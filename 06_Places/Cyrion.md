## 1. Summary

Cyrion is the capital of Estercost and, even discounting its farmland, the largest city in the world. From the air it stretches to the horizon in every direction; "Cyrion looked like Tokyo by way of the Emerald City of Oz" from the air, with massive lakes and whole forests inside the urban sprawl (Book 12, Ch48 L22). Book 7 calls it home to the Adventure Society's continental council, though Book 6 puts that council in Vitesse (Book 7, Ch43 L58; Book 6, Ch47 L604).

## 2. Location and layout

- A series of concentric walls rings the city proper, with vast spans of agricultural land in between, "some of the most magical and valuable growing land on the planet" and under "impressive protection" (Book 12, Ch48 L3).
- It is closer than Vitesse to the site of the operation that became a debacle of magical explosions, summoned messengers and unexpected outworlders (Book 7, Ch43 L58).
- Beyond the outermost wall lie the remains of the Builder cult's attack from before the messenger invasion: the toppled, overgrown forms of the Builder's world engineer golems, which rival diamond-rank monsters in size and look more like hills than engines of war (Book 12, Ch48 L4-5, "almost two decades after their demise"); the messengers had made no attempt to invade Cyrion (Book 12, Ch48 L4).

## 3. Society and rule

- Seat of the Estercost crown (the monarch is a queen in Book 12, Ch51 L13; her name was not found) and, in Book 7, the Adventure Society's continental council (Book 7, Ch43 L58; Book 6, Ch47 L604 places it in Vitesse, see Open_Questions 22).
- Cyrion's laws "were very much built to favour the aristocratic families, and house guards were technically a form of indentured servitude. Very well-paid servitude"; many noble houses use that power to keep guards from leaving (Book 12, Ch48 L53).

## 4. Magic level

High, with magical farmland. Not rated separately in the notes.

## 5. History

- Before the series: the capital of Estercost.
- Book 6: the Society's analytical office tracks events involving Jason from here.
- Book 12: Jason visits to prepare for the return to Earth, studying more than a decade of Clive's work on restoring the bridge between Pallimustus and Earth; the Cyrion branch director is Jacinta Adeline. The noble houses' abuse of indenture law triggers the System withdrawal.

## 6. Notable people

Jacinta Adeline (branch director), the noble houses, the Society's continental council, Clive Standish (bridge research).

## 7. Customs and daily life

A city of sectors: agricultural, noble, administrative. Its sheer size means commerce and law matter more than adventuring.

## 8. Dangers and opportunities

Political and legal: indenture law, noble rivalries, guarded farmland. Opportunities: archives, the council, the bridge research.

## 9. Hooks

- A newcomer petitions the continental council.
- A farmland dispute between houses.
- A house guard wants out of an indenture.

## Later developments (spoilers)

In Book 12 Jason announces that the System will be withdrawn from regions that operate with slavery, indentured servitude or similar legal systems; the Queen of Estercost and others ask for arbitration. Cyrion's noble houses using indenture to keep their guards is the background; the text does not state a single trigger.

## Open questions

- The Queen of Estercost is not named in the chapters read (Book 12, Ch51 L13). Duke Percival Headingway, patriarch of House Headingway, holds a title from Cyrion (Book 12, Ch53 L3).
