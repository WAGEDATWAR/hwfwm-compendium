## 1. Summary

A guild is an adventuring organisation that recruits, trains and backs its members and takes on a share of the Adventure Society's work. Guilds carry weight in the Society's surge quotas and in politics. Great families have their own guilds or schools; some local powers (the Aristocratic Faction) deliberately avoid personal power.

## 2. Burning Violet

Separate files in `Guilds/`: [Burning_Violet.md](Guilds/Burning_Violet.md), [Sapphire_Crown.md](Guilds/Sapphire_Crown.md), [Guild_Quotas_and_Contribution.md](Guilds/Guild_Quotas_and_Contribution.md) and [Team_Shining_Scabbard.md](Guilds/Team_Shining_Scabbard.md).

- **Where and what**: an elite guild whose house is a large but unassuming building directly across from Remore Academy in Vitesse (Book 12, Ch43 L64-68). It moved there after Roland Remore, a diamond-ranker, took over the guild long ago; he no longer manages it but his presence looms large (Book 12, Ch43 L64).
- **Relationship to the Academy**: after Rufus's grandfather became guild leader it became more associated with the Remore Academy (Book 6, Ch47 L125-129). Members are Rufus's family plus allies such as Gary and Farrah; most prestige-family graduates do not join, so it is mostly graduates without power or money. Farrah introduces herself in Rimaros as Burning Violet out of Vitesse (Book 7, Ch7 L53-54).
- **Standards** (Book 12, Ch44 L26-30): "elite amongst elites", yet not prejudicial about background; large enough to need head-breakers as well as politicians and ritualists. The staff includes the receptionist Monica; a screener tells Jason that one star at gold rank is not disqualifying.
- **Application** (Book 12, Ch45 L14): the chief membership officer, Neiman Bassingthwaite, tells Jason his membership "threatens to be the flashpoint that could set the whole guild ablaze": people opposed to the Remore family's control of the guild see Roland looming over it, and Jason's closeness to the family and the Adventure Society's treatment of him as a diamond-ranker would inflame them. Gabriel Remore complains that his father treats the guild as a personal fiefdom. Roland meets Jason, tells him that to move in their circles he must "move with consideration rather than emotion", and Jason answers that power rules. Humphrey then tosses a pouch of guild pins on the table and announces that Team Biscuit withdraws from the guild. Whether Roland deliberately arranged the interview as a lesson is not stated in the passages I read.

## 3. The Sapphire Crown

The royal family's guild in Rimaros (Book 7, Ch21 L11). The royal family support it and many royals are among its most capable members; in return every non-royal member must periodically serve in the royal guard. Membership is stringent, mostly from guild families; all members are examined quarterly, and while serving in the guard that rises to monthly or weekly. The royal family does not shield it from surge quotas: Trenchant Moore, a gold-ranker not currently serving in the guard, is responsible for a good portion of its quota. Power, not legacy, is the ultimate authority: Liara, a gold-ranker, commands more respect in the Crown and the Society than the silver-rank princess Vesper.

## 4. House Irios

The pre-eminent producer of defensive engineering in the Storm Kingdom (Book 7, Ch18 L70): the defences of many cities and fortress towns, including Rimaros, are designed and maintained by the house. Its artificers take pride in the family, and Irios family members control Livaros's defences when it is attacked. Zara was matched with a member of the family (the cause of the scandal in Book 7, Ch18 L66-72). See `06_Places/Rimaros.md`.

## 5. Remore Academy

A school in Vitesse run by Roland Remore, the diamond arch-chancellor, who is rarely seen on campus but whose presence is felt (Book 3, Ch69 L587-591). Rufus, Farrah and others are graduates; connections only open the door to the prestigious guilds, and the Academy gives its graduates the skills to walk through it (Book 6, Ch47 L125-131).

## 6. The Aristocratic Faction of Yaresh (Level 2)

Allayeth explains in Book 10 (Ch14 L33-35): a collection of wealthy aristocrats and a few very wealthy merchant barons who own most of the land in the region and provide most of the jobs. They use that influence to co-opt tenants and workers into ideologies, building a loud base that supports policies that keep it poor and ignorant. Distinctively, they have focused on power structures "completely divorced from personal power": no adventurers, no magical researchers, only money and political influence. They promote the idea that money and influence matter more than personal power and will undercut the city authorities and blame others for the consequences. Gormanston Bynes is a prominent member. In his own thoughts (Book 10, Ch47 L60-62) he has concluded that the faction is weak, foolish and oblivious to its path to self-destruction and that the ship must be drydocked and burned to ashes so a new one can be built. His niece Juniper reached silver rank at twenty-two during the surge. His son Calcifer Bynes is Director of the Office of External Affairs, insists on the style "Lord Bynes", and runs afoul of Arabelle and Jason.
