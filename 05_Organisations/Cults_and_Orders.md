## 1. Summary

Four hidden organisations run through the books. The **Order of the Reaper** is an ancient order of assassins that churches destroyed. The **Builder cult** serves a great astral being and uses star seeds and clockwork cores. The **Order of Redeeming Light** is a Purity zealot order that is in fact the work of Disguise. The **Church of Undeath** serves the corruption of the cycle itself.

## 2. The Order of the Reaper

- **What it was** (Book 2, Ch25 L73-74; Book 3, Ch31 L25-27): an ancient cult of assassins who venerated the Reaper, "the finality of death". Emir says they were "scoured from this world, root and branch, by a coalition of churches" (Book 2, Ch25 L73). Shade explains (Book 2, Ch44 L99-109) that the churches, locked out of the sealed astral space, made a second bargain with the vorger, deep-astral entities that can violate dimensional boundaries; the vorger have no physical substance, appear in swarms and warp the bodies of those they affect until the city gains another flesh abomination. Shade says the Order served as royal assassins ("a knife in the dark can be the deliverance of nations"), was accepted by the nations, the Adventure Society, the Magic Society and most churches, and was also a hunter of the undead: "more necromancers fell to the Order than princes or kings"; the church of the Unliving and another church formed "an unholy compact" to remove it (Book 2, Ch44 L72-78).
- **The last grandmaster** (Book 2, Ch44 L85-89): when the churches found the training facilities beneath Sky Scar Lake, the last grandmaster sent the initiates into a sealed astral space (the City of Fallen Echoes) and scattered the keys, so that someone worthy might one day reclaim what was left. The grandmaster who built the place was an outworlder with the dark essence, "akin to Jason"; Shade was his summoned familiar.
- **Legacy**: the Way of the Reaper martial art and skill books; relics found by Emir. Shade tells Jason the Order "does not maintain a roster of assassins at all" but makes "connections. Quiet allies", matching jobs to people who think them worthwhile, a "volunteer network" whose tests find agents with the right principles (Book 2, Ch60 L120-123). The iron-only entry is from earlier notes and was not rechecked.

## 3. The Builder cult

- **Beliefs and methods**: the Builder recruits cults with gifts of power, uses **star seeds**, constructs such as golems and clockwork animals, and **Clockwork Kings**, whose cores convert essence users (Book 2, Ch25 L37-45; notes, Book 8: clockwork cores alter and take over bodies, so converts cannot use essence abilities because the cores cannot violate the soul; Book 8, Ch33 L18-19).
- **Cult leaders**: Zato, the last silver-ranker left in the cult's local forces (Book 3, Ch7 L89), leads the evacuation of the island stronghold and decamps to the Vane Estate; Timos is an embedded cultist in Greenstone (Book 2, Ch59 L87). The cult's attempt to summon a clockwork king failed and instead "summoned some outworlder lunatic" (Book 2, Ch37 L31; Book 3, Ch51 L105; Book 3, Ch60 L38). The Builder possesses Dougall's body, which breaks down if it uses more than silver-rank power (Book 3, Ch51 L91), and later Thadwick Mercer's (Book 3, Ch61 L3; Book 3, Ch67 L6). In Book 10 Beaufort heads the Cult of the Builder's forces in Cardinas and is Lorenn's uneasy ally (Book 10, Ch65 L30): a gold-ranker who has given up his essence powers and is "some manner of semi-artificial monstrosity", though he looks like a beautiful blond elven man.
- **Church relation**: Purity's church works with the cult and is placed under inquiry by an ecumenical council of the other churches (Book 3, Ch1 L105; Disguise's role is covered in `Gods_and_Churches.md`).

## 4. The Order of Redeeming Light

- **Origin and nature** (Book 10, Ch36 L14-17): created by Disguise, masquerading as Purity; the Flames of Redemption are an extreme modification of lesser vampirism.
- **Effects and tools** (Book 8, Ch33 L18-19): the flames leave members with a distinct lack of imagination and a trend toward linear thinking. Pure servitors are essence users implanted with clockwork cores that the order has "purified" by putting a clockwork king through its rituals; the cores take over their bodies and block essence abilities. The order's principle is to take what is unclean, purify it and turn it into a weapon.
- **Operations**: massacres of isolated fortress towns disguised as monster attacks while adventurers are busy (Book 8, Ch9 L40); recruitment of volunteers; the capture of Gibson Amouz, a cousin of Liara's husband (Book 8, Ch20 L4), whom the Church of the Healer later recovers (Book 9, Ch28 L45).
- **People** (Book 8, Ch18 L3-6): Melody Jain is the overall leader of the order's local operations, with her second Sendira and church adviser Laront (a Purity priest who never passed through the flames, so he can safely visit cities). Cell leaders are silver like their commanders, a source of constant tension; some resent Melody's use of the order for her own ends.

## 5. The Church of Undeath (Level 3)

Hana Shavar says the church lies dormant for generations and, when it finally acts, it is like its god has saved up his chances to intervene; expect "dark miracles" (Book 10, Ch78 L38-41). In Cardinas its priests turn the dead into undead; its priests are led by Garth Larosse, a priest of Undeath and a zemravore (an undead that steals living hearts; Book 11, Ch29 L3: his power is the ability to kill his own aura, rendering him invisible to aura senses). Jason's allies defeat the church, and Jason sanctions the avatar of Undeath. See `Gods_and_Churches.md`.

- A priest of Purity discovers the flames are a curse.
- A cult safe house with a clockwork king.
