## 1. Summary

The Cabal is the oldest of Earth's three dominant magical forces: a loose collection of factions of vampires, fae and other creatures older than recorded history, with old money and old habits. It split when ancient vampires were woken and the vampire lords rose. Part of it sides with humanity and, in the end, is openly led by Boris Ket Lundi. Earth_Powers.md covers it briefly; this file is the full account.

## 2. Structure and character

- **The old magic**: "The Cabal represents the old magic of this world. Things older than history that dwell in the dark places" (Book 4, Ch10 L133). It is one of the three dominant forces beside the Network and the EOA, with a tight grip on old money (Book 4, Ch10 L127; Book 4, Ch11 L101).
- **A loose collection**: it "was always a loose collection of factions" (Book 6, Ch25 L61). Its members are creatures of many kinds: a troll-blooded fae who shapeshifts to pass for human in rural pubs (Book 12, Ch53 L58), and contingents that include a thirteen-foot fur-covered humanoid with a mane of mossy hair (Book 13, Ch1001 L98).
- **Concealment**: Dawn says the Cabal's members "have become experts at hiding their presence over the centuries" (Book 5, Ch54 L25). Many believed the angelic founders were a myth (Book 13, Ch954 L4).
- **Its official line on Jason**: Vermillion says it is the Cabal's position that Jason's liberty and independence be respected (Book 4, Ch15 L70).
- **Rivalry with the EOA**: the EOA's research into blood servants causes conflicts with the Cabal, which dislikes having its people kidnapped as research materials (Book 4, Ch11 L78).
- **Help in the monster waves**: individual factions help in places such as Siberia and the South Pacific where the Network is thin (Book 5, Ch24 L32).

## 3. Later developments, Books 5 to 6

<!-- level:2 -->
- **Stronger in the zones**: transformation zones make Cabal members stronger, since many had hit a ceiling in a world not magical enough, and the Cabal starts to approach parity with the Network (Book 5, Ch57 L8).
- **The vampires' problem**: vampires hit a different ceiling and fall into torpor, so their leaders must hibernate. They rush to wake their ancient ones, and the rest of the Cabal "reluctantly went along due to the need to compete with the Network" (Book 5, Ch57 L9-10). Lord Willoughby has been hibernating in a sarcophagus since 1794 (Book 5, Ch57 L16).
- **The attack on Sydney**: the Cabal makes an open assault on the Network building in the Sydney CBD, after which authorities cordon off the area (Book 5, Ch59 L32).
- **Splinters**: Craig Vermillion leads a splinter faction that does not work with the old vampires (Book 5, Ch62 L60), and by Book 6 the Cabal has fragmented. Some have formed a non-vampiric new Cabal and others have joined the Global Defence Network (Book 6, Ch25 L61).
- **The Arisen**: William Spencer, an ancient vampire and one of the Arisen (the vampires' own name), says not all of them want global dominion, and some are looking to carve out a place for themselves after the vampires lose (Book 6, Ch25 L60-64).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Boris's claim**: Boris tells Jason's team "We founded the Cabal" (Book 10, Ch79 L98). Jason's briefing says the magical elements hidden on Earth "ultimately formed the Cabal to keep their secrets" (Book 13, Ch1021 L36).
- **The schism**: the Cabal vampires dug up the old vampires, but most vampires were forced into obedience by bloodline magic or threat, and the Cabal's priority has been freeing them from the risen lords. "The whole mess caused a schism in the Cabal and cleaning house was ugly. We cast out any vampires that sided with the risen lords willingly" (Book 11, Ch42 L42-44). The vampires seized a lot of power in the Cabal, which cost Boris's quiet influence (Book 11, Ch42 L128).
- **Territory**: the Cabal holds some of the UK and Scandinavia (Book 11, Ch42 L40), and displaces Network factions in areas such as Russia and Africa (Book 11, Ch42 L59-65). It has "unambiguously split" from the lords and those who followed them (Book 11, Ch68 L1496).
- **Safe zones**: safe zones for vampires who are not with the lords exist mostly where the Cabal holds sway (Book 11, Ch42 L63).
- **Hierarchy**: Boris stands at the top; a member says to him "you're at least five steps above me in the Cabal hierarchy" (Book 11, Ch68 L524).
- **Under Boris**: openly led by Boris Ketland, the Cabal is "highly aggressive with their economic and social programs": he "toppled governments and cut some of the world's most exploitative business practices off at the knees" (Book 13, Ch1015 L49). Its humanitarian work since going public is far ahead of the UN team's (Book 13, Ch959 L114).
- **The vampire lords**: besides Elizabeth, who rules Venice (Book 11, Ch68 L2256-2261), the books name Wassily, Klaus and Georges (Book 6, Ch40 L18) and Magdalyn, one of the strongest surviving vampire lords (Book 13, Ch1009 L81). They are not part of the Cabal after the schism.

## 5. Using this in a story

The Cabal is the face of old power that has chosen a side. Use its members as grim, courteous, long-lived allies with their own agendas, and remember that the Cabal's help comes with a favour. A character who is "Cabal" can be a vampire, a fae, or a human with old-money ties, and a character who has gone over to the vampire lords is no longer one of them.
