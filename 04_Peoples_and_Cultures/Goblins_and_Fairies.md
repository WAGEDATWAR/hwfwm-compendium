## 1. Summary

Goblins and fairies appear as monsters and as people of Earth transformed by the zones; fairies are also a small hovering manifestation.

## 2. Facts

- **Goblins as monsters**: Jason jokes about killing ten goblins for an uncommon spear in the cannibals' cave (Book 1, Ch4 L26).
- **Transformed humans**: in West Canfield, Detroit, people were "turned into goblins and their homes into underground warrens", which became a lawless combat zone (Book 5, Ch54 L52).
- **Cabal beings**: the Cabal's mythological beings include centaurs, ogres, fairies and more (Book 5, Ch54 L55).
- **Elves, goblins and fairies**: people in a magic city were "turned into elves and goblins and fairies" (Book 6, Ch23 L91).
- **Fairy manifestation**: a small hovering fairy-like figure with an androgynous body bites Clive in Book 2 (Book 2, Ch26 L99).

<!-- level:2 -->
- **Earth beings in the open**: as the Cabal operated in the open, "centaurs, ogres, fairies, and more" were spotted by the media and revealed to the world (Book 5, Ch54 L55). A Network operative expects the Cabal to be "myths and fairy tale creatures" (Book 5, Ch61 L52).
- **Zone-turned people**: Emi says people in a magic city "were all turned into elves and goblins and fairies", so they were let live there (Book 6, Ch23 L91). Jason's cloud city is described as a fairy tale kingdom (Book 6, Ch19 L40), but that is a style, not a people.
<!-- /level -->

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Cabal members in human form**: fifteen years after the revelation the Cabal is often relaxed about masking its nature, "from diminutive fairies to looming ogres"; "when you were nine feet tall, with three-foot antlers, taking human form was just easier" (Book 13, Ch954 L5). The Cabal's members "seemed to have stepped straight out of folklore and fairy tales", which ties them to myriad belief systems (Book 11, Ch68 L1521). See The_Cabal.md.
<!-- /level -->

## 3. Using this in a story

Goblins and fairies are Earth-side cases; no native Pallimustus culture of either is established here.
