## 1. Summary

How faith appears day to day: crowded temple districts with hierarchies of their own, churches that serve as hospitals and archives, and gods whose rules limit what they can do.

## 2. Facts

- **Temple districts**: temples line the road to the Island's Divine Square and "gods had hierarchies of their own": the Temple of Roads sits behind the larger Temple of Journeys (Book 1, Ch45 L4). The square is crowded with booths selling religious paraphernalia and people proselytising (Book 1, Ch45 L5, L9).
- **Dominion**: "Dominion issues the divine right to kings and nobles. It is he who determines who rules, and who serves" (Book 1, Ch45 L18).
- **The Healer**: Neil makes a stand in the Temple of the Healer (Book 1, Ch94 L3); ex-clergy who offended their gods and were stripped of divinely gifted essences suffer a debilitating imbalance in body and soul (Book 4, Ch11 L16); the notes make the Healer the god who does this (notes, Book 1; the Healer is not named in that line).
- **Knowledge's temple**: in Rimaros it is "an important resource for the many magical researchers" with a symbiotic relationship with the Magic Society branch (Book 7, Ch9 L4); the temple is mostly a vast library covering multiple wings (Book 7, Ch9 L9).
- **Churches and research**: churches, especially Knowledge's, help fund the Magical Research Association (Book 12, Ch1 L58).
- **Death's paperwork**: the Church of Death issues certification of how many times a person has died (Book 7, Ch17 L104).
- **Limits on gods**: a god's favour does not mean intervention (Book 8, Ch43 L48-53); a goddess says she cannot see into the astral space where Jason holds prisoners (Book 10, Ch31 L69-75); the gods are "bound by convoluted limitations" (Book 8, Ch48 L22-26).
- **The Hierophant**: the Hierophant of the World-Phoenix is allowed to intervene in Pallimustus a single time (Book 7, Ch60 L27).

## 3. Using this in a story

Scenes in a temple district should have noise, commerce and rival sects; clergy are practical people with real duties.
