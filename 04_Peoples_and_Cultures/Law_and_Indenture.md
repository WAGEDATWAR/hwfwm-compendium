## 1. Summary

Greenstone and Estercost have no slaves, only indentured servants, and indenture is both a sentence and a business; in Book 12 the question of indenture drives the System's withdrawal.

## 2. Facts

- **Indenture, not slavery**: "We don't have slaves. We have indentured servants. A lot of criminals are sentenced to indenture, then their indenture is sold or auctioned to recoup the cost of their crimes" (Book 1, Ch92 L32). Jason finds the system disturbing and says both his world and this one need better systems (Book 1, Ch92 L33-34).
- **Captured enemies**: the Ustei do not accept slavery; "their culture doesn't allow warriors to be made slaves. For them, capture means release or death" (Book 1, Ch92 L36).
- **The Adventure Society agreement**: a clause in the Adventure Society's agreement with Greenstone gives anyone who completes a contract the right of refusal on anyone sentenced to indenture as a result of it (Book 1, Ch105 L66).
- **Hearings**: indenture hearings are a normal legal event (Book 2, Ch1 L77); the Greenstone court "was less like an American legal p[roceeding]" (Book 2, Ch3 L43).
- **House guards**: in Cyrion, house guards are "technically a form of indentured servitude" and the laws "were very much built to favour the aristocratic families" (Book 12, Ch48 L53).
- **Jason's argument**: if a family used "draconian measures" to hold people to cheap work, nothing would stop them (Book 1, Ch80 L78).
- **The System's withdrawal**: the System will withdraw access "in regions of Pallimustus currently operating with slavery, indentured servitude or similar legal systems" (Book 12, Ch50 L28). "The Queen of Estercost and several other interested parties" ask for arbitration over the withdrawal (Book 12, Ch51 L13).
- **Slave markets**: Jason's friend scouts Sadi Andali's slave markets, and Ben asks whether "the destruction of the Sadi Andali slave trade" is Jason's price (Book 12, Ch52 L35-41).

## 3. Using this in a story

Indenture is respectable in Estercost and a flashpoint with Earth-raised outsiders; scenes about it carry real moral weight.
