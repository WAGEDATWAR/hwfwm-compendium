## 1. Conversion

"The basic coin of the realm was the lesser spirit coin. Iron spirit coins were worth a hundred lesser coins ... After that, it was ten iron to the bronze, ten bronze to the silver and ten silver to the gold" (Book 1, Ch61 L65).

| Coin | In lesser coins | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Lesser | 1 | Book 1, Ch61 L65 |
| Iron | 100 | Book 1, Ch61 L65 |
| Bronze | 1,000 (ten iron) | Book 1, Ch61 L65 |
| Silver | 10,000 (ten bronze) | Book 1, Ch61 L65 |
| Gold | 100,000 (ten silver); three gold coins are worth three hundred thousand units | Book 1, Ch61 L65 |

## 2. What each is for

- **Lesser coins** run almost all home and business magic devices such as lamps and showers; higher ranks would burn them out (Book 1, Ch80 L40-41).
- **Iron coins** are used by bulk traders, adventurers and the wealthy elite (Book 1, Ch61 L65).
- **Sustenance**: a bronze coin will sustain bronze-rank adventurers without stressing their bodies (Book 1, Ch15 L92).
- **Larger infrastructure**, such as the loop line, uses more powerful coins (Book 1, Ch80 L41).
- **Prices**: armour for an iron-rank adventurer is priced in bronze coins, which is unusual (Book 1, Ch61 L114).

## 3. Where coins come from

A farm that makes a thousand lesser coins would make only ten iron coins or one bronze coin (Book 1, Ch80 L39). The Magic Society manages production (Book 1, Ch80 L23).
