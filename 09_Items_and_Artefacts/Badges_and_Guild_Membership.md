## 1. Summary

The badge as an object: how an Adventure Society badge is made, tied to its wearer, tracked, altered and replaced, and what a guild membership adds on top. The organisation file explains what the Society does with badges; this file is about the item and the ways it fails.

## 2. Facts

- **Who makes it**: the Adventure Society manages the badges but the Magic Society creates them, taking an aura imprint from the new member inside a magic circle in about a minute (Book 1, Ch62 L46).
- **What it carries**: every badge is paired with a tracking stone that tells the Society whether the member is alive or dead and finds them if they go missing (Book 1, Ch62 L47). Members claim adventure notices with it (Book 1, Ch62 L44) and hold it against a security screen to close a contract (Book 1, Ch107 L51).
- **The wallet**: the badge is a circular iron medallion embossed with a sword and rod crossed over a shield, carried in a leather wallet (Book 1, Ch62 L61).
- **Stars are on the metal**: an official taps a wedge-shaped stone to the badge and a star is added (Book 1, Ch98 L82), or removed (Book 2, Ch12 L83).
- **It tracks the badge, not the person**: the tracking stones trace the badges, so a discarded badge hides its owner (Book 3, Ch35 L43, L45). A change of aura leaves the imprint on the badge unmatched, so the wearer cannot be tracked until a new badge is issued (Book 2, Ch15 L10). When the Society wanted people's auras re-examined, their badges were also to be replaced (Book 2, Ch10 L85).
- **Jason's case**: the stone cannot track his aura imprint, and Farrah says some people have abilities that block magical tracking (Book 1, Ch62 L47-48).
- **A new badge for a changed aura**: when Jason's aura and personal crest change, he is told to present himself to the Magic Society for a new badge (Book 3, Ch24 L89).
- **Using the tracking**: adventurers find dead colleagues by following a crystal orb that points to the badge (Book 1, Ch84 L101; Book 1, Ch87 L39).

## 3. Later developments (spoilers)

- **A diamond in place of stars**: at gold rank in Book 12, Krensler gives Jason a new Adventure Society badge, gold, with a diamond symbol where one, two or three stars would normally be (Book 12, Ch37 L128).
- **The Burning Violet guild**: among his appointments in Vitesse, Jason joins the guild most of his companions belong to (Book 12, Ch43 L62). Its guild house stands across from the Remore Academy (Book 12, Ch43 L64). Applicants are taken by an intake clerk, assessed by an executive in an in-depth interview, and checked for identity late in the process (Book 12, Ch44 L20). The guild executive is divided over his membership, which Lord Bassingthwaite fears could become a flashpoint (Book 12, Ch45 L3, L8, L14).

## 4. Using this in a story

A badge is leverage as much as identification. It can summon help or find a body, and an unmatched badge makes a character untraceable. A character who has changed rank or aura needs a new one from the Magic Society, so a missing or old badge is a reasonable sign of trouble. Guild membership is a separate matter with its own politics; do not treat it as a rank.
