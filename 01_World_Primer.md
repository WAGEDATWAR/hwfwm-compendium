## 1. The setting in one page

The series follows Jason Asano, an ordinary Australian who is pulled out of his world by an accident and arrives, hairless and alone, in **Pallimustus**: a world very like Earth in size and shape, but soaked in magic. There, people absorb **essences** (crystallised magic), which give them supernatural abilities, and grow through **ranks** by hunting **monsters** and training. Adventurers, guilds, gods and rulers all depend on this system.

Pallimustus has a twin, **Earth**, which was made from the same template but has much less magic, so its people do not know magic exists (at first). The two worlds are linked, and the link matters more and more as the story proceeds. Above both worlds sits a larger cosmos of **great astral beings**, **gods**, **astral kings** and other powers who rarely interact directly with mortals but shape their lives.

In a roleplay you can set a story anywhere from the beginning (a rural iron-rank adventurer in Greenstone) to the end (a gold-rank world-defender at the edge of a cosmic war). The chief factors that change from period to period are: who knows what, how powerful the leading characters are, and what the dangers are (a Builder cult, a monster surge, messengers, vampires, Knowledge's plan).

## 2. Two worlds

### 2.1 Pallimustus

Pallimustus is an alternate Earth whose continents are similar but not identical. Key differences: Africa is separate from Eurasia; a large landmass in Southeast Asia pushes into Australia and consumes New Zealand; Iberia and Arabia are missing; Sri Lanka is huge (Book 1, Ch1 L65) and is called Pranay (Book 2, Ch34 L58). The Mediterranean equivalent is the **Gramid Passage**, which joins directly to the Indian Ocean (Book 6, Ch47 L703). It has two moons (Book 1, Ch1 L23). The year has twelve months of thirty days plus five extra days for the solstices, equinoxes and new year, and the year begins in spring (Book 1, Ch22 L19); the week has six days (Book 6, Ch10 L41). Time units (hours, minutes) match Earth's (Book 1, Ch22 L20).

The planet has a thin membrane to the astral, so magic is dense. Features of the world that follow from this:
- **Astral spaces** (pocket dimensions) attached to the planet through apertures such as waterfalls and caves.
- **Natural manifestations**: essences, awakening stones and monsters appear spontaneously.
- **Monster surges**: cyclical events (every ten years, Book 1, Ch31 L71) when magic spikes and monsters pour out. A surge is the world venting excess magic. The series' major surge begins in Book 7, about five years overdue (Book 7, Ch1 L56), which the books connect to a magical link to Earth that had been bleeding Pallimustus off. **(Later: Book 4-7)**
- Animals include **heidels** (horse-sized, scaled, two-headed and horned, Book 1, Ch17 L106) in place of horses.

Because magic is concentrated regionally, **magic level** varies by place. Greenstone is low-magic: "mid-iron" density, which Clive calls very low (Book 3, Ch34 L60). The wiki calls Vitesse much denser (wiki; no book figure), and the place files give what the books say about Rimaros and others. Astral spaces and surges can be higher still. Magic level affects what monsters appear, how quickly people can advance, and even what technology works.

### 2.2 Earth

Earth is the same template with a more rigid dimensional membrane, which the World-Phoenix reinforced billions of years ago, so it has less ambient magic (Book 4, Ch68 L79, L97; Book 4, Ch2 L11). Earth's hidden magical world has three powers:
- **The Cabal**: ancient, secretive, built around old magic and vampires, controlling old money. Its leaders include messengers hiding from other messengers. **(Later: Book 4, 10-11)**
- **The Network**: a coalition of secret societies that uses essences and monster cores. It maintains the **Grid** that detects "dimensional incursions" and keeps Earth's magic in check. It names ranks by category, matched to the spirit-coin colour: category one is iron, two bronze, and a silver-ranker is category three (Book 4, Ch21 L31-33; Book 4, Ch31 L48); it grades events by category as well (Book 4, Ch5 L13-16). It says "luminous" for holy (Book 4, Ch19 L36) and "affinity gems" for quintessence (Book 4, Ch23 L26).
- **The Engineers of Ascension (EOA)**: a technology-and-body-modification faction preparing for the revelation of magic.

Magic has been quietly leaking onto Earth for a long time and "goes public" during the story. By Books 5-6 the Grid fails, monsters pour in, **transformation zones** change cities and their people into other races, and vampires take Europe. **(Later: Book 5-6, 11, 13)**

### 2.3 The link between the worlds

A link made roughly five hundred years ago siphons magic from Pallimustus into Earth: an Earth outworlder who had joined the fake Purity's church returned home with magic Knowledge had bargained from the messengers (Book 13, Ch1017 L19-21). The siphon delays Pallimustus's monster surges, and the delay makes the eventual breakthrough more dangerous (Book 4, Ch68 L131-135). Repairing it is one of Jason's central tasks. Its completion creates a **bridge** that will become a true doorway between the worlds, which is the key to the Book 13 conflict. **(Later: Book 4-13)**

## 3. How magic works (the basics)

### 3.1 Essences

An **essence** is a manifestation of pure magic in the form of a small glass cube or crystal. Absorbing one (by ritual, or simply by holding and consuming it) imbues the person with five related **essence abilities**, one awakened at once and the rest later. A person can hold **four essences**; the fourth usually forms a **confluence** from the first three.

Essences come in rarities (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary), and no essence is simply better than another: a Duck essence in skilled hands can beat a Fox. Essences are named for their concept or animal (Fire, Wind, Blood, Dark, Sin, Dragon, Swift, Rat, Spider, Sword, Technology...). They show up in monster loot, are given by gods, and are collected and traded.

### 3.2 Confluence essences

When three essences are absorbed a **confluence essence** manifests, determined by the combination. In the early books the outcome for a combination is fixed and recorded by the Magic Society (see `03_Magic_and_Adventuring/Confluences.md` for the cites); by Book 12 the fixed combinations "are starting to add variety" (Book 12, Ch10 L91). Examples: Dark + Blood + Sin = Doom (affliction specialist); Earth + Fire + Potent = Volcano; Magic + Might + Wing = Dragon; Swift + Balance + Wind = Mystic. A confluence can be refused, but then cannot be replaced. Some confluences (Death, Corrupt, Sacrifice, Undeath, Succubus...) are **restricted** by the Adventure Society.

### 3.3 Awakening stones

Each essence holds five abilities; absorbing the essence awakens one, and the rest require **awakening stones**, which are consumable crystals. The Magic Society grades a stone one to five stars by how often it appears (see `03_Magic_and_Adventuring/Awakening_Stones.md`); the stone's theme steers what it awakens; the essence matters most, but race, knowledge and personality also influence the result. Gods and astral beings sometimes give stones to followers.

### 3.4 Abilities

Essence abilities come in types: **special attacks**, **spells**, **special abilities**, **auras**, **conjurations**, **summons and familiars** (often needing a ritual) and **rituals** in their own right. Abilities improve in rank as the user advances: an ability that begins as a single effect gains extra effects at each rank (Jason's Leech Bite, for instance, adds new afflictions at each stage). Abilities have mana and stamina costs and sometimes cooldowns. Many abilities inflict **afflictions** (lasting harmful conditions) or grant **boons**; the series gives them specific names.

### 3.5 Attributes

Every person has four base **attributes**: **Power**, **Speed**, **Spirit** and **Recovery**. They rise as essence abilities advance and are the measure of raw ability. A person cannot advance until all attributes have reached the next rank (Book 5, Ch25 L26-27).

### 3.6 Quintessence, spirit coins and other magical materials

- **Quintessence** (called **affinity gems** on Earth) appears as gems of an essence's theme (for example Dark Quintessence Gems, Book 1, Ch6 L15; crafting material, Book 1, Ch7 L68) and is used in crafting.
- **Monster cores** are crystallised magic from monsters. Eating one raises abilities slightly; this is the fast path to advancement but it stalls most people around silver and makes gold very hard.
- **Spirit coins** are the currency of the magical world. They are concentrated magic that a ranked person can consume to sustain themselves. See section 7.
- Other staples: crystal wash (cleaning solution used after monster fights), healing unguents, recovery potions, skill books, dimensional bags.

### 3.7 Rituals, formations and artifice

Beyond essence abilities there is "external magic": **ritual magic** (circles of chalk, ink or silver powder with chants), **formations and arrays** (large-scale rituals such as the Network's Grid), **alchemy** (potions, ointments), **artifice** (magical items, weapons, vehicles), and **skill books**, which impart skills by flooding the reader with text (they require consciousness and are strenuous).

### 3.8 Familiars and summons

A **familiar** is a deliberately created creature bonded to its owner, normally via a ritual. A **summoned familiar** can merge with the summoner and dies with them; a **bonded familiar** survives its owner but cannot vanish into them. Familiars can have their own abilities. Jason's three familiars (Shade, Colin, Gordon) are central to him. Other examples: Stash (Humphrey), Onslow (Clive), Shimmer and Gemini (Belinda).

## 4. Ranks and power

Ranks measure the quality of a person's magic and are a social class system as much as a power scale.

| Rank | Earth term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Normal (unranked) | Category 0 | Ordinary people, including those with some essences |
| Iron | Category 1 | Four essences absorbed (Book 1, Ch15 L100). Digestion changes (Book 2, Ch34 L73); can live on spirit coins; sense auras |
| Bronze | Category 2 | All abilities unlocked and at bronze; much tougher |
| Silver | Category 3 | Can fight through blows that would be lethal to a bronze-ranker (Book 5, Ch1 L38); some abilities allow flight at silver (Book 2, Ch9 L30-31); the usual ceiling for core-users |
| Gold | Category 4 | No need to breathe (Book 2, Ch34 L73); centuries-long life (Book 1, Ch22 L58) |
| Diamond | Category 5 | The peak of mortal power; few exist; rumoured not to die of old age |
| Transcendent | none | Gods and astral beings, effectively unkillable |

Facts that recur: people of a higher rank **suppress** those of lower rank ("tyranny of rank"); essence users age slower: bronze-rankers can live well past a hundred, silvers can double that, gold-rankers live for centuries, and it is rumoured that diamond-rankers cannot die of old age (Book 1, Ch22 L58); ranking up involves a "purge" in which impurities are expelled (often messy) and the body changes; advancing without monster cores is slower but leaves greater potential. **Half-transcendence** means peak diamond combined with transcending mortal nature.

Roles in society: iron and bronze make up most adventurers; silver are elite; gold are rare and politically important; diamonds are sovereign powers ("everyone gets one world-changing action", according to their etiquette). Title and respect follow rank.

## 5. Auras

An **aura** is the physical, intangible projection of a person's soul and will. It grows with rank. People learn to **project**, **retract** and **suppress** it; stronger auras can suppress weaker ones. Auras can carry emotions, blanket areas, reveal lies, or physically move matter at very high levels (messengers do this routinely). Uncontrolled gold-rank auras injure unranked people. Techniques include blending into a crowd (making an aura read as part of the ambient background) and masking (messenger devices that fake or block auras). **Titles** earned from deeds (Giant Slayer, Resolute, Indomitable, Godless Prophet...) alter a person's aura. **Soul crests** (see section 10) provide personal identification.

## 6. Monsters and surges

**Monsters** are magic made sentient without a soul. They manifest spontaneously where magic is dense, last from days to weeks, become more aggressive near the end, and dissolve into **rainbow smoke** leaving loot (cores, coins, materials, ointments, items). Monsters have ranks matching the quality of their magic. They range from hamsters and eels (lesser) to hydras, dragons, kraken and leviathans. Some are intelligent (the Dinosaur King, springclaw gorillas) and some come from specific origins (construct creatures, vorger from the deep astral, apocalypse beasts such as the sanguine horror and the world-taker worm).

Not every creature that looks like a monster is one: **magical creatures** (natural animals with magical traits) and **creatures** (Tartarian brutes, nagas) have souls and leave no rainbow smoke.

Adventurers hunt monsters because monsters left alone grow dangerous, produce loot and cores, and threaten settlements. The Adventure Society posts notices and contracts. Typical dangers: ambushes, tyranny of rank, afflictions, packs. Typical loot rules: usually only the one who kills or the party members get the loot (the System later automates this).

A **monster surge** is the world's pressure release. Magic density spikes, monsters of higher rank manifest, astral spaces open and the Society moves to "surge protocols" (contracts become orders). A surge lasts months. **(Later: Book 6-8)**

## 7. Economy, materials and daily life

- **Spirit coins** exist in lesser, iron, bronze, silver, gold and diamond forms. 100 lesser coins make one iron coin; each higher rank is ten times the last (Book 1, Ch36 L72; Book 1, Ch61 L65), so a gold coin equals about 100,000 lesser coins. A ranked person can eat a coin of their rank to restore resources; eating a higher coin temporarily raises them to that rank, with severe backlash. Coins from Jason bear his face and the words "PRODUCT OF JASON / G'DAY, MATE!" (Book 1, Ch2 L50-53).
- **Spirit coin farms** in low-magic regions are a major industry (Greenstone's biggest export).
- **Lodging, food and travel** vary by region: Greenstone has loop-terminal transit (Book 1, Ch45 L4); wealthy homes have magical plumbing and lighting; Vitesse has living plants on buildings; Rimaros is a city of floating islands.
- **Cloud constructs** (Jason's cloud flask, Emir's cloud palace) are magical buildings and vehicles made from cloud substance.
- **Communication**: the Magic Society's **water link** (Book 2, Ch6 L5) and the **sky link** communication tablets, whose inventor appears in Book 12, Ch14 L19 (the Sky Link Company, Book 12, Ch56 L4). **Transport**: heidel wagons, skyships, skimmers, portals (limited by rank and distances).
- **Daily staples** noticed by the series: crystal wash, healing unguents, strawberry-schnapps-flavoured recovery potions, elf and celestine fashion, tea, stews and sandwiches (Jason's cooking is a recurring feature). A recovery potion tasting of strawberry schnapps appears in Book 1, Ch6 L50, and crystal wash in Book 1, Ch32 L52.

## 8. Peoples (natures)

Each **nature** (race) has exactly six inherent **racial gifts** (Book 1, Ch15 L65) that can evolve with a blessing from a great astral being.

- **Humans**: the most common; faster advancement and special-attack affinity; the only native race of Earth that can use essences.
- **Elves**: spellcasters and healers; life and magic affinity; long-lived.
- **Leonids**: lion-headed, strong and fast (e.g. Gary).
- **Celestines**: metallic hair and eyes, skin from alabaster to dark; astral and holy affinity; not a natural race: two outworlders who have a child produce a celestine (Book 5, Ch44 L72); excellent portal users.
- **Draconians**: scaled, fire-breathing, claim dragon descent.
- **Smoulder**: earth-and-fire folk; the **brighthearts** are a magical sub-species adapted to the deep.
- **Runics**: blue-black skin with glowing runes.
- **Merfolk, goblins, valash** (humans turned into them by transformation zones, Book 9, Ch45 L57), **lehenik** (Nik's kind, from a word for "first", Book 11, Ch23 L42).
- **Outworlders**: people from another world whose souls rebuilt a body. They have unusual gifts (interface, quest system, inventory, map, "mysterious stranger" language adaptation) and cannot be easily tracked.
- **Messengers**: winged, gestalt beings who are not essence-users (see section 9).

## 9. The wider cosmos

### 9.1 The astral and the cosmos

Clive explains the cosmos as **dumpling soup** (Book 1, Ch69 L85): worlds are dumplings in the soup, which is the astral, the source of all magic. Astral spaces are congealed magic attached to a world; the **deep astral** is pure magic where a physical body cannot survive. **Universes** begin as seeds. **Interstice** is the oldest and largest city-world, with administrators and no rulers, where great astral beings do business in physical reality (Book 3, Ch69 L219-222). The cosmos is kept in order by the **Rules of Intrusion** (Book 12, Ch63 L7; "Laws of Intrusion" in Book 13, Ch1017 L44), which govern entry into worlds that have not opened to the cosmic community. **(Later: Book 2-4, 8, 11-12)**

### 9.2 Great astral beings (GABs)

Great astral beings are cosmic-scale transcendents who embody aspects of the cosmos. They operate through **prime vessels** (mortal avatars). Named GABs: the **World-Phoenix** (dimensional integrity, formerly the Boundary), the **Builder** (construction; the post was transferred to a new mortal after the old Builder was sanctioned), the **Reaper** (death's finality), the **Keeper of Moments/Sands** (time), the **All-Devouring Eye** (annihilation), the **Celestial Book** (magic's understanding), **Seeker of Songs**, **Whisper in Corners**, **Legion** (life), the **Word in the Silence**, and other nameless functionaries. They once sat under a **Cosmic Throne** and then sundered it. In Book 11 the throne is left empty as a neutral arbiter (Book 11, Ch65 L8), and in Book 12 Jason learns it "could never be restored to what it was", with an echo of him imprinted on it (Book 12, Ch1 L34). **(Later: Book 11-12)**

### 9.3 Gods

**Gods** are concept-based beings outside physical reality who manifest avatars, speak with Akashic Speech and give followers essences, stones and miracles. Each is bound to a world. Gods named in the books include Knowledge, Healer, Dominion, Hero, Death, Fertility, Hearth, Destruction, Discord, Undeath and Pain, with Purity (or Disguise) a special case; see `02_Cosmology_and_Powers/Gods_and_Churches.md`. Gods cannot enter another god's domain, cannot affect Jason inside his realm, and cannot sanction one another lightly. **Churches** are major institutions (the Church of the Healer provides healing; Dominion backs rulers; Knowledge has an archive and an army; Purity was a long-running fraud: its true god was replaced by a deceiver). **(Later: Book 8, 10-13)**

<!-- level:3 -->
### 9.4 Astral kings, messengers and the Council of Kings **(Level 2-3)**

An **astral king** is a being whose soul has become a small universe. Most astral kings began as **messengers**, a servant race grown on birthing trees, bound by a brand placed in the soul that enforces rank limits (Book 10, Ch4 L53-55; Book 11, Ch25 L49-51). **Messengers** are gestalt beings (body and soul fused), tall, winged, hierarchical and arrogant, who believe themselves the highest life in the cosmos. They are not essence-users; their power comes from their astral king. The **Council of Kings** is the ruling body of astral kings. The **Unorthodoxy** are free messengers hiding from the Council. Jason himself becomes a different sort of astral king, an **astral nexus**, with a **prime avatar**. **(Later: Book 8-12)**

<!-- level:3 -->
### 9.5 Authority and sanctioning **(Level 3)**

**Authority** is the fundamental power of transcendent beings (their flesh, blood and soul), and is currency in the cosmos. **Sanctioning** is a punitive or self-imposed transformation of a transcendent being's authority, for example the old Builder's replacement or Purity's self-sanctioning. The authority concept is central to the final books.

## 10. Souls, death and resurrection

- The **soul** is the person; the body is its expression. Healing magic uses the soul as a blueprint. **Soul scars** are permanent. **Soul crests** are magical tattoos printed on the soul, which appear on the body (Book 2, Ch70 L34-42).
- **Death**: the soul lingers near the body for seconds to an hour; gold-rank healers can reverse death inside that window. Great astral beings could resurrect people but the Reaper and the World-Phoenix object. Gods changed magic so resurrection is harder. **(Later: Book 7)**
- **Outworlders** are souls that wandered into the deep astral and rebuilt a body on arrival.
- **Reincarnation** exists: a soul renews as a different person (e.g. Gary).

## 11. Societies and institutions

- **The Adventure Society**: global monster-hunting organisation. Members wear badges that track kills and locate the body. Stars (1-3) rate judgement, not power. It runs notice boards, contracts, orders, field assessments and branch offices run by directors. A **Continental Council** supervises branches. **Guilds** (Burning Violet, Sapphire Crown...) are elite organisations whose members receive resources.
- **The Magic Society**: records essences, regulates spirit coin farms, runs the water link, researches magic, and holds the "living document" of essence combinations.
- **The Alchemy Association**: guild of alchemists.
- **The Church** (plural): a collection of religious organisations; the Healer's church is the largest.
- **The Red Table**: a blood cult with branches in remote areas (Book 1, Ch18 L5; Book 7, Ch55 L75).
- **Noble houses and monarchies**: kingdoms (the Storm Kingdom, Estercost), duchies, city-states. Diamond-rank rulers exist.
- **Indentured servitude** is a legal norm in many places (criminals' labour sold). It becomes a flash-point in Book 12. **(Later: Book 12)**

## 12. Geography at a glance

- **Estercost**: kingdom with capital Cyrion (largest city), **Vitesse** (city of flowers; Remore Academy), and the colony city **Greenstone** (low-magic delta city; Jason's early home).
- **The Storm Kingdom** (Rimaros): city-of-islands in the Sea of Storms, ruled by a gold-rank Storm King; adventurer island Livaros, quiet Arnote, trade island Provo (the three public islands, Book 7, Ch7 L70).
- **Yaresh**: elf rainforest city, with a natural array beneath it.
- **Boko**: desert academic city north of Greenstone (destroyed in Book 12, Ch33 L3).
- **Other regions**: Zartos (celestine underground city), Pranay (Sri Lanka-like; Jayapura), Kurdansk (Dreisil), the Dragon Lands (Antarctic analogue), Sadi Andali (lawless slave markets), Girlano (cartel-ruled), Segurado, Hornis, Kazlahk, Rakesh (first mentions: Zartos, Book 1, Ch16 L49; Dreisil, Book 6, Ch47 L390; Rakesh, Book 6, Ch47 L623; Hornis, Book 1, Ch102 L11; Girlano, Book 8, Ch24 L22; Segurado, Book 12, Ch24 L6; Sadi Andali, Book 12, Ch50 L126; Kazlahk, Book 11, Ch68 L1377; Jayapura, Book 2, Ch69 L66). The descriptions are from the place files.
- **Astral spaces and zones**: the City of Fallen Echoes (Book 2, Ch42 L96), the desert astral space (Book 1, Ch24 L34), Cardinas and the natural array (Book 10, Ch72 L31), transformation zones (Book 5, Ch44 L48-49; Book 6, Ch47 L3).
- **On Earth**: Casselton Beach (Jason's hometown), Asano Village, Saint-Étienne (France) and Nitra (Slovakia) domains, Sydney, Makassar, Broken Hill, the Australian artefact site.

## 13. History in outline

1. **Before the series**: the original Builder creates Earth and Pallimustus from a shared template; he is sanctioned and replaced by a new mortal Builder. Five hundred years ago Knowledge, Disguise (impersonating Purity) and an Earth outworlder (the Network's founder) set up the link and the Network; the Order of the Reaper is crushed by the churches. **(Level 3 reveal)**
2. **Books 1-3**: Jason arrives (age 23); adventures in Greenstone; the Builder cult's star seeds; the contest for the Scythe of the Reaper; the cult's boarding vessel; Jason dies and returns to Earth.
3. **Books 4-6**: Jason on Earth; the Network, Cabal and EOA; the Grid fails; Makassar; transformation zones; Slovakia and France; vampires; Jason and Farrah return to Pallimustus.
4. **Books 7-9**: the great surge; the Builder's invasion platforms and the One Day War; Jason's soul-space becomes an astral kingdom; the messengers and the Battle of Yaresh.
5. **Books 10-11**: the Cardinas expedition and the Death Pact; Undeath and the transformation zone; Gary's death; the aftermath of the sundered Cosmic Throne (Book 11, Ch64-65); Jason's interface becomes available to everyone with essences (Book 12, Ch1 L36).
6. **Books 12-13**: Jason's prime avatar; Boko; the abolition of the System in slave-law regions; the return to Earth; vampire war; Knowledge's plan (Book 13, Ch1015 L78); at the end Jason walks to the standing stones at Ascension's Gate (Book 13, Ch1024 L153).

See `08_History_and_Timeline/Master_Timeline.md` and the per-book timelines for the verified chronology.

## 14. Recurring facts worth remembering

- Jason's powers revolve around **afflictions** (long-lasting debilitating effects). Many characters' signature lines are used as incantations ("Feed me your sins", "Mine is the judgement, and the judgement is death").
- **Team Biscuit**: a familiar picked the team's name (Book 10, Ch47 L78; the wiki credits Stash); Jason's biscuits are a running joke (Book 1, Ch78).
- **Cloud houses** are a signature aesthetic; so are floral shirts, board games and pop-culture references.
- **Humour and pop culture**: Jason's Earth references (Knight Rider, Airwolf, Voltron...) are part of the tone.
- **Morality**: Jason refuses to treat people as tools; mercy and autonomy recur as themes; his world-changing actions have costs.
- **Gods and wishes**: gods often grant boons that are traps; "poison pills".

## 15. Running a scene at a given period

When setting a roleplay, decide **era** and **scale**:

| Era | Setting | Typical scale | Key dangers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (Book 1-2) | Greenstone, delta, desert astral spaces | Iron-rank | Blood cult, crime lords, Builder cult, monster surge precursors |
| Mid (Book 3) | Greenstone to the Reaper-trial astral space | Bronze | Star seeds, kidnapping, energy ghouls |
| Earth (Books 4-6) | Australia, Asia, Europe | Bronze to silver | Network politics, EOA, monster waves, vampires, transformation zones |
| Rimaros (Books 7-8) | Storm Kingdom | Silver | Builder fortresses, Order of Redeeming Light, royal politics |
| Yaresh (Books 9-10) | Elf rainforest, natural array | Silver | Messengers, worms, Undeath, elemental horrors |
| Zone and aftermath (Book 11) | Transformation zone, Earth | Silver to gold | Undeath, reality collapse, vampire war |
| Return (Books 12-13) | Pallimustus tour and Earth | Gold | Messenger kings, pirates, Elizabeth's blood weapons, Knowledge |

Era choice and spoiler handling are covered in `10_Roleplay_Support/Era_Selection.md` and `10_Roleplay_Support/Spoiler_Management.md`.

## 16. Glossary of core terms

**Adventurer**: someone who hunts monsters and takes contracts. **Affliction**: a lasting harmful condition. **Astral kingdom**: Jason's soul realm. **Aura**: projection of the soul. **Awakening stone**: crystal that awakens an essence ability. **Boon**: a lasting beneficial condition. **Cloud flask**: Jason's bonded item that makes cloud houses. **Confluence**: the special fourth essence. **Core**: crystallised magic from a monster. **Essence**: crystal of magic. **Gestalt**: body and soul fused. **GAB**: great astral being. **Grid**: Earth's detection network. **Hegemon**: Jason's title and aura power. **Messenger**: winged servant of an astral king. **Outworlder**: person from another world. **Prime avatar**: a physical anchor for an astral being's power. **Quintessence**: crafting gems of an essence's theme. **Rank**: iron through diamond. **Sanction**: punitive change of a being's authority. **Spirit coin**: currency of magic. **Star seed**: Builder implant that puppets its host. **System**: Jason's interface made universal. **Voice of the Will**: a bonded avatar of an astral king's will.
