This folder describes how magic and adventuring work in the world of *He Who Fights With Monsters*: the structures people live by, not a rule system. It contains no numbers for damage, success or cost; where the books give numbers (lifespans, exchange rates, ranges, counts) they are quoted as facts of the world. It replaces the earlier single combined document, which is now split by topic and re-checked against the chapter files.

## 1. How the pieces fit

Magic in the world is raw power from the astral. In a magic-rich world it condenses into things: **monsters**, which are living and unstable; **essences**, **quintessence** and **awakening stones**, which are not alive and stay until used (Book 1, Ch19 L34, L45-56). A person uses essences to gain abilities, stones to awaken them, and grows by rank. Everything else in this folder is a layer on that basic loop.

```
 astral magic ──> monsters ──> cores, loot ──> advancement (cores) or consumed
        │
        ├──> essences ──> abilities ──> ranks (with attributes, auras, familiars)
        ├──> quintessence ──> rituals, alchemy, artifice, items
        └──> awakening stones ──> abilities (and rituals)
```

## 3. Quick answers

- **How many essences can a person have?** Four, and the fourth is a confluence (Book 1, Ch21 L59-60).
- **What are the ranks?** Iron, bronze, silver, gold and diamond, with gods and great astral beings outside the ladder (Book 1, Ch22 L50-58).
- **How long do ranked people live?** Bronze well past a hundred, silver double that, gold for centuries (Book 1, Ch22 L58).
- **Can a person refuse the confluence?** Only clergy do, and nobody else should (Book 1, Ch21 L57).
- **What does a core do?** It speeds advancement and damages potential (Book 1, Ch31 L96-98).
- **Why do the afflictions matter?** Because defence against them is a game of resistance, cleansing and negation (see `Afflictions_Boons_and_Damage.md`).
- **How long do monsters last?** From a week or two for the lowest to a month at iron, longer above (Book 1, Ch19 L40).
- **How common are surges?** About every ten years, anywhere from eight to thirteen (Book 1, Ch31 L71-74).

## 4. Changes over the series

- **Level 1:** Books 1 to 3 establish the basics above, and the Reaper trials and Builder astral space add growth items, familiars and divine stones (Book 2, Ch13 L60-90; Book 2, Ch74 L40-50).
- **Level 2:** Book 4 moves to Earth, where magical ranks are named tiers that start at zero (Book 4, Ch11 L9-10). The Grid works as interconnected rituals built from the natural features of the world (Book 5, Ch5 L11). Book 5 and Book 6 bring monster waves and transformation zones (Book 5, Ch44 L48). Book 7 and Book 8 run a surge on Pallimustus (Book 7, Ch1 L56).
- **Level 3:** After Jason's second zone, magic on Earth "had risen" and essences and monsters appear directly (Book 11, Ch1 L16). Confluences stop being fixed (Book 11, Ch68 L1574-1580; Book 12, Ch10 L91). Resurrection becomes harder (Book 9, Ch81 L61). The System spreads from Jason (Book 12, Ch67 L197).

## 5. Common misconceptions

- That a pile of quintessence can stand in for an essence. Farrah calls it impossible (Book 1, Ch19 L50).
- That essences change who you are. The books treat that as a religious claim, with Rufus's answer that accountability is the wielder's (Book 1, Ch20 L84-86).
- That rank is the same as power. It is magical density (Book 1, Ch84 L57).

## 6. Roleplay notes

- Keep characters' abilities consistent with their essences; if inventing abilities, link them to the essence concept and the rank. A worked example is in `Jason_Power_Set_Reference.md`.
- Make afflictions and cleansing matter: the interplay is the signature tactical flavour of the series.
- Show the human side of rank: lifespans, fear of higher ranks, bureaucracy, the importance of potions and crystal wash.
- Use the setting's tone: wry humour, pop culture, found family, serious moral stakes.
- Do not let anyone solve problems with a single overwhelming ability; the books rely on teamwork, preparation and costs.
