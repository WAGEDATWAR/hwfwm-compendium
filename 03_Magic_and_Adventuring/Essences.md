## 1. Summary

An essence is the most powerful and rarest of the forms in which magic condenses into something that is not alive. It is absorbed into a person's soul and gives that person a set of five abilities tied to its concept. Four essences make a full set. A person with a full set becomes iron rank and is changed by it. Essences are how Pallimustus makes essence users: the people who fight monsters, serve in the churches and run most of the world's institutions.

## 2. How it works

**What an essence is.** Rufus lists three non-living manifestations of magic: essences, "the most powerful manifestation" and "also, the rarest", then quintessence, "kind of like chunks of essence", and awakening stones (Book 1, Ch19 L45-56). A pile of quintessence cannot be used as an essence; "there's always some crackpot who claims to have figured it out, but it isn't possible" (Book 1, Ch19 L48-50). Clive's explanation is that the astral is "the source of all magic", that some worlds soak up a lot of it, and that this world's magic takes forms such as essences, stones, quintessence and monsters (Book 1, Ch69 L99). Essences appear as cubes or crystals: Jason's blood essence is a red cube whose slick surface looks wet but is dry and warm (Book 1, Ch19 L68); his sin essence is a white and gold cube (Book 1, Ch21 L44).

**Where they come from.** Magic "just starts manifesting all over the place" in a world as rich in it as this one (Book 1, Ch19 L33). They are also bought and sold. Jason trades a rat essence to a broker for two awakening stones (Book 1, Ch87 L91), brokers run regular essence and stone auctions (Book 1, Ch113 L61; Book 2, Ch70 L30), and after the Book 2 expedition the market price of essences and stones reached "an all-time low" (Book 3, Ch1 L100). Families pool money to buy essence sets for promising young people, and criminals buy powerful ones (Book 3, Ch1 L101). Dean's family could "afford a full set of essences" for him from a living made with water quintessence (Book 1, Ch96 L18). Gods can also supply essences (section 3).

**Absorption.** Absorbing an essence is done by an essence ritual (Book 1, Ch15 L113). Farrah offers to perform one for Jason (Book 1, Ch15 L112), and a ritual is ordinarily required for stones too (Book 1, Ch22 L38-41). The prompt that follows asks whether to absorb and reports "You have absorbed 2 of 4 essences" (Book 1, Ch21 L4). Absorption is painful. Jason passes out for the first (the dark essence turns to smoke and invades his body), the blood essence melts over his hand and burns into his flesh (Book 1, Ch21 L3), and the sin essence fills him like inflating pressure (Book 1, Ch21 L46). Farrah observes that each absorption seems easier and asks whether subjects adapt (Book 1, Ch21 L10-11). Once absorbed, "an essence cannot be relinquished or replaced" (Book 1, Ch20 L53).

**Five abilities, one awakened.** Each essence "imbues 1 awakened essence ability and 4 unawakened essence abilities" (Book 1, Ch20 L52). A full set therefore has twenty abilities, and a person "can't even properly progress towards bronze rank until you awaken every essence ability" (Book 1, Ch22 L68).

**Attribute bonding.** Each essence bonds to one attribute and raises it from normal to iron: blood to Power, dark to Speed, sin to Recovery and doom to Spirit in Jason's case (Book 1, Ch21 L6, L49, L61). Mastering all of an essence's abilities then raises that attribute (Book 1, Ch21 L6).

**Order of use.** Farrah and Rufus advise using all essences first and stones afterwards, because abilities are shaped by the essences and abilities a person already has; "awaken too many abilities before you have all your essences, and your abilities will lack focus" (Book 1, Ch21 L36-37).

**The fourth essence.** The first three are chosen; the fourth is a confluence and arrives by itself (Book 1, Ch19 L30). See `Confluences.md`.

**Iron rank.** The fourth essence completes the set. Jason's message lists the changes: damage reduction against normal-rank sources, increased resistance to normal-rank effects, the ability to sense auras, and the ability to sustain himself with concentrated magic (Book 1, Ch21 L65-70). The body expels impurities as it moves toward "a state of perfection" (Book 1, Ch22 L9). See `Ranks_and_Advancement.md`.

## 3. Variations and exceptions

**Outworlder absorption.** Jason absorbed his first essence "without a ritual" and thinks it was tied to the same power that lets him speak every language (Book 1, Ch15 L114). The ritual knowledge from the skill book tells him a ritual is normally required, "except, he knew from experience that wasn't always true" (Book 1, Ch15 L113).

**Racial difference.** Humans "have their essence abilities increase faster than other races" (Book 1, Ch15 L58). Abilities and their awakening differ by person as well as race (Book 1, Ch15 L57).

**Divine essences.** Gods give their clergy "divine versions of regular essences", so clergy reject the confluence (Book 1, Ch21 L57). The Healer's church supplies the essences and stones for its healing abilities (Book 1, Ch35 L23). Divine essences come back to the god: when the Builder cult's captives are freed, those with divine essences have the power "returned to their god" and are weaker (Book 3, Ch61 L11). A freed group of cult-held priests who lose divine gifts either join another church or replace them with regular essences (Book 2, Ch64 L124-126). Ex-clergy stripped of divinely gifted essences suffer "a debilitating imbalance in the body and soul" (Book 4, Ch11 L16). Jason refuses divine essences because "the idea of some god repossessing my magic powers doesn't appeal" (Book 2, Ch56 L93); Clive adds that great astral beings cannot make essences "the way that gods can" but can make their own stones (Book 2, Ch56 L92).

**Restricted essences.** The Adventure Society keeps a list of essences and combinations "that pose an inherent threat to ordinary people", with the death essence at the top of it by itself, "because it always seems to go wrong" (Book 1, Ch20 L42-44). Membership is impossible with a restricted essence (Book 1, Ch20 L44). The combinations that produce the undeath confluence almost all involve death (Book 1, Ch20 L19). The Magic Society's living document lists every known essence and combination, with restricted ones flagged, and Jason's sin essence is in "the rarest category" (Book 1, Ch20 L97-104).

**Rarity.** Essences vary in how often they appear and how common they are. Sin is among the rarest (Book 1, Ch20 L104). A rune essence reaches Clive as an "epic four-star essence" (Book 2, Ch9 L21). Low-rarity essences are the ones ordinary buyers afford (Book 1, Ch108 L28). The rat essence is "not popular with anyone who could afford to be choosy" (Book 1, Ch87 L91).

**Not all essences are simple.** An essence can seem to carry a meaning far from its name: Neil says it takes arrogance to absorb "the essence of defying the gods", and Jason replies that the sin essence is not about defying gods for him (Book 3, Ch8 L37-44).

## 4. Examples from the books

- Jason: dark, blood and sin, giving the doom confluence (Book 1, Ch20 L110; Ch21 L54-62).
- Farrah: fire, earth and potent, giving volcano (Book 1, Ch19 L30).
- Sophie: swift, balance and wind, giving mystic (Book 2, Ch5 L82-87).
- Belinda: magic, trap and adept, giving charlatan (Book 2, Ch32 L67-72).
- Clive: magic, rune and balance, giving karmic (Book 2, Ch28 L56).
- Neil: shield, growth and renewal, giving prosperity (Book 2, Ch28 L44).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Essences change who you are.** Anisa's church holds that "the essences we use change who we are" and favours only the ones it sees as holy or pure, saying other essences taint the soul (Book 1, Ch20 L84). Rufus's reply is that essence abilities "aren't inherently good or bad. Like a sword, they can be used to oppress or protect"; accountability lies with the wielder (Book 1, Ch20 L86). He allows that the restricted list covers "rare and extreme cases" (Book 1, Ch20 L89). An American religious type told Jason "Essences are god's test to see who is worthy of the power" (Book 4, Ch43 L10).
- **Rarer means better.** For stones, Rufus says "rare stones don't give out better abilities, just more specific ones" (Book 1, Ch19 L66); the same applies in spirit to essences, whose power comes from concept and user.
- **You can make an essence from quintessence.** Farrah calls it impossible (Book 1, Ch19 L50).
- **A confluence can be found loose.** "You can't find a volcano essence anywhere" (Book 1, Ch19 L31).
