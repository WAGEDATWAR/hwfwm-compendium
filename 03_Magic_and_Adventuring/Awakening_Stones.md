## 1. Summary

An awakening stone is a consumable crystal that awakens one essence ability the user has not yet awakened. It is the third kind of non-living magic Rufus lists, after essences and quintessence (Book 1, Ch19 L56). The stone does not choose the ability; it steers toward a theme, and what comes out depends mostly on the essences the user holds. Stones are how a new essence user fills out the twenty abilities that a full set carries, and they are bought, auctioned, given as rewards and kept as family wealth.

## 2. How it works

**What a stone does.** Each stone's description says the same thing: "Awakens an essence ability", with the requirement "Unawakened essence ability" (Book 1, Ch22 L99-102). Jason is shown a count of how many abilities he still has to awaken, such as "You have 10 unawakened essence abilities" (Book 1, Ch22 L132). Stones can also be used in other kinds of magic: "sometimes awakening stones get used as part of very powerful rituals" (Book 1, Ch22 L45), as the cult did with its feast stones in the sacrifice chamber (Book 1, Ch19 L57-58).

**What decides the ability.** Farrah: "the biggest determinate of what ability you get is the essence it comes from" (Book 1, Ch19 L67). A stone has a theme, and the theme sets the range of powers: a feast stone is "related to the concept of a feast, but that can manifest in any of hundreds of powers" (Book 1, Ch19 L63), which Farrah corrects to "thousands" (Book 1, Ch19 L64). The abilities a person has already, and the essences they have used, steer the stone. This is why Farrah and Rufus advise taking every essence before using stones: otherwise "your abilities will lack focus" (Book 1, Ch21 L36-37).

**Rarity and specificity.** "The more rare an awakening stone is, the more specific the powers" (Book 1, Ch19 L64). A common stone "can give you any ability the rarest could", it just draws from a larger pool; "rare stones don't give out better abilities, just more specific ones" (Book 1, Ch19 L66). The stones people seek are those with a desirable specialty, and "stones with desirable specialties tend to be the most expensive" (Book 1, Ch22 L104). A mid-rarity stone is "almost always a useful utility power" (Book 1, Ch22 L110). A high-rarity stone is "more specialised" (Book 1, Ch39 L147-148) and restricts the possible abilities to just one or two types (Book 2, Ch57 L36).

**Grading.** The Magic Society grades stones on a scale of one to five stars "based on how frequently they are known to appear world-wide", working with brokers and the Adventure Society; Jason's ability also sorts them into five stages of rarity: common (one star), uncommon (two stars), up to epic (four stars, "the second highest rarity") and legendary (Book 2, Ch9 L9, L17). In the Adventure Society's wording, a stone of the apocalypse is "unranked, legendary" (Book 1, Ch22 L128) and a stone of omens "unranked, epic" (Book 1, Ch39 L137).

**Using one.** The stone melts into the user's hand or arm and sinks in without fanfare (Book 1, Ch22 L71, L112, L152), and Jason's system confirms "You have awakened the blood essence ability [Leech Bite]" (Book 1, Ch22 L72). Most people need a ritual for each stone, and a person with a whole row "going through a half-dozen rituals would take hours" (Book 1, Ch22 L38-42). Clive's essence ability, Enact Ritual, makes drawing and performing the circles more convenient (Book 2, Ch6 L43). Clive draws the circle and completes the ritual when a team member uses an uncommon stone of preparation (Book 2, Ch66 L66).

**Hunger afterwards.** A stone of the feast leaves Jason ravenous: "I think the stone of the feast made it worse" (Book 1, Ch22 L75). The cure is a spirit coin (Book 1, Ch22 L76-77).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Themes by name.** Stones are named for a theme or concept. The text names many: feast, adventure, apocalypse, omens, wrath, absolution, judgement, hand, foot, eyes, sky, purgation, preparation, rebirth, karma, avatar, celestials, moment, surge, champion, the stars, the Reaper, magus, fish, rain, ruin, focus, defiance and more. Jason's first stone is the "Awakening Stone of the Stars" (Book 1, Ch4 L40). A shopping list includes awakening stones of the eyes, the hand and the foot (Book 2, Ch5 L105-107). Neil and Jason select awakening stones of the avatar, and Humphrey selects an awakening stone of rebirth (Book 2, Ch63 L25). One speaker mentions an awakening stone of defiance (Book 9, Ch78 L129).
- **Tendencies of a theme.** The text gives these: eyes "a good shot at giving a perception power" (Book 2, Ch5 L105); hand awakens empty-hand abilities and attacks; foot, unarmed attacks and movement abilities (Book 2, Ch5 L108-110); sky, a very good chance of a flight power, though "it could just as easily give you a special attack effective against enemies in the air" (Book 2, Ch9 L29, L38); purgation, "almost certainly some kind of cleansing ability" (Book 2, Ch9 L47); magus, "a common awakening stone, especially with humans... almost guaranteed a spell, which humans rarely awakened, with an outside chance of a coveted summoning or familiar power" (Book 1, Ch87 L91); omens, a hope for an aura power (Book 1, Ch39 L150).
- **Human aptitude.** Humans have an aptitude for special attacks and rarely awaken spells without a magus stone (Book 1, Ch87 L91).
- **One aura only.** A person can have just one aura ability; the stone that would give a second does not work (Book 2, Ch13 L70). A tablet can remove an existing aura ability (Book 2, Ch13 L76-78).
- **Divine stones.** Gods craft stones for a specific outcome. Knowledge sends Jason two: a Divine Awakening Stone of Inevitability, to bestow an aura power (Book 2, Ch13 L60-62; the ability is [Inescapable Doom] in one line and [Inevitable Demise] in another, Book 2, Ch13 L62, L66), and a Divine Awakening Stone of Persistence, to bestow a specific spell (Book 2, Ch13 L83-85), with a Soul-Purgation Tablet to make room (Book 2, Ch13 L71-76). Jason shuts the case, calling it "a shiny red apple" and a poison pill (Book 2, Ch13 L96, L122). A god can take back what it gave: Jason says "the idea of some god repossessing my magic powers doesn't appeal" (Book 2, Ch56 L93).
- **Great astral beings' stones.** "The stones the astral beings produce aren't divine stones that the astral beings can revoke. They're just ordinary awakening stones whose aspect aligns with the great astral being in question" (Book 2, Ch56 L94). Relatively common from the Reaper and the Celestial Book, not from the World-Phoenix (Book 7, Ch11 L75).
- **Non-essence uses.** The same stone items fuel rituals (see `Rituals_Arrays_and_Formations.md`).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Rare means better.** Farrah and Rufus both deny it (Book 1, Ch19 L66; Book 1, Ch22 L104).
- **A stone can be aimed.** "There are no guarantees when it comes to awakening powers, not even with the rarest of stones" (Book 1, Ch22 L107). Clive: "I'm just managing expectations" (Book 2, Ch9 L40).
- **A stone saves you from needing a full set.** Rufus's advice is to take all four essences first (Book 1, Ch21 L36-37).
- **Stones are only for abilities.** They also fuel rituals (Book 1, Ch22 L45).

## 6. Later developments

- **Level 1 (Book 2):** Stones are rewards. The Adventure Society raised the bounty on Jason to a four-star stone for each person on the team that caught him, with a cap of six after people formed giant groups (Book 2, Ch9 L17-18). The Society gives out stones for exceptional service, rarely, and as rewards for contributions in open contracts (Book 1, Ch64 L81). A Geller family tradition supplies scions "with enough awakening stones to get started" (Book 1, Ch64 L80). As a rule, "we don't give out awakening stones to people for killing monsters above their rank", so that people are not tempted to jump ranks (Book 1, Ch91 L34).
- **Level 2 (Book 9):** Stones keep their reputation for surprising results. In a joking exchange, one speaker admits to using a stone of defiance and a companion teases that they expected an ability from a chef-type essence (Book 9, Ch78 L126-129).
- **Level 3:** After the throne is restored, stones continue to be used; the fixing of confluences, not stones, is what changes (see `Confluences.md`).
