## 1. Summary

Monsters are the most common way magic manifests in a magic-rich world. They appear from nothing, usually in the wild, live for a time that depends on their rank, and then dissolve back into raw magic if not killed first. They have ranks like essence users; the ranks are not given by essences but by the density of the magic that makes them. Hunting them before they reach their aggressive final stage is the bulk of an adventurer's work, and what they leave behind funds much of the economy.

## 2. How it works

**Where they come from.** Rufus: "Most magic manifestations are monsters. They just appear, hopefully in the wilderness, but the magic they're made of isn't stable. Eventually they break down and dissolve back into magic. Killing them just makes it faster" (Book 1, Ch19 L34). The astral is "the source of all magic" and monsters are one of the forms the magic takes in a world that soaks up a lot of it (Book 1, Ch69 L99).

**Rank.** Monsters "exist within a certain power threshold. Whether an essence user or a monster, each rank has a suppressive effect on lower ranks" (Book 1, Ch19 L42). They do not use essences (Book 1, Ch19 L41-42). Rank "was a function of their magical density, not actual power": the strongest iron-rank monsters are stronger than the weakest bronze ones, with the difference usually made up in numbers (Book 1, Ch84 L57).

**Lifespan and aggression.** "Lesser monsters only last a week or two. They start so close to breaking down that they're aggressive from the moment they appear, but they aren't really a threat. An old woman with a broom can handle them. Iron rank monsters last about a month, getting aggressive in the final week or so. It goes up from there" (Book 1, Ch19 L40). "Monsters frequently aren't a problem when they first manifest, but as they get closer to breaking down they become highly aggressive. The bulk of our job as adventurers is hunting them down before they reach that stage" (Book 1, Ch19 L36). A region that has gone without hunters for two months will have iron-rank monsters in their berserk stage, while bronze monsters that spawned in that period will not have reached it (Book 3, Ch7 L5).

**Dissolution and loot.** A dead monster "dissolved into rainbow smoke" (Book 1, Ch32 L40). Anything it spilled dissolves too, and the smoke can harm a person who gets its fluids in the mouth (Book 1, Ch27 L91). The loot is whatever it leaves behind: monster cores, spirit coins, materials and the occasional essence or stone (Book 1, Ch27 L94-96; Book 1, Ch73 L46-51). One blood weaver produced three essences at once, an event "less common than even a legendary essence appearing" (Book 4, Ch21 L136). A monster that is not a monster but an animal does not dissolve (Book 1, Ch19 L35-36).

**Where they are strong.** Magic density sets the usual rank: around Greenstone "iron-rank monsters are the norm, with a good smattering of bronze-rank. Silvers can show up, but only very occasionally" (Book 1, Ch30 L43). In Vitesse's region "you get more silver rank monsters than anything. You see as many golds as you do bronze, and sometimes even diamond rank monsters" (Book 1, Ch30 L44). In a saturated place monsters hunt in packs: "even normally solitary monsters appeared in packs" (Book 2, Ch53 L29). A silver-rank monster can linger for years before breaking down in a low-magic area (Book 2, Ch43 L56). Diamond monsters usually come only in surges (Book 7, Ch10 L19).

**Intelligence.** "Monsters would become more intelligent at higher ranks, their powers more exotic" (Book 2, Ch75 L89). Gold-rank springclaw gorillas "were smart and spawned in large herds" (Book 12, Ch47 L3). Most people know the monsters of their own area "usually by description. Most run before they ever get a good look" (Book 1, Ch84 L55).

**Rank-up rule of thumb.** "A good adventurer should be able to handle monsters one rank up, so long as the match-up is good" (Book 2, Ch47 L59).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Constructs and golems.** Siege golems and world engineers are constructs, not spawned monsters in the ordinary sense; world engineers are "diamond-rank golems larger than most buildings" (Book 8, Ch6 L21).
- **Astral entities.** Nightmare hags are "diamond-rank entities from the astral that have no physical existence in this place and cannot harm you directly" (Book 2, Ch57 L68).
- **Shape-shifters.** Amalgeths "always start at silver rank, but if they reproduce enough times, they advance to gold" (Book 10, Ch32 L77).
- **Anomalies.** In the transformation zones, monsters called anomalies (living, unliving, reality-dysphoric, greater) appear (Book 6, Ch2 L10; Book 6, Ch8 L73; Book 6, Ch11 L43; Book 6, Ch15 L4).
- **Apocalypse beasts.** A creature "made from alchemy, blood and things best left unmentioned" can be a sanguine horror, an apocalypse monster that Jason says "can scour a world of life" (Book 1, Ch12 L70; Book 1, Ch71 L65).
- **Monsters as familiars.** A familiar is made like a deliberate monster, with a creature from the deep astral inhabiting it (Book 1, Ch69 L127).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Monsters are evil.** They are manifestations of unstable magic that dissolves; Rufus dislikes people who relish killing "everyone you find" (Book 1, Ch19 L37-38).
- **Rank equals strength.** Rank is magical density, not power (Book 1, Ch84 L57).
- **Animals and monsters are alike.** A snake that is an animal does not dissolve like a monster (Book 1, Ch19 L35).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Level 2:** The haul from the Book 3 astral space is described in Book 4 as many duplicate essences from the same monsters: half plant essences, the rest mostly venom, might and animal essences (Book 4, Ch2 L18).
- **Level 2:** Transformation zones spawn anomalies (Book 6, Ch2 L10).
- **Level 3:** Blood oaks and vampires are Earth's late-series monsters (Book 13, Ch958 L79); a Book 10 expedition meets gold-rank spider moles in the shaft (Book 10, Ch61 L19).
