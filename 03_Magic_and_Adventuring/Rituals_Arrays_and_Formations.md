## 1. Summary

Ritual magic is the practice of drawing a diagram, a circle with lines and glyphs, and powering it with external magic to do something an essence ability cannot. It is used for absorbing essences, awakening stones, summoning familiars, spreading wards and tracking, and, in permanent form, as formations and arrays. Ritual magic is learned, not given by an essence, and a competent ritualist is a valuable member of any team. Anyone can learn external magic; only essence users can combine it with essence powers.

## 2. How it works

**What it is.** Farrah's summary: "Ritual magic relies on external sources of magic. That's ambient magic, plus more concentrated sources, like quintessence or spirit coins" (Book 1, Ch23 L27). "External magic doesn't require you to have an essence. There are people who make careers out of learning a specialised slice of external magic" (Book 1, Ch23 L30), like plumbers who know "just enough to do a specific job" (Book 1, Ch23 L31-32). The fundamental theory is shared across rituals, magical craftsmanship and alchemy: "Same basis, different applications" (Book 1, Ch23 L32).

**The circle and the ambient magic.** A ritual diagram must be adjusted to the local magic: "Every time you draw a magic diagram you need to adjust for the ambient magic conditions. A weak source of congealed magic like the core of a lesser monster is a perfect way to check your work" (Book 1, Ch23 L15). A ritual room is designed with a permanent circle and a regulated environment (Book 1, Ch23 L16-17). A flat stone floor helps: Emir's ritual room has "a floor made from a single slab of black stone, cut perfectly level and smooth" (Book 2, Ch6 L41). A cloud floor is poor for circles (Book 3, Ch25 L38). Materials vary with the ritual: a salt circle will do for ordinary summoning, while exotic summons call for obsidian dust and iron filings (Book 1, Ch93 L41). A permanent brass circle is made by carving channels into stone and pouring molten metal (Book 3, Ch7 L96).

**Clive's gift.** Clive's racial gift balances the ambient magic: "It balances out the ambient magic so you don't need to adjust your ritual circle" (Book 1, Ch85 L60), and his essence ability, Enact Ritual, draws diagrams in lines of golden light (Book 1, Ch103 L44; Book 2, Ch6 L43). "His ability to draw them with his power, along with balancing out the ambient magic, saved immense amounts of time when going through many rituals in sequence" (Book 2, Ch66 L90).

**Essence rituals.** "Essence rituals are very gentle. It's only if you shove the essence inside yourself without one that the experience is a harsh one" (Book 2, Ch7 L130). In a ritual the essences turn to glowing dust that swirls around the user; when the last essence is absorbed the dust stops glowing and falls (Book 2, Ch7 L117). The standard sequence for Sophie's stones in Emir's palace takes hours, with Clive resetting the circle between stones (Book 2, Ch66 L90; Book 2, Ch67 L3, L162).

**Casting rituals as powers.** A ritual can itself be an essence ability. Farrah notes that "rituals that are also essence powers tend to be quick and dirty", like Gary's and her short-lived summons (Book 1, Ch23 L38-39). A familiar summoning is an "elaborate" ritual needed only per rank (Book 1, Ch23 L42). See `Familiars_Summons_and_Conjurations.md`.

**Large rituals.** The blood cult's star seed ritual uses a magic circle "to gather and concentrate the ambient magic to fuel the star seed", supplemented periodically with spirit coins (Book 3, Ch19 L37). Carlos's soul-projection ritual, used to check for star seeds, is "a gold rank ritual", devised for the occasion and rare in Greenstone (Book 3, Ch23 L19). A circle for it was "easily the most sophisticated circle Jason had ever seen", with sections coloured red, green and blue as well as gold (Book 3, Ch23 L32). Clive's combat ritual circles glow on the floor under his feet during a fight (Book 3, Ch38 L30).

**Divine rituals.** A god's rituals work differently: "divine rituals are not so vulnerable to vagaries of ambient magic as the mundane varieties" (Book 2, Ch19 L77). They "still draw on the power of ambient magic but are infused with the glorious might of the divine. My god's will moves the magic and not the other way around" (Book 2, Ch19 L79).

**Mirage chambers.** A mirage chamber is a training room with a control block of runes at one end, carved into a stone block under a window (Book 1, Ch48 L3). It runs at a chosen rank: in Greenstone Rufus asks to borrow one but the family can only run it at bronze rank for limited periods because local magical density is low; Danielle offers hers at iron rank instead (Book 1, Ch37 L41-55).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Formation, array, grid.** Farrah is a specialist in "formation magic and arrays. Permanent and semi-permanent versions of ritual magic, which is one of the core magical fields" (Book 5, Ch54 L11). Jason's account: "Formations were permanent or semi-permanent ritual effects. Arrays were formations layered in sequence or even atop one another", with "some interdisciplinary crossover with artifice" (Book 4, Ch25 L20).
- **The Grid.** On Earth the grid is "like the landscape is somehow operating as a series of interconnected rituals, creating a magical array from the natural features of the world itself... nodes the size of cities, with elements made up of mountains, hills and rivers", covering the entire planet (Book 5, Ch5 L11). See `Earth_Magic.md`.
- **Landscaped magic.** Clive says of a large estate that whoever designed it showed "the foresight to wait for plants to grow over decades, planning out the shifts in magic as plants and trees grew" (Book 2, Ch18 L11).
- **Dice and triggers.** Humphrey's summoning die activates its power by being rolled in a "simple circle of powdered chalk" (Book 3, Ch10 L5).
- **Ritual knowledge from skill books.** Jason's ritual skill book gave him "the bare minimum to be considered a proper ritual magician" (Book 1, Ch23 L36). See `Skill_Books_and_Learning.md`.
- **Who is allowed to draw.** A person who does not know external magic cannot awaken a familiar summoning power (Book 1, Ch23 L37).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **Absorption of essences and stones:** Book 1, Ch21 (Jason, no ritual needed), Book 2, Ch7 (Sophie).
- **Familiar summonings:** the first sanguine horror in Book 1, Ch23; Shade's bronze resummoning materials in Book 2, Ch63.
- **A ritual room with permanent circle:** Landemere Vane's room, found under his burned house and described as "an ordinary, if impressive, ritual room" which "predates the settlement of Greenstone" (Book 3, Ch11 L90).
- **Portal rituals:** In Landemere's old ritual room, the Builder cult's agents build an astral bridge from a brass circle and a crude brick archway (Book 3, Ch7 L96-97).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Magic circles are universal.** Each needs adjusting to local magic (Book 1, Ch23 L15).
- **Divine rituals are just bigger.** They are less vulnerable to the vagaries of ambient magic than mundane rituals (Book 2, Ch19 L77).
- **Rituals need an essence.** External magic "doesn't require you to have an essence" (Book 1, Ch23 L30).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** Clive's racial gift and Enact Ritual speed up circle-drawing in the Book 2 sessions (Book 2, Ch66 L90).
- **Level 2:** On Earth, Farrah is called "the foremost expert in array magic on the planet" (Book 5, Ch36 L84) and the Grid is shown to be a planetary array (Book 5, Ch5 L11).
