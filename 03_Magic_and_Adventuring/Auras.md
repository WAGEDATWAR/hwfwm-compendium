## 1. Summary

An aura is the projection of a ranked person's soul. It grows with rank, is read by other ranked people as a sense of danger, strength and mood, and is how adventurers judge each other before words are spoken. Controlling the aura is a skill with three basic parts: projecting it, restraining it and suppressing other people's. Auras can be hidden, masked, aimed at normal people to intimidate them, and, at silver rank, begin to lift the body off the ground.

## 2. How it works

**Aura sense.** A new iron-ranker gains the ability to sense auras (Book 1, Ch21 L69). Jason's first impression of other ranked people is a feeling of danger, which Rufus explains is "our auras. Now that you're iron rank, you can sense them", and which gets more reliable as he learns to tell the difference "between a monster, an essence user and a regular person" (Book 1, Ch22 L31-33). The sense is "not completely reliable" because "some monsters can hide their auras. People can too, if they have an aura power" (Book 1, Ch22 L35). Ordinary people are "faint, barely detectable, while those with essences were much clearer"; a village normally has one or two with an essence, and a full-set iron-ranker "radiated out like a beacon" (Book 1, Ch35 L50).

**The three controls.** Farrah teaches Jason to control his aura "in three ways": "projecting your aura and restraining it", then "using your aura to suppress the auras of other people", which is "harder than the others", to be left alone "until you reach a certain level of proficiency" (Book 1, Ch40 L64-70). Projection is "pushing your aura out to affect people"; restraining is "drawing it in, whether to hide it, or just not be rude" (Book 1, Ch40 L66). Everyone projects until they learn not to: "It's what makes people with no aura control easy to read" (Book 1, Ch40 L74). Later the text repeats that "all aura control techniques were variations or extensions of those three" (Book 4, Ch74 L10).

**Why it matters.** Anyone with a perception power will eventually see auras clearly, so an uncontrolled aura reads like signs, including location and emotions, and "know when you lie" (Book 1, Ch40 L50). The skill with which an aura is controlled "is how adventurers make their first impressions on one another. If you can't do it properly, people won't take you seriously. Excellent aura manipulation marks you as an adventurer of training and distinction" (Book 1, Ch40 L80). Farrah calls the lack of an aura ability a serious gap: "anyone who hits bronze rank and can't manage their aura is a second-rate adventurer" (Book 1, Ch39 L152).

**A gold-ranker's aura.** At gold an uncontrolled aura is dangerous to normal people: "If you reach gold rank and can't control your aura properly, you can hurt normal people just by going near them" (Book 1, Ch40 L44). Such people are required to wear a suppressor: Farrah once saw "a gold ranker who forgot to put on an aura suppressor before going to a market. People were passed out, bleeding out their ears" (Book 1, Ch40 L46-48).

**Aura weapons.** Control can be turned on normal people. Jason, in Book 4, slowly unleashes his aura in a room: "Normal humans couldn't detect auras unless they were projected in a specific way. It was a simple use of basic projection control... a tool that essence users employed to intimidate normals" (Book 4, Ch7 L92). Higher-rank auras can be pointed at lower-rank ones: a bronze-rank woman suppresses Jason's with her own, which is "the very height of rudeness" (Book 1, Ch65 L91). A silver-ranker's weight can be felt without being a suppressing force (Book 1, Ch101 L16).

**Blending in.** A gold-ranker can use aura control to pass through a crowd unnoticed, and a high-end silver can do the same (Book 4, Ch74 L6). The technique blends projection and retraction to merge the aura into the ambient magic, with delicate suppression to blunt others' aura senses (Book 4, Ch74 L12-13). Normal people "were aura blind unless someone with aura control didn't want them to be" (Book 4, Ch74 L14). Jason's inspirations are Farrah's orthodox teaching, his own experience, Vermillion's use of aura on normals, the Mirror King's aura that "had felt like a part of the world around it", and Shade's masking (Book 4, Ch73 L95-100).

**Masks and suppressors.** Magic items hide or damp an aura. Farrah mentions aura suppressors (Book 1, Ch40 L46); a thief's methods include "aura masking" (Book 1, Ch100 L85); an aura mask is breached by using essence abilities or failing to keep a strong aura suppressed (Book 1, Ch102 L93); aura masking will not work against a tracking effect on the aura (Book 1, Ch103 L16). A suppression collar can negate a target's powers; Jason is able to "completely negate the effects of an iron-rank suppression collar" after practice, and holds off a bronze one for a few moments (Book 3, Ch24 L30).

**Silver and levitation.** "Levitation, Jason had discovered, was a perk of reaching silver-rank. It was an intrinsic property of a silver-rank soul, allowing the aura it projected to physically affect the environment" (Book 4, Ch73 L92). Farrah warns that it is "not even a practical ability. It requires intense concentration, has minimal effect, and is easy to disrupt with just some basic aura suppression" (Book 4, Ch73 L93). At silver it was "mostly just a power to levitate that helped their increasingly heavy bodies walk on weaker surfaces or even water" (Book 6, Ch14 L6).

**Aura signatures.** An aura has a signature that places can record: Emir's cloud palace stores Jason's signature in its registry (Book 2, Ch24 L13) and cannot let him in when it changes (Book 2, Ch66 L8). A soul crest carries a unique imprint on the aura that stays the same even when the aura changes, "impossible to track or falsify through even the strongest magic" (Book 2, Ch70 L37).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Aura powers.** Some abilities are auras (Hegemony, Cleansing Breeze), which are a different thing from the aura itself: they are passive effects projected around the user (Book 1, Ch40 L37-41; Book 2, Ch9 L115-119). A person can have only one (see `Awakening_Stones.md`).
- **Physical force.** Most aura abilities cannot move objects. "An aura power common to the force confluence famously allowed auras to move things physically", and Jason's cannot (Book 8, Ch60 L5).
- **Perception and silver.** Farrah says her perception power does not improve her aura sense until silver rank (Book 1, Ch40 L52).
- **Monsters.** "Higher-rank monsters can" manipulate their auras; at low ranks the most to expect is "a few stealthy monsters that restrain their auras to hide better" (Book 1, Ch40 L76).
- **Transcendent echo.** Soul damage from gods can change an aura: after multiple divine presences press on him, Jason's "aura has been damaged by the direct, concerted focus of multiple transcendent-level entities", the recovery refines it, and it now carries "an echo of transcendent power" (Book 2, Ch65 L86-87).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **Titles from soul scars** (full list in Titles_and_Blessings.md). Jason's Interface uses titles to translate soul scars into game terms (Book 4, Ch2 L26). Resolute raises resistance to suppression from higher-ranked auras (Book 3, Ch14 L82); Indomitable enhances resistance and "ignores rank disparity", and adds a resistance bonus to allies in his aura (Book 4, Ch2 L23-24); Spirit Warrior increases both suppressive force and resistance, lets him resist other magical suppression, and lets him "attack their soul directly" after fully suppressing their aura (Book 3, Ch21 L151-152); Godless Prophet records the refinement after gods press on him (Book 2, Ch65 L85-86). Each changes his aura signature (Book 3, Ch14 L83; Book 3, Ch21 L153).
- **Rank disparity.** Aura strength is "the inherently superior strength of higher-rank auras", which some of Jason's titles do not cancel (Book 3, Ch21 L110).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Auras are just a feeling.** They can be used as weapons and as disguise.
- **Normals cannot feel them.** Normals can sense strong, directed auras on some level but are "aura blind" unless the wielder allows it (Book 4, Ch74 L14).
- **Suppressing an aura is simple.** Farrah calls it the hardest of the three controls (Book 1, Ch40 L70).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** In Book 3 Jason refines his suppression until he can hold off an iron-rank collar entirely (Book 3, Ch24 L30).
- **Level 2:** After his Book 4 return to Earth, Jason rebuilds his aura control from scratch to use on normal people with "art and finesse" (Book 4, Ch72 L38).
- **Level 3:** The Numen gift in Book 12 makes his avatar immune to rank suppression and lets him negate aura abilities by fully suppressing the user's aura (Book 12, Ch67 L196).
