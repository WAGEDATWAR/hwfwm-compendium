## World Primer: the Worlds of *He Who Fights With Monsters*

This primer is the foundation of the compendium. It explains the setting in plain language and summarises the facts that come up again and again. Later documents expand every section. Where a fact only becomes true later in the story it is marked **(Later: Book N)**.

---

### 1. The setting in one page

The series follows Jason Asano, an ordinary Australian who is pulled out of his world by an accident and arrives, hairless and alone, in **Pallimustus**: a world very like Earth in size and shape, but soaked in magic. There, people absorb **essences** (crystallised magic), which give them supernatural abilities, and grow through **ranks** by hunting **monsters** and training. Adventurers, guilds, gods and rulers all depend on this system.

---

### 2. Two worlds

#### 2.1 Pallimustus

The planet has a thin membrane to the astral, so magic is dense. Features of the world that follow from this:
- **Astral spaces** (pocket dimensions) attached to the planet through apertures such as waterfalls and caves.
- **Natural manifestations**: essences, awakening stones and monsters appear spontaneously.
- Animals include **heidels** (horse-sized, scaled, two-headed and horned, Book 1, Ch17 L106) in place of horses.

Because magic is concentrated regionally, **magic level** varies by place. Greenstone is low-magic: "mid-iron" density, which Clive calls very low (Book 3, Ch34 L60). The wiki calls Vitesse much denser (wiki; no book figure), and the place files give what the books say about Rimaros and others. Astral spaces and surges can be higher still. Magic level affects what monsters appear, how quickly people can advance, and even what technology works.

#### 2.2 Earth

- **The Engineers of Ascension (EOA)**: a technology-and-body-modification faction preparing for the revelation of magic.

### 3. How magic works (the basics)

#### 3.1 Essences

An **essence** is a manifestation of pure magic in the form of a small glass cube or crystal. Absorbing one (by ritual, or simply by holding and consuming it) imbues the person with five related **essence abilities**, one awakened at once and the rest later. A person can hold **four essences**; the fourth usually forms a **confluence** from the first three.

Essences come in rarities (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary), and no essence is simply better than another: a Duck essence in skilled hands can beat a Fox. Essences are named for their concept or animal (Fire, Wind, Blood, Dark, Sin, Dragon, Swift, Rat, Spider, Sword, Technology...). They show up in monster loot, are given by gods, and are collected and traded.

#### 3.2 Confluence essences

#### 3.3 Awakening stones

Each essence holds five abilities; absorbing the essence awakens one, and the rest require **awakening stones**, which are consumable crystals. The Magic Society grades a stone one to five stars by how often it appears (see `03_Magic_and_Adventuring/Awakening_Stones.md`); the stone's theme steers what it awakens; the essence matters most, but race, knowledge and personality also influence the result. Gods and astral beings sometimes give stones to followers.

#### 3.4 Abilities

Essence abilities come in types: **special attacks**, **spells**, **special abilities**, **auras**, **conjurations**, **summons and familiars** (often needing a ritual) and **rituals** in their own right. Abilities improve in rank as the user advances: an ability that begins as a single effect gains extra effects at each rank (Jason's Leech Bite, for instance, adds new afflictions at each stage). Abilities have mana and stamina costs and sometimes cooldowns. Many abilities inflict **afflictions** (lasting harmful conditions) or grant **boons**; the series gives them specific names.

#### 3.5 Attributes

#### 3.6 Quintessence, spirit coins and other magical materials

- **Quintessence** (called **affinity gems** on Earth) appears as gems of an essence's theme (for example Dark Quintessence Gems, Book 1, Ch6 L15; crafting material, Book 1, Ch7 L68) and is used in crafting.
- **Monster cores** are crystallised magic from monsters. Eating one raises abilities slightly; this is the fast path to advancement but it stalls most people around silver and makes gold very hard.
- **Spirit coins** are the currency of the magical world. They are concentrated magic that a ranked person can consume to sustain themselves. See section 7.
- Other staples: crystal wash (cleaning solution used after monster fights), healing unguents, recovery potions, skill books, dimensional bags.

#### 3.7 Rituals, formations and artifice

Beyond essence abilities there is "external magic": **ritual magic** (circles of chalk, ink or silver powder with chants), **formations and arrays** (large-scale rituals such as the Network's Grid), **alchemy** (potions, ointments), **artifice** (magical items, weapons, vehicles), and **skill books**, which impart skills by flooding the reader with text (they require consciousness and are strenuous).

#### 3.8 Familiars and summons

A **familiar** is a deliberately created creature bonded to its owner, normally via a ritual. A **summoned familiar** can merge with the summoner and dies with them; a **bonded familiar** survives its owner but cannot vanish into them. Familiars can have their own abilities. Jason's three familiars (Shade, Colin, Gordon) are central to him. Other examples: Stash (Humphrey), Onslow (Clive), Shimmer and Gemini (Belinda).

---

### 4. Ranks and power

Ranks measure the quality of a person's magic and are a social class system as much as a power scale.

| Rank | Earth term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Normal (unranked) | Category 0 | Ordinary people, including those with some essences |
| Iron | Category 1 | Four essences absorbed (Book 1, Ch15 L100). Digestion changes (Book 2, Ch34 L73); can live on spirit coins; sense auras |
| Bronze | Category 2 | All abilities unlocked and at bronze; much tougher |
| Gold | Category 4 | No need to breathe (Book 2, Ch34 L73); centuries-long life (Book 1, Ch22 L58) |
| Diamond | Category 5 | The peak of mortal power; few exist; rumoured not to die of old age |
| Transcendent | none | Gods and astral beings, effectively unkillable |

Facts that recur: people of a higher rank **suppress** those of lower rank ("tyranny of rank"); essence users age slower: bronze-rankers can live well past a hundred, silvers can double that, gold-rankers live for centuries, and it is rumoured that diamond-rankers cannot die of old age (Book 1, Ch22 L58); ranking up involves a "purge" in which impurities are expelled (often messy) and the body changes; advancing without monster cores is slower but leaves greater potential. **Half-transcendence** means peak diamond combined with transcending mortal nature.

Roles in society: iron and bronze make up most adventurers; silver are elite; gold are rare and politically important; diamonds are sovereign powers ("everyone gets one world-changing action", according to their etiquette). Title and respect follow rank.

---

### 5. Auras

An **aura** is the physical, intangible projection of a person's soul and will. It grows with rank. People learn to **project**, **retract** and **suppress** it; stronger auras can suppress weaker ones. Auras can carry emotions, blanket areas, reveal lies, or physically move matter at very high levels (messengers do this routinely). Uncontrolled gold-rank auras injure unranked people. Techniques include blending into a crowd (making an aura read as part of the ambient background) and masking (messenger devices that fake or block auras). **Titles** earned from deeds (Giant Slayer, Resolute, Indomitable, Godless Prophet...) alter a person's aura. **Soul crests** (see section 10) provide personal identification.

---

### 6. Monsters and surges

**Monsters** are magic made sentient without a soul. They manifest spontaneously where magic is dense, last from days to weeks, become more aggressive near the end, and dissolve into **rainbow smoke** leaving loot (cores, coins, materials, ointments, items). Monsters have ranks matching the quality of their magic. They range from hamsters and eels (lesser) to hydras, dragons, kraken and leviathans. Some are intelligent (the Dinosaur King, springclaw gorillas) and some come from specific origins (construct creatures, vorger from the deep astral, apocalypse beasts such as the sanguine horror and the world-taker worm).

Not every creature that looks like a monster is one: **magical creatures** (natural animals with magical traits) and **creatures** (Tartarian brutes, nagas) have souls and leave no rainbow smoke.

Adventurers hunt monsters because monsters left alone grow dangerous, produce loot and cores, and threaten settlements. The Adventure Society posts notices and contracts. Typical dangers: ambushes, tyranny of rank, afflictions, packs. Typical loot rules: usually only the one who kills or the party members get the loot (the System later automates this).

### 7. Economy, materials and daily life

- **Spirit coins** exist in lesser, iron, bronze, silver, gold and diamond forms. 100 lesser coins make one iron coin; each higher rank is ten times the last (Book 1, Ch36 L72; Book 1, Ch61 L65), so a gold coin equals about 100,000 lesser coins. A ranked person can eat a coin of their rank to restore resources; eating a higher coin temporarily raises them to that rank, with severe backlash. Coins from Jason bear his face and the words "PRODUCT OF JASON / G'DAY, MATE!" (Book 1, Ch2 L50-53).
- **Spirit coin farms** in low-magic regions are a major industry (Greenstone's biggest export).
- **Cloud constructs** (Jason's cloud flask, Emir's cloud palace) are magical buildings and vehicles made from cloud substance.
- **Daily staples** noticed by the series: crystal wash, healing unguents, strawberry-schnapps-flavoured recovery potions, elf and celestine fashion, tea, stews and sandwiches (Jason's cooking is a recurring feature). A recovery potion tasting of strawberry schnapps appears in Book 1, Ch6 L50, and crystal wash in Book 1, Ch32 L52.

### 8. Peoples (natures)

Each **nature** (race) has exactly six inherent **racial gifts** (Book 1, Ch15 L65) that can evolve with a blessing from a great astral being.

- **Humans**: the most common; faster advancement and special-attack affinity; the only native race of Earth that can use essences.
- **Elves**: spellcasters and healers; life and magic affinity; long-lived.
- **Leonids**: lion-headed, strong and fast (e.g. Gary).
- **Draconians**: scaled, fire-breathing, claim dragon descent.
- **Smoulder**: earth-and-fire folk; the **brighthearts** are a magical sub-species adapted to the deep.
- **Runics**: blue-black skin with glowing runes.
- **Outworlders**: people from another world whose souls rebuilt a body. They have unusual gifts (interface, quest system, inventory, map, "mysterious stranger" language adaptation) and cannot be easily tracked.
- **Messengers**: winged, gestalt beings who are not essence-users (see section 9).

### 10. Souls, death and resurrection

- The **soul** is the person; the body is its expression. Healing magic uses the soul as a blueprint. **Soul scars** are permanent. **Soul crests** are magical tattoos printed on the soul, which appear on the body (Book 2, Ch70 L34-42).
- **Outworlders** are souls that wandered into the deep astral and rebuilt a body on arrival.
- **Reincarnation** exists: a soul renews as a different person (e.g. Gary).

### 11. Societies and institutions

- **The Adventure Society**: global monster-hunting organisation. Members wear badges that track kills and locate the body. Stars (1-3) rate judgement, not power. It runs notice boards, contracts, orders, field assessments and branch offices run by directors. A **Continental Council** supervises branches. **Guilds** (Burning Violet, Sapphire Crown...) are elite organisations whose members receive resources.
- **The Magic Society**: records essences, regulates spirit coin farms, runs the water link, researches magic, and holds the "living document" of essence combinations.
- **The Alchemy Association**: guild of alchemists.
- **The Church** (plural): a collection of religious organisations; the Healer's church is the largest.
- **Noble houses and monarchies**: kingdoms (the Storm Kingdom, Estercost), duchies, city-states. Diamond-rank rulers exist.

## Abilities and Attributes

### 1. Summary

An essence ability is a power an essence user has from an essence. Each essence gives five; one is awakened at absorption and the rest are awakened later by stones. Each ability has a type, a cost, a cooldown and a rank, and gains a new effect at each rank. Four attributes, Power, Speed, Spirit and Recovery, each bond to one of the four essences and rise in rank as that essence's abilities are mastered. Abilities and attributes together decide when a person can move up a rank.

### 2. How it works

**The five abilities.** An essence "imbues 1 awakened essence ability and 4 unawakened essence abilities" (Book 1, Ch20 L52). The awakened one is a function of the essence and the person: Jason's dark essence gave Midnight Eyes at absorption (Book 1, Ch8 L35), blood gave Blood Harvest (Book 1, Ch21 L7), sin gave Punish (Book 1, Ch21 L50) and doom gave Inexorable Doom (Book 1, Ch21 L62). Further abilities come from awakening stones (see `Awakening_Stones.md`). A full set is twenty.

**Types.** The System sorts abilities into types that show on each ability's description, with keywords after them. The types visible in the books are special attack, spell, special ability, aura, conjuration and familiar (with summon and ritual tags), plus a separate list of racial abilities. Examples from Jason's screens:
- Special attack (melee, drain, wounding, blood): Leech Bite (Book 1, Ch27 L28).
- Spell (drain): Blood Harvest (Book 1, Ch21 L15).
- Special ability (dimension, teleport): Path of Shadows (Book 1, Ch22 L116).
- Aura (holy, unholy): Hegemony (Book 1, Ch40 L37).
- Conjuration (darkness, light, dimension): Cloak of Night (Book 1, Ch8 L62).
- Familiar (ritual, summon): Sanguine Horror (Book 1, Ch23 L62).

**Cost, cooldown, rank.** An ability screen lists a cost in mana, stamina or both, in words (low, moderate, and so on), a cooldown (none, or a time such as forty seconds) and a current rank with a percentage: "Current rank: Iron 0 (00%)" (Book 1, Ch22 L117-119). Mirage Step costs "low stamina and mana" with a forty-second cooldown (Book 2, Ch8 L174-175).

**Attributes.** The four attributes are Power, Speed, Spirit and Recovery. Each essence bonds to one of them and raises it from normal to iron: "[Blood Essence] has bonded to your [Power] attribute" (Book 1, Ch21 L6), dark to Speed (Book 1, Ch8 L34), sin to Recovery (Book 1, Ch21 L49), and doom to Spirit (Book 1, Ch21 L61). "Master all blood essence abilities to increase your [Power] attribute" (Book 1, Ch21 L6). Once an essence's abilities are all awakened, the linked attribute "will advance in conjunction with lowest-rank" ability of that essence (Book 2, Ch8 L171).

**Knowing what an ability does.** Ordinary essence users must feel their way into a power by instinct; "unlike other essence users, Jason had never been forced to fathom out his abilities by feel", because he has System messages (Book 3, Ch20 L17). The Magic Society keeps records of what abilities do at later ranks, and Clive urges Jason to look them up (Book 2, Ch9 L31-35).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Variable subtypes.** A spell can have "variable subtypes, contingent on effect" (Book 2, Ch50 L57).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Sophie.** Mirage Step is the last of her five mystic abilities (Book 2, Ch8 L170-171), and she awakened confluence abilities before her essence abilities, which Clive calls "quite unusual" (Book 2, Ch8 L45).
- **Clive.** His Enact Ritual makes drawing and performing rituals more convenient (Book 2, Ch6 L43).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Strong abilities are better.** Farrah and Rufus both lay out a scale from individually strong abilities to abilities that "underwhelm in isolation, but used together become very dangerous", with affliction specialists at the second end (Book 1, Ch22 L88-93). "Simple doesn't mean bad" (Book 1, Ch22 L90).
- **Combat power matters most.** Gary: "just having a bunch of destructive combat powers makes you less useful to a team", and a few good utility powers help one get jobs (Book 1, Ch22 L111).

## The Adventuring Profession

### 1. Summary

Adventuring is the work of killing monsters and clearing hazards for pay. In practice an adventurer is a licensed contractor: they take posted notices and contracts from a jobs hall, work in a team, and are paid in spirit coins and loot. Most are not heroes in the story sense, and the profession is as much about judgement and bookkeeping as combat. This file describes how the work runs. The organisation is described in `05_Organisations/Adventure_Society.md`.

### 2. How it works

**What an adventurer does.** Rufus: "The bulk of our job as adventurers is hunting them down before they reach that stage", meaning the aggressive end of a monster's life (Book 1, Ch19 L36). Farrah and Rufus both reject the idea that adventuring is "just going places and killing everyone you find" (Book 1, Ch19 L37-38).

**Notices.** A monster notice, "whether from a notice board or the Adventure Society directly, has three pieces of information, so long as that information is known. The name of the monster, or a description. The number of monsters, and the approximate location" (Book 1, Ch57 L33).

**The jobs hall.** The jobs hall is "an annex of the main administration building", "a moderate-sized room divided into rows by standing bulletin boards" with a desk where a clerk records contracts (Book 1, Ch63 L54; Book 1, Ch64 L5). Contracts for areas near the city are in the hall; "those further afield were posted on each town or village's noticeboard", and every month the Society sends people to patrol those areas for a full month away, "not a popular task" (Book 1, Ch64 L3). A director can change how notices are posted: Greenstone's new director stopped keeping a separate board for incentivised contracts (Book 1, Ch63 L81).

**Pay and bonuses.** The Society pays iron spirit coins for an iron-rank monster contract, ten up to a hundred depending on number, travel and difficulty; bonuses can run "all the way up to an awakening stone, although such a reward was extremely rare" (Book 1, Ch64 L12). The Society "was known to give out awakening stones for exceptional service, although rarely" (Book 1, Ch64 L81). As a rule, "we don't give out awakening stones to people for killing monsters above their rank. It would just incentivise people getting themselves killed trying to jump ranks" (Book 1, Ch91 L34).

**Badges and stars.** A new member gets a badge, a circular iron medallion embossed with a sword and rod crossed over a shield, "one-star, iron rank" (Book 1, Ch62 L61, L66). The ranking system "has two parts": the first is the member's essence rank, and the second is stars, "not an assessment of your power, but your judgement" (Book 1, Ch62 L68-70). Everyone begins at one star and the maximum is three (Book 1, Ch62 L70). One-star contracts "are pure monster hunts, with no complicated elements to deal with" (Book 1, Ch62 L70). Two stars mean "at least some level of actual, unsupervised responsibility", investigating "potentially dangerous situations or unknown phenomena", and leading small expeditions of one-star members (Book 1, Ch62 L82). Three stars means "they trust you to handle yourself when things get political", and three-star members are "expected to anticipate and manage consequences at a higher level" (Book 1, Ch62 L85-86). "You can expect to drop a star rank each time you increase a tier in power" (Book 1, Ch62 L91). The badge confers prestige, not authority: "any actual authority comes from the contract you are carrying out" (Book 1, Ch62 L64). Society officials are outside the rating system (Book 1, Ch62 L72). The badge does not track the wearer if their aura resists tracking (Book 1, Ch62 L55-59).

**Teams.** "Rick's team had an archetypal distribution of roles, with a couple of resilient front-liners, some damage specialists and a healer. Not every team could find a good healer... team dynamics were as much about the balance of personalities as the balance of powers" (Book 1, Ch66 L60). When a team loses a front-liner it needs a new one (Book 2, Ch15 L38; Book 2, Ch29 L8). A group works "as a team, instead of as individuals" against a bronze monster, directed by a leader (Book 1, Ch77 L60).

**Loot.** Teams split loot as a group (Book 2, Ch21 L14) and keep records for splitting later: Jason "took out a notebook and recorded all the loot for splitting up later" (Book 2, Ch48 L61). Choices can reflect need: the team lets Jason and Henrietta split an elemental's loot because each has expensive summoning rituals to pay for (Book 3, Ch14 L79). Rufus gives spare summoning materials to Jason when dividing loot (Book 2, Ch63 L111).

**Retirement and longevity.** Silver-rank arena professionals "keep going until silver-rank longevity is no longer enough, at which point they retire and make their way to gold for the extended life span" (Book 2, Ch73 L14). Gary retires after Book 3 to become a full-time weaponsmith (Book 3, Ch69 L895). Silver-rankers in low-magic Greenstone are "semi-retired at best" (Book 1, Ch61 L32).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Rule of thumb.** Rufus's rule of thumb is to handle monsters one rank up only with a good match-up (Book 2, Ch47 L59).
- **Arena sport.** Iron-rank monster-hunt events judge teams on performance against identical monsters (Book 2, Ch73 L37).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Rick Geller's team**, with front-liners, damage dealers and a healer (Book 1, Ch66 L60).
- **Jason's first contract** logged at the jobs hall desk (Book 1, Ch64 L5).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **The badge is authority.** It is not; the contract carries it (Book 1, Ch62 L64).
- **Higher stars mean more power.** Stars measure judgement, not power (Book 1, Ch62 L70).
- **Adventuring is killing for sport.** Rufus refuses to work with those who relish it (Book 1, Ch19 L38).

### 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** Jason's one-star iron badge is the starting point (Book 1, Ch62 L66); Greenstone's jobs hall changes with a new director (Book 1, Ch63 L77).

## Afflictions, Boons and Damage

### 1. Summary

An affliction is a condition that harms its target for a time; a boon is a condition that helps. Both are named in brackets in System messages, carry tags (poison, disease, curse, holy, unholy, magic and others) and can stack. Defence against afflictions is a game of resisting, cleansing and negating: a cleanse removes an affliction, resistance reduces the chance or strength of one landing, and some afflictions are built to defeat both. In this world a character who understands which tool beats which condition has a large tactical advantage, and Jason's power set is built around that interplay.

### 2. How it works

**Resistance.** Resistance is a chance and strength modifier. Jason's Sin Eater increases his own resistance to afflictions and gives him Resistant each time he resists or cleanses one (Book 1, Ch28 L93). His Hegemony aura raises allies' resistance and lowers enemies', and lowers it further for each instance of Sin (Book 1, Ch40 L41). In battle only a fraction land: "as many as one in three afflictions were taking hold" against a flesh monster in Book 2 (Book 2, Ch52 L51), and "almost half" against a lizard after resistance-lowering stacks (Book 3, Ch8 L76).

**Cleansing.** A cleanse removes afflictions. Jason's Feast of Absolution removes "curses, diseases, poisons and unholy afflictions", plus holy afflictions if the target is an ally, from a target other than himself, and it "circumvents all effects that prevent cleansing" (Book 1, Ch34 L40); a few chapters later, at Iron 1 (19%), the same spell removes holy and unholy afflictions from an ally or an enemy and "circumvents any resistance or immunity to cleanse effects" (Book 1, Ch39 L70-71). Cleansing triggers other abilities: "Cleansing afflictions has triggered [Sin Eater]" (Book 1, Ch34 L47). A cleansing aura, such as Cleansing Breeze, adds resistance to curses, diseases, magic afflictions, poisons and unholy afflictions and negates poisons in the air; it is a holy effect (Book 2, Ch9 L119).

**Cleanse conditions.** Many afflictions are written to resist cleansing: Mark of Sin "cannot be cleansed while target retains any instances of Sin or Legacy of Sin" (Book 1, Ch87 L99). A curse can protect another: a patient arrives with two curses, one adding poison to the other, and "the curse that kept making more of the other two couldn't be cleansed until the other curse was cleansed" (Book 1, Ch103 L130).

**Holy and unholy.** Effects carry a holy or unholy tag, and some effects cannot touch them. Hendren's ongoing cleansing powers "could not eliminate holy effects", and he had only one power that could (Book 3, Ch59 L38). Another ability grants resistance to curses, magic and unholy afflictions, and bars unholy boons (Book 3, Ch67 L93). Hegemony is "both a holy and unholy ability at the same time", which fascinates Jason (Book 1, Ch40 L42).

### 3. Damage types

- **Physical and elemental.** Normal cutting, piercing, fire and similar damage. Rank gives damage reduction against lower-rank sources (Book 1, Ch21 L67).

## Alchemy, Artifice and Magical Materials

### 1. Summary

Alongside essences and stones the world has a layer of working magic that does not need an essence: quintessence gems, spirit coins, potions, ointments, enchanted tools and growth items that rise in rank. This file describes what these materials are and how they are used. It does not list individual named items; those are in `09_Items_and_Artefacts/`.

### 2. How it works

**Quintessence.** Quintessence is "kind of like chunks of essence" and "gets used a lot more" than essences: "Ritual magic, alchemy, weapon forging" (Book 1, Ch19 L47-52). It appears as small gems named for their theme: dark quintessence is "small black gems" (Book 1, Ch7 L68). Water quintessence forms when magic-rich water from an astral space reaches the sea and condenses "huge quantities of magically-imbued water into a little crystal" (Book 1, Ch24 L40). Villages along the coast live by finding it (Book 1, Ch24 L48). Gary says he goes "through quintessence by the pile" for weapons and armour (Book 1, Ch19 L53). A quintessence gem can be used directly in an item: a bracelet "keeps the wearer cool and refreshed" with a water gem, and a gem refills its energy (Book 1, Ch31 L27-28; Book 1, Ch68 L114). A quintessence pile cannot be made into an essence (Book 1, Ch19 L48-50).

**Spirit coins.** A spirit coin is a crystal in the colour of a rank; the iron-rank coin "had the metal-grey colour of iron but was actually made of crystal" (Book 1, Ch22 L77). Eaten, it "dissolved with an intense fizzing sensation... tangy and energetic" and restores power (Book 1, Ch22 L77). Ranked people "sustain themselves on spirit coins and go without food entirely" (Book 3, Ch1 L5). A coin an hour or so can replenish reserves, with no food for a day or more (Book 3, Ch14 L18), and captors give an unconscious captive a coin to eat to keep him healthy (Book 3, Ch17 L26). Coins are grown on farms, where "special moulds cause magic to crystallise", and changes in ambient magic can ruin whole batches (Book 1, Ch80 L21, L43). A vehicle runs on coins fed into a slot (Book 1, Ch80 L104). The currency role is in `Economy_and_Money.md`; the exchange rates are "a full hundred lesser coins to one iron-rank coin, after which denominations went up in multiples of ten" (Book 1, Ch36 L72).

**Potions.** A recovery potion restores health, stamina and mana; "until the remnant magic fully dissipates, consuming further health, stamina or mana potions will result in toxic side-effects" (Book 1, Ch6 L53-54). Potions come in ranks: Jason's first is a bronze-rank, rare Recovery Potion (Book 1, Ch6 L46). Healers' patients are limited by cost: both ritual magic and alchemy have ways to heal, "but the cost and expertise required placed both out of the reach for most people" (Book 1, Ch35 L22).

**Alchemy.** Alchemy is brewing potions and elixirs. Jory's "true calling was alchemy", and his research is to make products cheaper and simpler: "If I can make alchemical products affordable to everyone, not only can it help a huge number of people, but it will open up huge new markets" (Book 1, Ch35 L10, L21). Alchemy and weapon forging use quintessence (Book 1, Ch19 L52). Alchemists belong to the Alchemy Association, which Jory joins alongside the Adventure Society (Book 1, Ch36 L28; Book 1, Ch38 L52). Alchemy sits beside medicine as an everyday tool: the shaving cream Jason mentions came from "an alchemist friend" (Book 1, Ch59 L8).

**Crafting.** Monster materials are crafting inputs: snakeskin and dark quintessence are crafting materials (Book 1, Ch7 L68). Gary makes weapons and armour (Book 1, Ch19 L53). Magic craftspeople range from leatherworkers and weapon-smiths to engravers and alchemists (Book 2, Ch42 L86).

**Growth items.** A growth item gains forms and powers as its rank rises when fed the right materials. Emir's cloud palace "is a growth item" (Book 2, Ch40 L61). Jason's cloud flask is "iron rank [growth], legendary", bound to him, and its upgrade to bronze needs 1000 bronze air quintessence, 1000 bronze water quintessence, 200 bronze dimension quintessence and 10,000 bronze spirit coins (Book 2, Ch74 L40-50). Legendary growth weapons come in matched sets (Book 2, Ch51 L98; Book 2, Ch53 L70).

**Soul-bound items.** A soul-bound growth item "cannot be used by anyone else" and "tends to take on" its owner's properties as it advances; Clive says that "if you meet someone who seems like a good person but has a hideous and twisted soul-bound item, stay clear" (Book 2, Ch74 L41, L72-74). Emir's cloud palace, "outrageously grandiose, yet welcoming and beautiful", reflects him (Book 2, Ch74 L74). Soul-bound items "are rare, even compared to other growth items", and "The advancement requirements are usually quite prohibitive" (Book 2, Ch74 L45).

**Other tools.** Crystal wash is an odourless cleaner that "clears off everything"; its absence is a running problem (Book 1, Ch32 L57). Recording crystals capture images, messages and fights (Book 1, Ch54 L52; Book 1, Ch61 L129). Dimensional storage includes bags (Book 1, Ch17 L21); Jason's inventory has forty spaces, with stacking (Book 1, Ch61 L40).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Low-magic zones.** Coin farms work only where magic is stable: "No activity, no life. Very stable ambient magic" (Book 1, Ch80 L21).
- **Divine supply.** A church can supply healing essences and stones (Book 1, Ch35 L23).
- **Prices.** Coins bought in bulk go through brokers; an adventurer can be paid in coins up to an awakening stone for a contract (Book 1, Ch64 L12).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Jory's clinic**, an alchemy lab and medical clinic in one building (Book 1, Ch35 L63).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Quintessence can replace essences.** It cannot (Book 1, Ch19 L48-50).
- **Healing potions are always safe to chain.** They cause toxic side effects until the remnant magic dissipates (Book 1, Ch6 L54).
- **Soul-bound items are interchangeable.** They are bound and reflect the owner (Book 2, Ch74 L41, L72).

## Auras

### 1. Summary

An aura is the projection of a ranked person's soul. It grows with rank, is read by other ranked people as a sense of danger, strength and mood, and is how adventurers judge each other before words are spoken. Controlling the aura is a skill with three basic parts: projecting it, restraining it and suppressing other people's. Auras can be hidden, masked, aimed at normal people to intimidate them, and, at silver rank, begin to lift the body off the ground.

### 2. How it works

**Aura sense.** A new iron-ranker gains the ability to sense auras (Book 1, Ch21 L69). Jason's first impression of other ranked people is a feeling of danger, which Rufus explains is "our auras. Now that you're iron rank, you can sense them", and which gets more reliable as he learns to tell "a monster from an essence user from a regular person" (Book 1, Ch22 L31-33). The sense is "not completely reliable" because "some monsters can hide their auras. People can too, if they have an aura power" (Book 1, Ch22 L35). Ordinary people are "faint, barely detectable, while those with essences were much clearer"; a village normally has one or two with an essence, and a full-set iron-ranker "radiated out like a beacon" (Book 1, Ch35 L50).

**Why it matters.** Anyone with a perception power will eventually see auras clearly, so an uncontrolled aura reads like signs, including location and emotions, and "know when you lie" (Book 1, Ch40 L50). The skill with which an aura is controlled "is how adventurers make their first impressions on one another. If you can't do it properly, people won't take you seriously. Excellent aura manipulation marks you as an adventurer of training and distinction" (Book 1, Ch40 L80). Farrah calls the lack of an aura ability a serious gap: "anyone who hits bronze rank and can't manage their aura is a second-rate adventurer" (Book 1, Ch39 L152).

**A gold-ranker's aura.** At gold an uncontrolled aura is dangerous to normal people: "If you reach gold rank and can't control your aura properly, you can hurt normal people just by going near them" (Book 1, Ch40 L44). Such people are required to wear a suppressor: Farrah once saw "a gold ranker who forgot to put on an aura suppressor before going to a market. People were passed out, bleeding out their ears" (Book 1, Ch40 L46-48).

**Masks and suppressors.** Magic items hide or damp an aura. Farrah mentions aura suppressors (Book 1, Ch40 L46); a thief's methods include "aura masking" (Book 1, Ch100 L85); an aura mask is breached by using essence abilities or failing to keep a strong aura suppressed (Book 1, Ch102 L93); aura masking will not work against a tracking effect on the aura (Book 1, Ch103 L16). A suppression collar can negate a target's powers; Jason is able to "completely negate the effects of an iron-rank suppression collar" after practice, and holds off a bronze one for a few moments (Book 3, Ch24 L30).

**Aura signatures.** An aura has a signature that places can record: Emir's cloud palace stores Jason's signature in its registry (Book 2, Ch24 L13) and cannot let him in when it changes (Book 2, Ch66 L8). A soul crest carries a unique imprint on the aura that stays the same even when the aura changes, "impossible to track or falsify through even the strongest magic" (Book 2, Ch70 L37).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Aura powers.** Some abilities are auras (Hegemony, Cleansing Breeze), which are a different thing from the aura itself: they are passive effects projected around the user (Book 1, Ch40 L37-41; Book 2, Ch9 L115-119). A person can have only one (see `Awakening_Stones.md`).
- **Perception and silver.** Farrah says her perception power does not improve her aura sense until silver rank (Book 1, Ch40 L52).
- **Monsters.** "Higher-rank monsters can" manipulate their auras; at low ranks the most to expect is "a few stealthy monsters that restrain their auras to hide better" (Book 1, Ch40 L76).
- **Transcendent echo.** Soul damage from gods can change an aura: after multiple divine presences press on him, Jason's "aura has been damaged by the direct, concerted focus of multiple transcendent-level entities", the recovery refines it, and it now carries "an echo of transcendent power" (Book 2, Ch65 L86-87).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Rank disparity.** Aura strength is "the inherently superior strength of higher-rank auras", which some of Jason's titles do not cancel (Book 3, Ch21 L110).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Suppressing an aura is simple.** Farrah calls it the hardest of the three controls (Book 1, Ch40 L70).

### 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** In Book 3 Jason refines his suppression until he can hold off an iron-rank collar entirely (Book 3, Ch24 L30).

## Awakening Stones

### 1. Summary

An awakening stone is a consumable crystal that awakens one essence ability the user has not yet awakened. It is the third kind of non-living magic Rufus lists, after essences and quintessence (Book 1, Ch19 L56). The stone does not choose the ability; it steers toward a theme, and what comes out depends mostly on the essences the user holds. Stones are how a new essence user fills out the twenty abilities that a full set carries, and they are bought, auctioned, given as rewards and kept as family wealth.

### 2. How it works

**What a stone does.** Each stone's description says the same thing: "Awakens an essence ability", with the requirement "Unawakened essence ability" (Book 1, Ch22 L99-102). Jason is shown a count of how many abilities he still has to awaken, such as "You have 10 unawakened essence abilities" (Book 1, Ch22 L132). Stones can also be used in other kinds of magic: "sometimes awakening stones get used as part of very powerful rituals" (Book 1, Ch22 L45), as the cult did with its feast stones in the sacrifice chamber (Book 1, Ch19 L57-58).

**What decides the ability.** Farrah: "the biggest determinate of what ability you get is the essence it comes from" (Book 1, Ch19 L67). A stone has a theme, and the theme sets the range of powers: a feast stone is "related to the concept of a feast, but that can manifest in any of hundreds of powers" (Book 1, Ch19 L63), which Farrah corrects to "thousands" (Book 1, Ch19 L64). The abilities a person has already, and the essences they have used, steer the stone. This is why Farrah and Rufus advise taking every essence before using stones: otherwise "your abilities will lack focus" (Book 1, Ch21 L36-37).

**Rarity and specificity.** "The more rare an awakening stone is, the more specific the powers" (Book 1, Ch19 L64). A common stone "can give you any ability the rarest could", it just draws from a larger pool; "rare stones don't give out better abilities, just more specific ones" (Book 1, Ch19 L66). The stones people seek are those with a desirable specialty, and "stones with desirable specialties tend to be the most expensive" (Book 1, Ch22 L104). A mid-rarity stone is "almost always a useful utility power" (Book 1, Ch22 L110). A high-rarity stone is "more specialised" (Book 1, Ch39 L147-148) and restricts the possible abilities to just one or two types (Book 2, Ch57 L36).

**Grading.** The Magic Society grades stones on a scale of one to five stars "based on how frequently they are known to appear world-wide", working with brokers and the Adventure Society; Jason's ability also sorts them into five stages of rarity: common (one star), uncommon (two stars), up to epic (four stars, "the second highest rarity") and legendary (Book 2, Ch9 L9, L17). In the Adventure Society's wording, a stone of the apocalypse is "unranked, legendary" (Book 1, Ch22 L128) and a stone of omens "unranked, epic" (Book 1, Ch39 L137).

**Using one.** The stone melts into the user's hand or arm and sinks in without fanfare (Book 1, Ch22 L71, L112, L152), and Jason's system confirms "You have awakened the blood essence ability [Leech Bite]" (Book 1, Ch22 L72). Most people need a ritual for each stone, and a person with a whole row "going through a half-dozen rituals would take hours" (Book 1, Ch22 L38-42). Clive's essence ability, Enact Ritual, makes drawing and performing the circles more convenient (Book 2, Ch6 L43). Clive draws the circle and completes the ritual when a team member uses an uncommon stone of preparation (Book 2, Ch66 L66).

**Hunger afterwards.** A stone of the feast leaves Jason ravenous: "I think the stone of the feast made it worse" (Book 1, Ch22 L75). The cure is a spirit coin (Book 1, Ch22 L76-77).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Tendencies of a theme.** The text gives these: eyes "a good shot at giving a perception power" (Book 2, Ch5 L105); hand awakens empty-hand abilities and attacks; foot, unarmed attacks and movement abilities (Book 2, Ch5 L108-110); sky, a very good chance of a flight power, though "it could just as easily give you a special attack effective against enemies in the air" (Book 2, Ch9 L29, L38); purgation, "almost certainly some kind of cleansing ability" (Book 2, Ch9 L47); magus, "a common awakening stone, especially with humans... almost guaranteed a spell, which humans rarely awakened, with an outside chance of a coveted summoning or familiar power" (Book 1, Ch87 L91); omens, a hope for an aura power (Book 1, Ch39 L150).
- **Human aptitude.** Humans have an aptitude for special attacks and rarely awaken spells without a magus stone (Book 1, Ch87 L91).
- **One aura only.** A person can have just one aura ability; the stone that would give a second does not work (Book 2, Ch13 L70). A tablet can remove an existing aura ability (Book 2, Ch13 L76-78).
- **Divine stones.** Gods craft stones for a specific outcome. Knowledge sends Jason two: a Divine Awakening Stone of Inevitability, to bestow an aura power (Book 2, Ch13 L60-62; the ability is [Inescapable Doom] in one line and [Inevitable Demise] in another, Book 2, Ch13 L62, L66), and a Divine Awakening Stone of Persistence, to bestow a specific spell (Book 2, Ch13 L83-85), with a Soul-Purgation Tablet to make room (Book 2, Ch13 L71-76). Jason shuts the case, calling it "a shiny red apple" and a poison pill (Book 2, Ch13 L96, L122). A god can take back what it gave: Jason says "the idea of some god repossessing my magic powers doesn't appeal" (Book 2, Ch56 L93).
- **Non-essence uses.** The same stone items fuel rituals (see `Rituals_Arrays_and_Formations.md`).

## Confluences

### 1. Summary

A confluence is the fourth essence a person receives. It is not picked, bought or found. It is produced by the three essences already absorbed, and it arrives the moment the third is used. In the main run of the series a given trio of essences always gives the same confluence, and the Magic Society keeps a list of the known trios. Confluences are rated and talked about as if they were essences in their own right, and many of the best-known power sets belong to a confluence.

### 2. How it works

**Arrival.** Farrah explains that "once you use your third essence, a fourth one manifests itself on the spot" and that confluences are "a result of the three essences you already have"; her own fire, earth and potent gave the volcano essence (Book 1, Ch19 L30). Confluences "only manifest after three essences are used", and cannot be found loose: "You can't find a volcano essence anywhere" (Book 1, Ch19 L31).

**The scene.** When Jason uses his third essence, three shimmering cubes rise from his torso and merge into one new cube; the System tells him the "confluence of your essences has produced the doom essence", which he "may claim or reject", and that a refused confluence "will not be available to you again" (Book 1, Ch21 L53-54). Rufus's practical advice is that refusing means one "can only replace it with a normal essence" and "I promise you that is not the way to go" (Book 1, Ch21 L57). Only the clergy refuse, because their gods give them divine versions of regular essences (Book 1, Ch21 L57).

**Confluence abilities come late.** It is "quite unusual to awaken the confluence essence abilities first", and some advocates of a theory say people with a very strong sense of self do it (Book 2, Ch8 L45-49).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Rare essences, common results.** Sword, myriad and adept give master as readily as sword, swift and adept, in a different style: Lance, a spell-using elf, shares the confluence with Valdis, who relies on special attacks (Book 3, Ch1 L89-90).

## Essences

### 1. Summary

An essence is the most powerful and rarest of the forms in which magic condenses into something that is not alive. It is absorbed into a person's soul and gives that person a set of five abilities tied to its concept. Four essences make a full set. A person with a full set becomes iron rank and is changed by it. Essences are how Pallimustus makes essence users: the people who fight monsters, serve in the churches and run most of the world's institutions.

### 2. How it works

**What an essence is.** Rufus lists three non-living manifestations of magic: essences, "the most powerful manifestation" and "also, the rarest", then quintessence, "kind of like chunks of essence", and awakening stones (Book 1, Ch19 L45-56). A pile of quintessence cannot be used as an essence; "there's always some crackpot who claims to have figured it out, but it isn't possible" (Book 1, Ch19 L48-50). Clive's explanation is that the astral is "the source of all magic", that some worlds soak up a lot of it, and that this world's magic takes forms such as essences, stones, quintessence and monsters (Book 1, Ch69 L99). Essences appear as cubes or crystals: Jason's blood essence is a red cube whose slick surface looks wet but is dry and warm (Book 1, Ch19 L68); his sin essence is a white and gold cube (Book 1, Ch21 L44).

**Where they come from.** Magic "just starts manifesting all over the place" in a world as rich in it as this one (Book 1, Ch19 L33). They are also bought and sold. Jason trades a rat essence to a broker for two awakening stones (Book 1, Ch87 L91), brokers run regular essence and stone auctions (Book 1, Ch113 L61; Book 2, Ch70 L30), and after the Book 2 expedition the market price of essences and stones reached "an all-time low" (Book 3, Ch1 L100). Families pool money to buy essence sets for promising young people, and criminals buy powerful ones (Book 3, Ch1 L101). Dean's family could "afford a full set of essences" for him from a living made with water quintessence (Book 1, Ch96 L18). Gods can also supply essences (section 3).

**Absorption.** Absorbing an essence is done by an essence ritual (Book 1, Ch15 L113). Farrah offers to perform one for Jason (Book 1, Ch15 L112), and a ritual is ordinarily required for stones too (Book 1, Ch22 L38-41). The prompt that follows asks whether to absorb and reports "You have absorbed 2 of 4 essences" (Book 1, Ch21 L4). Absorption is painful. Jason passes out for the first (the dark essence turns to smoke and invades his body), the blood essence melts over his hand and burns into his flesh (Book 1, Ch21 L3), and the sin essence fills him like inflating pressure (Book 1, Ch21 L46). Farrah observes that each absorption seems easier and asks whether subjects adapt (Book 1, Ch21 L10-11). Once absorbed, "an essence cannot be relinquished or replaced" (Book 1, Ch20 L53).

**Five abilities, one awakened.** Each essence "imbues 1 awakened essence ability and 4 unawakened essence abilities" (Book 1, Ch20 L52). A full set therefore has twenty abilities, and a person "can't even properly progress towards bronze rank until you awaken every essence ability" (Book 1, Ch22 L68).

**Attribute bonding.** Each essence bonds to one attribute and raises it from normal to iron: blood to Power, dark to Speed, sin to Recovery and doom to Spirit in Jason's case (Book 1, Ch21 L6, L49, L61). Mastering all of an essence's abilities then raises that attribute (Book 1, Ch21 L6).

**Order of use.** Farrah and Rufus advise using all essences first and stones afterwards, because abilities are shaped by the essences and abilities a person already has; "awaken too many abilities before you have all your essences, and your abilities will lack focus" (Book 1, Ch21 L36-37).

**The fourth essence.** The first three are chosen; the fourth is a confluence and arrives by itself (Book 1, Ch19 L30). See `Confluences.md`.

**Iron rank.** The fourth essence completes the set. Jason's message lists the changes: damage reduction against normal-rank sources, increased resistance to normal-rank effects, the ability to sense auras, and the ability to sustain himself with concentrated magic (Book 1, Ch21 L65-70). The body expels impurities as it moves toward "a state of perfection" (Book 1, Ch22 L9). See `Ranks_and_Advancement.md`.

### 3. Variations and exceptions

**Outworlder absorption.** Jason absorbed his first essence without using a ritual ("I just kind of absorbed it") and thinks it was tied to the same power that lets him speak every language (Book 1, Ch15 L114). The ritual knowledge from the skill book tells him a ritual is normally required, "except, he knew from experience that wasn't always true" (Book 1, Ch15 L113).

**Racial difference.** Humans "have their essence abilities increase faster than other races" (Book 1, Ch15 L58). Abilities and their awakening differ by person as well as race (Book 1, Ch15 L57).

**Restricted essences.** The Adventure Society keeps a list of essences and combinations "that pose an inherent threat to ordinary people", with the death essence at the top of it by itself, "because it always seems to go wrong" (Book 1, Ch20 L42-44). Membership is impossible with a restricted essence (Book 1, Ch20 L44). The combinations that produce the undeath confluence almost all involve death (Book 1, Ch20 L19). The Magic Society's living document lists every known essence and combination, with restricted ones flagged, and Jason's sin essence is in "the rarest category" (Book 1, Ch20 L97-104).

**Rarity.** Essences vary in how often they appear and how common they are. Farrah says the sin essence is in the rarest category (Book 1, Ch20 L104). A rune essence reaches Clive as an "epic four-star essence" (Book 2, Ch9 L21). Low-rarity essences are the ones ordinary buyers afford (Book 1, Ch108 L28). The rat essence is "not popular with anyone who could afford to be choosy" (Book 1, Ch87 L91).

**Not all essences are simple.** An essence can seem to carry a meaning far from its name: Neil says it takes arrogance to absorb "the essence of defying the gods", and Jason replies that the sin essence is not about defying gods for him (Book 3, Ch8 L37-44).

### 4. Examples from the books

- Jason: dark, blood and sin, giving the doom confluence (Book 1, Ch20 L110; Ch21 L54-62).
- Farrah: fire, earth and potent, giving volcano (Book 1, Ch19 L30).
- Sophie: swift, balance and wind, giving mystic (Book 2, Ch5 L82-87).
- Belinda: magic, trap and adept, giving charlatan (Book 2, Ch32 L67-72).
- Clive: magic, rune and balance, giving karmic (Book 2, Ch28 L56).
- Neil: shield, growth and renewal, giving prosperity (Book 2, Ch28 L44).

The full list of essences and who holds them is in `Essence_Catalogue.md`.

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Rarer means better.** For stones, Rufus says "rare stones don't give out better abilities, just more specific ones" (Book 1, Ch19 L66); the same applies in spirit to essences, whose power comes from concept and user.
- **You can make an essence from quintessence.** Farrah calls it impossible (Book 1, Ch19 L50).
- **A confluence can be found loose.** "You can't find a volcano essence anywhere" (Book 1, Ch19 L31).

## Familiars, Summons and Conjurations

### 1. Summary

A familiar is a creature made or called to serve one person for life. A summoned familiar is built by ritual from magical materials and a being drawn from the deep astral; a bonded familiar is a creature that answers a call. Familiars are different from the short-lived summons many essence users can conjure in the middle of a fight, and from conjurations, which are things drawn out of mana. All three are essence abilities and come from awakening stones or from the essences themselves; none can be used by anyone who lacks the necessary ritual knowledge.

### 2. How it works

**What a summoned familiar is.** Clive's explanation: "Summoning a familiar is like deliberately creating a monster. A chunk of magic is brought into our world and forms a body. What makes it different from a monster is that it also summons a creature from the deep astral. Such entities are purely magical, like a soul. They normally can't exist in physical reality, any more than we can exist in the astral. But they inhabit the body you've made. Give it a mind, and stability. So it doesn't break down and go berserk" (Book 1, Ch69 L127). An outworlder's body is akin to a monster's or a summoned familiar's: "physical substance forged out of raw magic" (Book 2, Ch25 L59). Summoned familiars from the deep astral are "an endless supply of bizarre and terrifying horrors" (Book 2, Ch64 L15).

**Summoning versus calling.** Farrah explains the two kinds. A summoning "is a more elaborate ritual. Unlike a regular summoning, you should only need it each time you go up a rank. Unless your familiar gets killed, in which case you'll have to summon it back." A call-type power works differently: "a creature that has an affinity to that call will come and form a bond with the person. Less costly than summoning, but if that kind of familiar dies, you can't just summon it again" (Book 1, Ch23 L42-44).

**Summoned versus bonded.** A bonded familiar survives its owner's death; a summoned one does not, but a summoned familiar "can disappear into the summoner's body, which bonded familiars can't" (Book 1, Ch75 L27-28). A bonded familiar can be sensed at all times, a summoned one only while subsumed in the summoner (Book 3, Ch5 L71). Humphrey and his dragon Stash "could always sense one another... over any distance" (Book 2, Ch42 L70). Bonding is more reliable: "when going for a bonded familiar instead of a summoned one, things are much more likely to go your way, if you prepare accordingly", and "as close as you'll come to a guaranteed result with any awakening stone" (Book 1, Ch75 L33).

**The ritual.** To summon Jason's first familiar, he draws the circle with blood and magical materials while the rest watch; the circle and the pool of blood vanish into the floor "drawn back through the astral channel created by the summoning" (Book 1, Ch23 L61-70). Each rank-up needs a resummoning with materials suited to the new rank: Jason brings bronze-rank materials in advance and two sets for each familiar in case a vessel is destroyed (Book 3, Ch14 L78). The text lists a requirement for Shade's bronze resummoning as 343 dark quintessence gems (Book 2, Ch63 L92). Other circles can be simple: "Most people just need a circle of good old salt" (Book 1, Ch93 L41).

**Who can have one.** "You can only awaken that kind of essence ability if you already understand ritual magic" (Book 1, Ch23 L37). Jason's ritual skill book gave "the bare minimum" to be a proper ritual magician and is "how you awakened a familiar summoning power" (Book 1, Ch23 L36). Rufus has no summoning power because his obsession is swordsmanship (Book 1, Ch23 L35-37).

**Subsuming.** A summoned familiar "can temporarily disperse its body and place its spirit inside the summoner" (Book 1, Ch23 L82). Belinda's lantern familiar Shimmer sits in her eyes, turning them silver; purple eyes mark another's summoned familiar (Book 3, Ch5 L69). Belinda's familiar can see hidden enemies from her eyes and fire disruptive-force beams from them (Book 2, Ch72 L57).

**Summons that are not familiars.** A temporary summon "was not a summoned familiar but a temporary summons, like Gary's forge golem or Farrah's magma elemental. It would only last for a limited time, but he could afford to risk it in ways that he couldn't with a familiar" (Book 2, Ch28 L46). Farrah and Gary "call up short-lived monsters" as essence powers, and "Rituals that are also essence powers tend to be quick and dirty" (Book 1, Ch23 L38-39).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Swarm familiars are rare.** Rick: "Swarm-type familiars are really rare. I've seen more dragon and phoenix familiars" (Book 1, Ch68 L22).
- **Many familiars.** Henrietta keeps three summoned familiars inside her body and a fourth, a phoenix, bonded (Book 3, Ch5 L70). Jason and Clive each have summoned familiars (Book 2, Ch28 L48).
- **Appearance echoes the owner.** Clive notes that Jason's two familiars "are both reflective of your appearance" in his cloak (Book 2, Ch72 L133).
- **Apocalypse beast familiars.** Jason's first, the Sanguine Horror, is described as "an apocalypse beast", and Clive notes Jason "is not the only adventurer to have an apocalypse beast familiar" (Book 2, Ch63 L100; Book 3, Ch8 L33).
- **Powers not tied to a familiar.** Neil's summon "would only last for a limited time" and works best when fighting alone (Book 2, Ch28 L46).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Humphrey's dragon Stash.** A bonded familiar (Book 2, Ch42 L70).
- **Belinda's lantern.** A summoned astral lantern familiar called Shimmer (Book 2, Ch71 L50; Book 3, Ch5 L69).
- **Henrietta's phoenix.** The only bonded familiar among four she has, "rare and elusive" (Book 3, Ch5 L70).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **A familiar will eat you.** When Colin's swarm crawls into Jason's wounds, Gary asks if it is eating him; Farrah says, "It's his familiar" (Book 1, Ch23 L74-76).
- **Summoned means disposable.** It costs ritual materials at each rank-up (Book 3, Ch14 L78).
- **Bonded is better.** Jason's summoned familiars "had many advantages over a bonded familiar like Humphrey's, but a bond had its own advantages" (Book 2, Ch42 L70).

## Monster Cores and Training

### 1. Summary

An essence user can grow in power in two ways. One is to consume monster cores, which is easy and can be bought. The other is to train, fight real danger and meditate. Rufus's teaching in Book 1 is that the first method eats into the potential of the second. The setting's politics, from the aristocratic families of Greenstone to the Network on Earth, turn on which method a group has used, and on what it costs them later.

### 2. How it works

**Cores.** A monster core is a teardrop-shaped crystal dropped by a monster, red and more vibrant for higher ranks. It is "the magic core of an iron rank monster (crafting material, magic core)" (Book 1, Ch29 L79-81). The same cores are crafting material and the means of advancing. Lesser cores come from the lowest monsters (Book 1, Ch2 L32); iron, bronze and higher are labelled by rank (Book 1, Ch27 L94; Book 1, Ch99 L155).

**What a core does.** Rufus: "Every core increases your abilities a little, but only a little. It takes hundreds of iron-rank cores to reach bronze rank, and that's for humans. For everyone else, it takes even more. It takes iron-rank cores when you're iron rank, bronze when you're bronze, and so forth" (Book 1, Ch31 L96).

**What a core costs.** "Every time you use a monster core to raise your abilities, it makes the other method a little less effective. The impact is minimal, at first, but every core you use eats into your potential. If you used cores to get to where I am, the top end of bronze rank, then cores would the only thing that works anymore" (Book 1, Ch31 L98). Farrah describes the feel: "if you use magic cores to advance, you get to sleep in" (Book 1, Ch39 L30). The magic inside a person "feels unruly ... Like some wild creature", and for core users it is "like feeding the beast drugged meat to make it compliant. The beast still has its strength, but the owner can't make use of it properly" (Book 1, Ch31 L126-130).

**Who uses cores.** "In the city we're going to, Greenstone, almost everyone uses cores. So long as you have the money to buy them you can reach bronze rank without ever facing a monster. But every rank requires more and more cores. By the time you reach silver rank, things slow right down as the costs go up significantly. Most core users don't make it to gold" (Book 1, Ch31 L100). Most aristocratic families "only have a few truly powerful adventurers, while the rest use cores" (Book 1, Ch31 L102). Arena fighters rank up on cores, but the professionals "stop using cores before they hit gold" because "getting to gold rank using monster cores is a tough ask"; being at the peak of silver "keeps them at the top of their game" (Book 2, Ch73 L8-12). A grandmother taken in by a protective friend is given "money, essences and enough monster cores to rank her up to bronze" (Book 2, Ch64 L151).

**The real method.** Rufus lists three elements (Book 1, Ch31 L108-122):
1. **Training.** Push every attribute to its limits, not just physical ones: observation, memory games and puzzles train perception, "part of your spiritual strength" (Book 1, Ch31 L110-114).
2. **Fighting monsters.** "To truly break through your limits, you must truly push up against them. Only with genuine danger can you go further and do more than you ever thought possible" (Book 1, Ch31 L118).
3. **Meditation.** "The other two elements are about breaking through your own limits. Meditation is about consolidating that gain. It's where you take the fleeting moments in which you were better than you've ever been before, and make that your new normal" (Book 1, Ch31 L122).

**Aura training.** Farrah adds aura training, since "your chances of getting to gold start with a good foundation, which means aura powers and aura training" (Book 1, Ch40 L48). See `Auras.md`.

## Monsters

### 1. Summary

Monsters are the most common way magic manifests in a magic-rich world. They appear from nothing, usually in the wild, live for a time that depends on their rank, and then dissolve back into raw magic if not killed first. They have ranks like essence users; the ranks are not given by essences but by the density of the magic that makes them. Hunting them before they reach their aggressive final stage is the bulk of an adventurer's work, and what they leave behind funds much of the economy.

### 2. How it works

**Where they come from.** Rufus: "Most magic manifestations are monsters. They just appear, hopefully in the wilderness, but the magic they're made of isn't stable. Eventually they break down and dissolve back into magic. Killing them just makes it faster" (Book 1, Ch19 L34). The astral is "the source of all magic" and monsters are one of the forms the magic takes in a world that soaks up a lot of it (Book 1, Ch69 L99).

**Rank.** Monsters "exist within a certain power threshold. Whether an essence user or a monster, each rank has a suppressive effect on lower ranks" (Book 1, Ch19 L42). They do not use essences (Book 1, Ch19 L41-42). Rank "was a function of their magical density, not actual power": the strongest iron-rank monsters are stronger than the weakest bronze ones, with the difference usually made up in numbers (Book 1, Ch84 L57).

**Lifespan and aggression.** "Lesser monsters only last a week or two. They start so close to breaking down that they're aggressive from the moment they appear, but they aren't really a threat. An old woman with a broom can handle them. Iron rank monsters last about a month, getting aggressive in the final week or so. It goes up from there" (Book 1, Ch19 L40). "Monsters frequently aren't a problem when they first manifest, but as they get closer to breaking down they become highly aggressive. The bulk of our job as adventurers is hunting them down before they reach that stage" (Book 1, Ch19 L36). A region that has gone without hunters for two months will have iron-rank monsters in their berserk stage, while bronze monsters that spawned in that period will not have reached it (Book 3, Ch7 L5).

**Rank-up rule of thumb.** "A good adventurer should be able to handle monsters one rank up, so long as the match-up is good" (Book 2, Ch47 L59).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Astral entities.** Nightmare hags are "diamond-rank entities from the astral that have no physical existence in this place and cannot harm you directly" (Book 2, Ch57 L68).
- **Apocalypse beasts.** A creature "made from alchemy, blood and things best left unmentioned" can be a sanguine horror, an apocalypse monster that Jason says "can scour a world of life" (Book 1, Ch12 L70; Book 1, Ch71 L65).
- **Monsters as familiars.** A familiar is made like a deliberate monster, with a creature from the deep astral inhabiting it (Book 1, Ch69 L127).

## Ranks and Advancement

### 1. Summary

Rank is the world's measure of power. It runs from normal people through iron, bronze, silver, gold and diamond; beyond diamond are gods and great astral beings, who are not part of the ladder. Essence users and monsters share the ladder, and each rank "has a suppressive effect on lower ranks" (Book 1, Ch19 L42). Rank changes the body, the length of life, what a person can do with their aura and the way other people treat them. It is the setting's class system.

### 2. How it works

**The ladder.** Normal people with fewer than four essences are unranked. Four essences make iron rank (Book 1, Ch15 L100; Book 1, Ch21 L65-66). Rufus names diamond as the highest rank (Book 1, Ch22 L50-51). The sequence in the text is iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond (Book 1, Ch22 L58).

**What iron does.** The System lists what iron gives Jason: damage reduction against normal-rank damage, increased resistance to normal-rank effects, aura sense, and the ability to sustain himself with concentrated magic (Book 1, Ch21 L67-70). Reaching iron "made your body advance closer to a state of perfection, which included purging your body of impurities" (Book 1, Ch22 L9). Iron-rankers then eat spirit coins: "Then you can just eat some coins as well" (Book 1, Ch19 L28); with a full set of essences a person "doesn't need to eat or drink anymore", though they can for pleasure (Book 1, Ch15 L85).

**Advancing past iron.** A person cannot "properly progress towards bronze rank" until every essence ability is awakened (Book 1, Ch22 L68). The System then reports "Progress to bronze rank: 50% (4/4 essences complete)" (Book 3, Ch14 L98), counting essences whose abilities are complete. An ability held back says "cannot advance further until all attributes have reached bronze rank" (Book 3, Ch15 L56). See `Abilities_and_Attributes.md`. Methods of advancing, and how monster cores damage them, are in `Monster_Cores_and_Training.md`.

**Lifespan.** "All essence users age slower. At iron rank you wouldn't notice the difference, but bronze rankers can live well past a hundred. Silvers can double that; reach silver rank young enough and you'll look young for decades. Gold rankers live for centuries, and I'm not even sure diamond rankers can die of old age" (Book 1, Ch22 L58). Rufus calls the idea that diamond rank is immortal "a rumour, but a persistent one" and says there is an unwritten rule that diamond rankers do not share their limits (Book 1, Ch22 L60). The same rule is part of why diamond rankers are "mysterious figures pursuing goals known only to them" (Book 1, Ch22 L55).

**Badges.** Adventure Society badges are tied to the wearer's aura: "Adventurers need new badges at each rank for a reason", because a change in rank changes the aura and tracking stones stop working (Book 3, Ch32 L88).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

**Rank suppression.** "Whether an essence user or a monster, each rank has a suppressive effect on lower ranks. We're all bronze rank. If you were to fight any of us, your iron-rank abilities would have much less effect" (Book 1, Ch19 L42). Resistances grow with rank disparity: a skilled iron-ranker's afflictions are shrugged off by a higher-ranked enemy (Book 1, Ch87 L137). A bandit leader assumes Jason's spells failed "due to rank disparity" (Book 3, Ch28 L78). A silver-ranker fights as "silver-rank was silver-rank and the monster didn't die" even against attacks that ignore disparity (Book 3, Ch69 L808). Spirit coins offer a temporary workaround: "You can overcome that briefly by boosting your attributes with spirit coins... not for very long" and it "will leave you weaker once the strength fades" (Book 1, Ch19 L43).

**Ignoring disparity.** Some abilities and titles remove part of the penalty. A System text from Jason's Book 3 title says: "Ignore the enhanced resistances derived from rank disparity. This only affects the enhanced resistance from being higher rank, not other sources of resistance" (Book 3, Ch21 L109), and a second line ignores "the enhanced aura suppression and aura suppression resistance derived from rank disparity" without touching "the inherently superior strength of higher-rank auras" (Book 3, Ch21 L110). Sophie's Boundary Breaker removes the damage reduction from disparity (Book 3, Ch69 L808). Humphrey's Hero's Drive lets him ignore resistances of disparity (Book 3, Ch67 L95). Clive notes that Humphrey and Jason "can overlook rank disparity in certain regards" (Book 3, Ch37 L9).

**Transcendent damage.** It ignores "all forms of physical and magical defence, damage reduction and resistances", so an iron-rank ability that deals it can annihilate a bronze monster (Book 1, Ch99 L169). See `Afflictions_Boons_and_Damage.md`.

**Racial difference.** Cores take more for people who are not human, and humans' abilities advance faster (Book 1, Ch15 L58; Book 1, Ch31 L96).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Rufus and Farrah.** Both bronze "not that far off silver" when Jason is first an essence user (Book 1, Ch22 L33).
- **Greenstone's Society.** Elspeth Arella is a silver-rank bureaucrat (Book 1, Ch101 L16).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **A higher rank always wins.** Disparity-ignoring abilities and transcendent damage exist (see section 3), and Jason's team repeatedly beats higher-ranked enemies.
- **Rank is earned only by killing.** Cores let rich people reach bronze "without ever facing a monster" (Book 1, Ch31 L100).
- **Immortality is certain.** Rufus: "It's a rumour, but a persistent one" (Book 1, Ch22 L60).

## Rituals, Arrays and Formations

### 1. Summary

Ritual magic is the practice of drawing a diagram, a circle with lines and glyphs, and powering it with external magic to do something an essence ability cannot. It is used for absorbing essences, awakening stones, summoning familiars, spreading wards and tracking, and, in permanent form, as formations and arrays. Ritual magic is learned, not given by an essence, and a competent ritualist is a valuable member of any team. Anyone can learn external magic; only essence users can combine it with essence powers.

### 2. How it works

**What it is.** Farrah's summary: "Ritual magic relies on external sources of magic. That's ambient magic, plus more concentrated sources, like quintessence or spirit coins" (Book 1, Ch23 L27). "External magic doesn't require you to have an essence. There are people who make careers out of learning a specialised slice of external magic" (Book 1, Ch23 L30), like plumbers who know "just enough to do a specific job" (Book 1, Ch23 L31-32). The fundamental theory is shared across rituals, magical craftsmanship and alchemy: "Same basis, different applications" (Book 1, Ch23 L32).

**The circle and the ambient magic.** A ritual diagram must be adjusted to the local magic: "Every time you draw a magic diagram you need to adjust for the ambient magic conditions. A weak source of congealed magic like the core of a lesser monster is a perfect way to check your work" (Book 1, Ch23 L15). A ritual room is designed with a permanent circle and a regulated environment (Book 1, Ch23 L16-17). A flat stone floor helps: Emir's ritual room has "a floor made from a single slab of black stone, cut perfectly level and smooth" (Book 2, Ch6 L41). A cloud floor is poor for circles (Book 3, Ch25 L38). Materials vary with the ritual: a salt circle will do for ordinary summoning, while exotic summons call for obsidian dust and iron filings (Book 1, Ch93 L41). A permanent brass circle is made by carving channels into stone and pouring molten metal (Book 3, Ch7 L96).

**Clive's gift.** Clive's racial gift balances the ambient magic: "It balances out the ambient magic so you don't need to adjust your ritual circle" (Book 1, Ch85 L60), and his essence ability, Enact Ritual, draws diagrams in lines of golden light (Book 1, Ch103 L44; Book 2, Ch6 L43). "His ability to draw them with his power, along with balancing out the ambient magic, saved immense amounts of time when going through many rituals in sequence" (Book 2, Ch66 L90).

**Essence rituals.** "Essence rituals are very gentle. It's only if you shove the essence inside yourself without one that the experience is a harsh one" (Book 2, Ch7 L130). In a ritual the essences turn to glowing dust that swirls around the user; when the last essence is absorbed the dust stops glowing and falls (Book 2, Ch7 L117). The standard sequence for Sophie's stones in Emir's palace takes hours, with Clive resetting the circle between stones (Book 2, Ch66 L90; Book 2, Ch67 L3, L162).

**Casting rituals as powers.** A ritual can itself be an essence ability. Farrah notes that "rituals that are also essence powers tend to be quick and dirty", like Gary's and her short-lived summons (Book 1, Ch23 L38-39). A familiar summoning is an "elaborate" ritual needed only per rank (Book 1, Ch23 L42). See `Familiars_Summons_and_Conjurations.md`.

**Large rituals.** The blood cult's star seed ritual uses a magic circle "to gather and concentrate the ambient magic to fuel the star seed", supplemented periodically with spirit coins (Book 3, Ch19 L37). Carlos's soul-projection ritual, used to check for star seeds, is "a gold rank ritual", devised for the occasion and rare in Greenstone (Book 3, Ch23 L19). A circle for it was "easily the most sophisticated circle Jason had ever seen", with sections coloured red, green and blue as well as gold (Book 3, Ch23 L32). Clive's combat ritual circles glow on the floor under his feet during a fight (Book 3, Ch38 L30).

**Divine rituals.** A god's rituals work differently: "divine rituals are not so vulnerable to vagaries of ambient magic as the mundane varieties" (Book 2, Ch19 L77). They "still draw on the power of ambient magic but are infused with the glorious might of the divine. My god's will moves the magic and not the other way around" (Book 2, Ch19 L79).

**Mirage chambers.** A mirage chamber is a training room with a control block of runes at one end, carved into a stone block under a window (Book 1, Ch48 L3). It runs at a chosen rank: in Greenstone Rufus asks to borrow one but the family can only run it at bronze rank for limited periods because local magical density is low; Danielle offers hers at iron rank instead (Book 1, Ch37 L41-55).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Landscaped magic.** Clive says of a large estate that whoever designed it showed "the foresight to wait for plants to grow over decades, planning out the shifts in magic as plants and trees grew" (Book 2, Ch18 L11).
- **Dice and triggers.** Humphrey's summoning die activates its power by being rolled in a "simple circle of powdered chalk" (Book 3, Ch10 L5).
- **Ritual knowledge from skill books.** Jason's ritual skill book gave him "the bare minimum to be considered a proper ritual magician" (Book 1, Ch23 L36). See `Skill_Books_and_Learning.md`.
- **Who is allowed to draw.** A person who does not know external magic cannot awaken a familiar summoning power (Book 1, Ch23 L37).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Absorption of essences and stones:** Book 1, Ch21 (Jason, no ritual needed), Book 2, Ch7 (Sophie).
- **Familiar summonings:** the first sanguine horror in Book 1, Ch23; Shade's bronze resummoning materials in Book 2, Ch63.
- **A ritual room with permanent circle:** Landemere Vane's room, found under his burned house and described as "an ordinary, if impressive, ritual room" which "predates the settlement of Greenstone" (Book 3, Ch11 L90).
- **Portal rituals:** In Landemere's old ritual room, the Builder cult's agents build an astral bridge from a brass circle and a crude brick archway (Book 3, Ch7 L96-97).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Magic circles are universal.** Each needs adjusting to local magic (Book 1, Ch23 L15).
- **Divine rituals are just bigger.** They are less vulnerable to the vagaries of ambient magic than mundane rituals (Book 2, Ch19 L77).
- **Rituals need an essence.** External magic "doesn't require you to have an essence" (Book 1, Ch23 L30).

### 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** Clive's racial gift and Enact Ritual speed up circle-drawing in the Book 2 sessions (Book 2, Ch66 L90).

## Skill Books and Learning

### 1. Summary

A skill book is a consumable magical book that implants knowledge directly into the reader. It can teach a language, a martial art, the basics of ritual magic or a branch of astral magic. A skill book gives technique and information, not understanding or practice, and the people who use them well prepare beforehand and consolidate afterwards. They are the fast, flawed route to ability in a skill, in the same way that cores are the fast, flawed route to rank.

### 2. How it works

**Reading one.** The book describes itself and its requirements on a System panel. Ritual Magic I "can impart the fundamentals of performing magic rituals", and its only requirement is the "ability to use skill books" (Book 1, Ch7 L50-51). Higher books list more: Astral Magic II needs bronze rank, the ability to use skill books, and basic and intermediate ritual magic theory and basic astral magic theory (Book 1, Ch7 L41-43). The player-style response is: "Jason was familiar with skill books from video games that instantly gave out spells or special abilities" (Book 1, Ch7 L46).

**What happens.** The book floats over the reader, opens, and its text rises from the page as glowing runes that swarm around the reader and sink into them (Book 1, Ch50 L65-73). The process is painful: "Using a skill book is strenuous. The more it's trying to teach you, the greater the strain" (Book 1, Ch50 L71). A big book takes long and hurts more: the martial-art skill book that Jason uses was bigger than any Farrah had seen (Book 1, Ch50 L67).

**Staying conscious.** "People often pass out while using them, but the information isn't passed on as well once they're unconscious. It takes them longer to consolidate what they've learned afterwards" (Book 1, Ch50 L72). Rufus urges Jason to "try and last out the whole thing" (Book 1, Ch50 L69).

**How it feels.** The knowledge arrives as memory of something never learned: "Remembering something he never learned in the first place was an odd sensation, like déjà vu" (Book 1, Ch15 L113). A reader recognises a thing as if he had always known it (Book 1, Ch10 L38).

**What it gives and what it does not.** Rufus: "The thing you need to understand about the skill book is that it isn't going to teach you how to fight... It will teach you technique, not how to use it. It's a shortcut that saves you years of repetitive exercise, but that isn't fighting... Even the best hammer doesn't push the nails in itself" (Book 1, Ch50 L20-22). Farrah: "Knowing isn't the same as understanding. You've used a ritual magic skill book, but knowing practical applications isn't the same as grasping the theory" (Book 1, Ch39 L112). Rufus: "Learning to fight through skill books is ultimately faster than training from nothing because you save yourself years of repetition to ingrain the skills. There is a danger, however, of not fully comprehending the techniques. By preparing well before using a skill book, then consolidating well after, you avoid developing flaws in your skillset" (Book 1, Ch39 L115). The Remore family academy has to beat the habit of confusing technique with fighting out of "skill book users" (Book 1, Ch39 L117).

**Preparation.** Before a martial skill book Jason is made to learn to stand, move and sit properly: "When it stops feeling awkward, you will be ready to use a skill book" (Book 1, Ch40 L26).

**Contents are practical.** "People make skill books with knowledge practical for adventurers, not for farmers. It's one of many reasons that skill books aren't proper magic instruction. They only give the flimsiest theoretical grounding" (Book 1, Ch85 L8). Jason's ritual book gives him the minimum "to be considered a proper ritual magician", enough to awaken a familiar summoning power and to perform an essence ritual (Book 1, Ch23 L36; Book 1, Ch29 L87).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **The ability to use them.** Jason's racial ability Mysterious Stranger lists "Essence, awakening stone and skill-book absorption" as one of its functions, alongside language adaptation and immunity to identification and tracking (Book 1, Ch7 L55-58). Jason reads the description as the power to use skill books (Book 1, Ch7 L60). For others, the requirement of being able to use skill books is met by ordinary means, since the Remore academy trains with them (Book 1, Ch39 L109, L117).
- **Languages.** Gary notes that Jason sounds as if he "learned the language from a skill book" (Book 1, Ch15 L41).
- **Martial art.** Way of the Reaper: Five Forms I is a skill book detailing "the foundational techniques for all five forms" (Book 1, Ch50 L41), later found in separate volumes in a hidden cache (Book 1, Ch88 L80-85). Jason's experience is that the forms "aren't just five mini martial arts bundled into a skill book anthology" (Book 1, Ch75 L72). The skill book gave him proficiency with an array of weaponry, although Rufus concentrated training on only a few (Book 1, Ch56 L18).
- **Opponents' view.** Sophie, who has fought skill-book users, says fighting Jason "didn't feel like that" (Book 2, Ch1 L47).
- **Knowledge without ground.** A reader can find a text "above his head" when the field is more specific than the skill book (Book 1, Ch12 L15).
- **Cooking and crafts.** Jason wants "a cooking magic skill book" to extricate syrup from food (Book 1, Ch38 L11).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Ritual Magic I** taken from Landemere Vane and read in Book 1 (Book 1, Ch8 L88-93; Book 1, Ch69 L84).
- **Astral magic book**, also from Landemere (Book 1, Ch69 L84).
- **Way of the Reaper: Five Forms I**, Rufus's gift (Book 1, Ch56 L47; Book 1, Ch50 L41).
- **Magic Society records** are not skill books but are sold as tablets of public knowledge on powers (Book 1, Ch92 L95).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Use it and you're good.** Jason's own assumption: "Isn't the whole point of skill books to just use them and you're good?" (Book 1, Ch39 L110). Rufus and Farrah correct it.
- **A skill book is proper instruction.** Clive: it is not (Book 1, Ch85 L8).
- **A skill book makes you a fighter.** It teaches technique, not the fighting (Book 1, Ch50 L22).

### 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** Jason's skill-book martial art becomes the basis of his fighting once he learns to combine the forms rather than use them separately (Book 1, Ch75 L72).

## Souls, Death and Healing

### 1. Summary

A soul is the part of a person that essences and ranks change. It shapes the body, projects the aura, leaves marks when wounded and, on death, lingers briefly before drifting into the astral. Healing magic can reverse a death only in that grace period and only with powerful magic. Everyday healing is done by the Healer's church, by alchemists and by healing abilities, and it is in short supply compared to demand.

### 2. How it works

**Death and the grace period.** Clive: "When the soul dies, it only lingers with the body for a small time. Usually minutes, but an annihilated body might have the soul depart in seconds, while freezing to death might have it linger for an hour or even longer. It's why if a gold rank healer can repair the body in that grace period, the death can be turned back" (Book 2, Ch25 L53). After that, "an untethered soul is an astral object and drifts into the astral" (Book 2, Ch25 L55).

**Outworlders.** "An outworlder is someone whose soul has re-entered a physical reality, reflexively manifesting a body for itself... like a monster" (Book 2, Ch25 L57-58; Book 2, Ch25 L59). The Builder and others do not just resurrect their own minions because the great astral beings "are careful about encroaching upon one another's interests" (Book 2, Ch25 L51).

**Crests.** A soul crest "is a magical tattoo printed not on the body, but on the soul. That imprint would appear on the body in turn, in a form that resisted design. The form of the crest was a visible reflection of the bearer's true nature" (Book 2, Ch70 L36). The unique imprint on the aura stays the same even when the aura changes, making it "a guaranteed proof of identity" once recorded (Book 2, Ch70 L37-38). The Immortal Crest is an iron-rank consumable applied by a mystical tattooist, usable only on an iron-rank essence user (Book 2, Ch70 L33-35). The tattoo is purged as the wearer ranks up, so a new one is needed (Book 2, Ch70 L42). Humphrey has one in vivid blue (Book 2, Ch70 L44).

**Healing in practice.** "Most healing was done through the church of the Healer. From what he'd been told, their god supplied the essences and awakening stones that gave them their healing abilities. They could be sought out for a fee, but also sent people around the delta to heal people at more reasonable prices. It sounded good, but Jason had seen firsthand that there was always more demand for such services than supply" (Book 1, Ch35 L23). Both ritual magic and alchemy have ways to heal, but "the cost and expertise required placed both out of the reach for most people" (Book 1, Ch35 L22). The Healer personally tells a patient that an alchemist could not regrow a foot, and offers to station a healer at Jory's clinic (Book 1, Ch95 L69, L126).

**Healer powers.** Healing abilities exist for cleansing and recovery; see `Afflictions_Boons_and_Damage.md`. A healer who stops a person dying within the grace period is a "gold rank healer" in Clive's account (Book 2, Ch25 L53).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Healing the immune.** A person with an immunity may need special handling; see the afflictions that negate immunity in `Afflictions_Boons_and_Damage.md`.

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Farrah's death** is the example Jason's team recalls when Clive explains the grace period (Book 2, Ch25 L54).
- **Humphrey's crest**, with its blue sigil (Book 2, Ch70 L44).
- **Jory's clinic**, an alchemy lab and healing station (Book 1, Ch35 L63).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **A good healer can undo any death.** Only within the grace period, and only against bodies that can be repaired (Book 2, Ch25 L53).
- **A crest can be chosen.** It cannot be (Book 2, Ch70 L39).

## Unusual and Unique Magic

### 1. Summary

Most magic in the setting follows the essence-and-rank structure described elsewhere in this folder. A smaller body of magic does not: the gifts a person is born with or earns as a race, the outworlder abilities that give Jason a game-like interface, titles drawn from soul damage, time magic, and the powers of great astral beings and gods. This file collects them so a storyteller knows what exists and who has what. It does not attempt to explain the cosmology; see `02_Cosmology_and_Powers/`.

### 2. How it works

**The outworlder gifts.** Jason's first status screen lists six racial abilities labelled Outworlder: Interface, Quest System, Inventory, Map, Astral Affinity and Mysterious Stranger (Book 1, Ch1 L37-43).
- **Interface:** the screens and System messages; "interpret reality through a recognisable medium" (Book 1, Ch87 L160).
- **Quest System:** quests with rewards, including a "racial gift transfiguration" (Book 2, Ch61 L130).
- **Inventory:** a grid of forty slots with a currency counter; items are plucked from and pushed into the screen (Book 1, Ch1 L82-89).
- **Map:** a self-updating map (Book 2, Ch61 L141).
- **Astral Affinity:** "Increased resistance to dimension effects and astral forces. Dimension abilities have increased effect and transcendent damage is increased" (Book 1, Ch1 L93).
- **Mysterious Stranger:** "Language adaptation. Essence, awakening stone and skill-book absorption. Immunity to identification and tracking effects" (Book 1, Ch1 L96-99).

**Evolutions of racial gifts.** A racial gift can transfigure when the owner completes a task or takes a blessing. In Book 2 Jason's Interface becomes Party Interface, with a contacts list and party features that members keep while they remain in the party (Book 1, Ch87 L157-162). Map becomes Tactical Map, which tracks nearby allies and enemies (Book 2, Ch61 L138-142). His teammates' gifts evolve in the same reward: Clive's Human Ambition to Thirst For Knowledge, Neil's Life Affinity to Life Guard, Sophie's Mana Integrity to Mana Wellspring, Humphrey's Special Attack Affinity to Attack of the Mirage Dragon (Book 2, Ch61 L144-147). Quest System later becomes Defiant, which loses the quest effects and gains the ability to "ignore the enhanced resistances derived from rank disparity" and rank-disparity aura suppression (Book 3, Ch21 L105-111). Mysterious Stranger evolves into Dark Rider in Book 2 (Book 2, Ch63 L83-85).

**Titles.** Titles are given by the System for soul damage that changes the aura (see `Auras.md`).

### 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Immunity to identification.** Jason's identification immunity means the Adventure Society badge does not track him (Book 1, Ch62 L55-59).

### 4. Examples from the books

- **Humphrey's Hero's Drive.** The text credits Humphrey's Hero's Drive with letting him ignore the resistances of rank disparity (Book 3, Ch67 L95).
- **Sophie's Boundary Breaker.** Eliminates the damage reduction from rank disparity (Book 3, Ch69 L808).
- **Jason's interface** appears to other people in his party (Book 1, Ch87 L162).

### 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **Racial gifts are rare.** Every race has them, although most never evolve.

### 6. Changes over time

- **Level 1:** The Reaper trials reward the team with racial transfigurations (Book 2, Ch61 L130-147).

## Daily Life: Calendar, Food, Clothing and Transport

### 1. Summary

The practical texture of life in Greenstone and the delta: the calendar, what people wear and eat, how they travel and what runs on spirit coins.

### 2. Facts

- **Food**: preparation is often familiar, with bread, soup, sandwiches and cake, but the ingredients are mostly different because farms raise different animals and trees bear different fruit (Book 1, Ch45 L8). Jason lists local sauce ingredients with a village cook (Book 1, Ch25 L13).
- **Housing**: Leust, one of the delta's largest towns, has paved roads and stone buildings "instead of mud-brick" (Book 1, Ch93 L5); in the Island, green stone appears everywhere (Book 1, Ch45 L5).
- **Transport**: heidels are "the size and shape of a horse, but with scales instead of hair and two heads, each with a horn" (Book 1, Ch17 L106); the Island's districts are linked by a subterranean, submarine transit line called the loop (Book 1, Ch43 L5), with terminals laid out like train stations (Book 1, Ch37 L83).
- **Devices**: lamps, showers and most household magic run on lesser spirit coins (Book 1, Ch80 L40).

### 3. Using this in a story

Heat, coin-run conveniences and a loop line make Greenstone feel modern; outside it, heidels, mud-brick and the sun set the pace.

## Economy and Money

### 1. Summary

How money works on Pallimustus: spirit coins in six denominations that also power devices and sustain essence users, farms that make them, and who controls them.

### 2. Facts

- **Denominations**: "The basic coin of the realm was the lesser spirit coin. Iron spirit coins were worth a hundred lesser coins, used by bulk traders, adventurers and other members of the wealthy elite. After that, it was ten iron to the bronze, ten bronze to the silver and ten silver to the gold"; three gold coins are "worth three hundred thousand units of the basic currency" (Book 1, Ch61 L65).
- **Why the lesser coin is the basic unit**: higher-rank coins are "not very useful for everyday life"; almost all home and business magic devices, such as lamps and showers, run on lesser coins and higher ranks would burn them out, while larger infrastructure such as the loop line uses more powerful coins (Book 1, Ch80 L40-41).
- **Sustenance**: ranked people can live on spirit coins; Rufus says a bronze coin will sustain bronze-rank adventurers without stressing their bodies, so it is about not going over your limit (Book 1, Ch15 L92).
- **Farms**: spirit coins are made on farms using special moulds that cause magic to crystallise, in delicate, very stable ambient magic (Book 1, Ch80 L21).
- **Why Greenstone**: most areas have too high a magical density to make lesser coins; the smallest denomination they can manage is usually iron or even bronze, and Greenstone was founded because the low-magic desert is a rare place for low-end farms (Book 1, Ch80 L37, L42). A farm that makes a thousand lesser coins would make only ten iron coins or one bronze coin (Book 1, Ch80 L39).
- **Volume**: spirit coins are Greenstone's largest export, and the desert farms produce almost three percent of the lesser coins used worldwide; most farms are run by the Geller or Mercer families under Magic Society regulation (Book 1, Ch80 L27-28).
- **Hazards**: volatile weather, a monster spawning nearby or an adventurer using abilities can ruin a batch, so adventurers wait outside the walls (Book 1, Ch80 L43); there is no defence against a monster spawning too close to a farm, and the loss is absorbed (Book 1, Ch80 L44-45).
- **Control**: "The Magic Society manages all of that"; coin production is the main source of its political power and the reason it must stay politically neutral (Book 1, Ch80 L23). An unregistered farm must be found and shut down (Book 1, Ch42 L64).
- **Escorts**: adventurers escort shipments of coins from a farm back to the city (Book 1, Ch77 L51).
- **Prices**: armour for an iron-rank adventurer is priced in bronze coins, which is unusual (Book 1, Ch61 L114).
- **Wealth and rank**: Jason offers three gold coins as a starting payment (Book 1, Ch61 L63-65), and later funds Jory's alchemy facility (Book 3, Ch16 L81; the amount is not stated).
- **Permits and trade**: trade and work permits get a person into the Island's trade lanes; aristocrats, adventurers and residents use the wide lane (Book 1, Ch36 L71).
- **Indenture as income**: criminals' indentures are sold or auctioned to recoup the cost of their crimes (Book 1, Ch92 L32; see Law_and_Indenture.md).

### 3. Using this in a story

Money in a scene is rank-shaped: lesser coins buy lamps and showers, iron coins buy equipment for the elite, and a gold coin can change a community's fortunes.

## The Adventure Society

### 1. Summary

The Adventure Society is one of the three great organisations of Pallimustus. Its main purpose is monster extermination: it posts notices, issues contracts, assesses and registers adventurers, and keeps records of their kills. Its insignia is a **sword and rod crossed over a shield**, stitched on its staff's clothing and embossed on its badges (Book 1, Ch37 L93; Ch62 L61).

### 2. Joining

- **Application**: the applicant fills in personal details and undergoes a magical scan of their body and essences with an Adventure Society official, a Magic Society clerk (Russell, with a clipboard) and an acolyte of Knowledge, Gabrielle Pellin, who holds up a crystal orb as an aura reader; the essences she reads are written down (Book 1, Ch38 L118-149). Jason had already checked his combination against the **restricted list** (Book 1, Ch38 L135). Rufus tells Jason (Book 1, Ch20 L44) that it is impossible to become a member with a restricted essence; Death is restricted by itself and other restrictions are mostly combinations, because the confluence is usually the dangerous essence.
- **Field assessment**: an instructor takes a group of candidates out for a week, during which they must complete postings from the adventure boards of delta towns and villages (Book 1, Ch54 L35). Field testing takes place at the start of every month and candidates sign up at the administration desk (Book 1, Ch38 L158). Vincent Trenslow and Guy Spalding both act as instructors ("Instructor Trenslow" and "Instructor Spalding"; Book 1, Ch54 L36); Spalding is also Albert Bertinelli's supervisor. Adventurers also patrol outlying villages on a monthly rota, an unpopular punishment duty that means a month away from the city (Book 1, Ch25 L17; Ch64 L3).

### 3. Badges and stars

- **Badge**: a circular iron medallion embossed with the sword-and-rod-over-shield emblem, carried in a leather wallet (Book 1, Ch62 L61). It serves as identification, lets the holder copy a notice by touching the medallion to it and then to a blank sheet (Book 1, Ch32 L87), and is paired with a tracking stone: "It tells us if you're alive or dead, and lets us find you if you go missing or die" (Book 1, Ch62 L47). The Magic Society makes badges, taking an aura imprint in a magic circle (Book 1, Ch62 L46); Jason's tracking stone cannot track him because of his Mysterious Stranger gift (Book 1, Ch62 L47-54). Rufus's is bronze for his rank (Book 1, Ch32 L87).
- **Stars** show the Society's confidence in judgement, not power (Vincent, Book 1, Ch62 L81-85): two stars mean some unsupervised responsibility and different kinds of contract; three stars are "much the same as two, but more so". The notes add that stars drop when rank rises (Book 1, Ch62 L91); Lucian tells Pochard to put up the contract on an off-the-books spirit coin farm as a three-star contract so that "someone who'll actually do the work" takes it (Book 1, Ch42 L67), and Pochard answers that "if it involves the spirit coin farms, the Adventure Society will make it three-star" (Book 1, Ch42 L68); and Jason is later given a gold badge with a diamond symbol where one, two or three stars would normally be.
- **Stones for kills**: no awakening stones are awarded for killing monsters above the killer's rank (Vincent, Book 1, Ch91 L34).

### 4. Work

- **Notices** are posted on boards or issued directly; a notice carries "the name of the monster, or a description", the number of monsters and the approximate location (Book 1, Ch57 L33).
- **Contracts** are jobs: escort, extermination, investigation, delivery.

### 5. Structure and branches

- **Branch**: a director and deputy; in Greenstone the director is Elspeth Arella, the deputy Genevieve Picot (Book 1, Ch65 L60; Ch98 L63), and officials include Vincent Trenslow and Albert Bertinelli.

### 6. Society and policy

- **Restricted essences**: Rufus says Death is restricted by itself (Book 1, Ch20 L44), and the Magic Society's living document shows one restricted combination, the Succubus essence, which takes two insanely rare essences (Book 1, Ch20 L104-110). A corrupt essence is on the list: a Magic-Society-bound adventurer hands one looted from an elemental to the Adventure Society "since it was on the restricted list" (Book 3, Ch7 L55); the Society offers good trades for restricted essences (Book 2, Ch65 L96). The wiki's fuller list and its Sacrifice-confluence pit fighter were not found in the text.

### 7. Hooks

- A newcomer's field assessment goes wrong.
- A continental inquiry arrives at a corrupt branch.
- A contribution-board dispute between a guild and independents.
- A restricted-essence user hides among adventurers.

### 8. Open questions

- (Resolved) The Society and the Magic Society do take part jointly in the application scan (Book 1, Ch38 L118-134).

## Greenstone

### 1. Summary

Greenstone is a colony city at the mouth of a river delta in the low-magic far south of the Africa-like continent. It is the first city Jason knows on Pallimustus and the setting of most of Books 1-3. It was founded because its conditions suit low-end spirit coin farms: stable low magic, in a place that is also livable and close to the desert (Book 1, Ch80 L42). It is a place of green marble, walled harbours, crime lords with gentlemanly manners, and a Society full of ambitious, mid-ranked people who use monster cores rather than fight for their ranks.

### 2. Location and layout

- **Setting**: a vast river delta inside a western coastal region that is mostly lifeless desert; the delta is fertile ground between dune seas. Locals and adventurers wear loose, breathable clothes because of the heat.
- **The Old City**: "the original city of Greenstone, situated on the original harbour"; the Island was meant to be a massive breakwater (Book 1, Ch35 L15). It is built mostly from the same red and yellow stone as the desert, with a massive port along its whole shoreline, and "Old City is where the money is made, but the Island is where the money goes" (Book 1, Ch35 L19, L59; Book 1, Ch36 L98). The city authorities pay little attention to it so long as the elite's interests are secure, which makes its three biggest crime lords its de facto rulers (Book 1, Ch42 L22). Districts have names from old families, such as Cavendish, whose tavern the Townhouse is the largest building there (Book 1, Ch71 L3, L38).
- **The Island**: the affluent district built entirely of green marble (Book 1, Ch36 L68). Travel to it "was restricted without a valid reason for entry": trade and work permits admit a person to the trade lanes, and aristocrats, adventurers and residents come and go freely; a day's entry costs an iron-rank spirit coin (Book 1, Ch36 L71-72). Staying past sunset needs lodgings, which earn a temporary residence permit and a tracking stone (Book 1, Ch36 L89, L95; Book 1, Ch39 L34). Vehicles on the Island are magic-driven, not animal-drawn, and prices there are twice or three times Old City's (Book 1, Ch39 L32-33). The guild district in the north-west holds the Adventure Society campus (Book 1, Ch38 L3).
- **The Divine Square**: a huge crowded square on the Island, paved even in high-grade green stone, ringed by streets of temples that show "gods had hierarchies of their own"; booths sell religious paraphernalia, and even the flagstones are high-grade green stone (Book 1, Ch45 L4-5). The Temple of the Healer is one of the central temples on it (Book 1, Ch94 L3) and all the major temples front it (Book 2, Ch10 L92).
- **The Fortress**: a huge stone building in the grimy heart of Old City's warehouse district, "older even than the city walls", once the shelter for residents during a monster surge; now neutral ground where the Big Three divide responsibility and profit, and the best place in Old City to glimpse the Island elites (Book 1, Ch42 L21-23). Fights are held there in a steel cage, as Sophie's Nightingale bouts (Book 1, Ch50 L85-91).
- **Water and tunnels**: delta water runs through an elaborate tunnel network under the Old City and drains into an artificial strait between the Old City and the Island; Adventure Society contracts clear monsters such as stone-chewer rats from it. Transit uses loop terminals (Book 1, Ch45 L4); Jason's "magic trams or something?" (Book 1, Ch37 L81) is only his guess.
- **Surroundings**: green-marble quarry villages (Quarry Village Four, a lake village fed by a waterfall from an astral-space aperture), desert, mangrove and marsh. Boko, a desert academic city, lies to the north.

### 3. Society and rule

- **Colonial status**: the city began as a colony from the wider kingdom. The Adventure Society is a major force, but the isolation makes the local branch dependent on local powers; Rufus's remark to the village mayor is that "compromises must be made."
- **The Adventure Society branch** is directed by Elspeth Arella. Vincent Trenslow runs assessments and assigns contracts.
- **The Magic Society branch** is directed by Lucian Lamprey, a silver-ranker who watches Fortress fights from a viewing box (Book 1, Ch50 L103; Book 1, Ch52 L46). His deputy is Pochard Finn, whose son Cassowary serves as Lucian's dogsbody (Book 1, Ch49 L5; Book 1, Ch50 L104).
- **The great families**: the Gellers (an estate, with a mirage chamber), the Mercers (Thalia, Cassandra, Thadwick; the Mercer estate has a gate and guards), and the Remore family's academy annex.
- **Faiths**: temples in the Divine Square, including the Healer's. Purity's Anisa Lasalle begins the books in Rufus's party.

### 4. Magic level

Greenstone is a low-magic region where "iron-rank monsters are the norm, with a good smattering of bronze-rank" (Book 1, Ch19 L40; Book 1, Ch30 L43). This shapes the city: core use is nearly universal, because fighting for advancement is slow when monsters are few and weak; a bronze-rank mirage chamber can run only for short periods; there are only "forty or so silver rankers in the whole city", most semi-retired (Book 1, Ch61 L32), and the narration notes that Greenstone's other silver-rankers show more of their age than Emir, Danielle and Thalia (Book 1, Ch108 L35). Resting spirit coins and potions matter more than in higher-magic cities. The city exports green marble and lesser spirit coins.

### 5. History

- Founded as a colony and spirit coin farm. Almost nothing is known about the region's history before the original Greenstone Colony, as Clive observes (Book 1, Ch86 L25); the Gellers' ancestors came as the region was first being settled (Book 1, Ch37 L21).
- Book 1: Jason arrives, joins the Adventure Society, helps destroy a Blood Cult branch and fights alongside Rufus and Farrah.
- Books 2-3: Sophie's indenture, Emir's contest for the Scythe of the Reaper, Mercer family intrigue, the oasis tyrant, the Builder cult.
- Later: Greenstone remains a recurring home and reference point.

### 6. Notable people

Elspeth Arella (Adventure Society director), Vincent Trenslow (official), Lucian Lamprey (Magic Society director), Clarissa Ventress, Cole Silva, Adris Dorgan, Dink, Emir Bahadir and Constance, the Gellers (Danielle, Humphrey), the Mercers (Thalia, Cassandra, Thadwick; the Mercer estate has a gate and guards), Jory Tillman (alchemist), Clive Standish, Beth Cavendish (water healer), Hiram (aperture guard at Quarry Village Four), Arash (runic juice vendor), Jamar (bartender), the Berts (Bertinelli octuplets, clerks).

### 7. Customs and daily life

Aristocrats and the wealthy live on the Island; ordinary people in the Old City. Adventurers cluster in the Society campus and its taverns. Temples hold public services in the Divine Square. Fights at the Fortress are a major entertainment; recording crystals broadcast and preserve them. Heat makes loose dress universal. Local food and drink include runic juice from Arash's stall.

A water-and-islands astral space reached from the desert by a submerged aperture. A large Greenstone expedition goes in at the climax of Book 1 to find what is disrupting it. They are ambushed by a construct army.

The incident ties the Builder cult to the region and sets up Book 2.

## Awakening Stones

### 1. Summary

Single-use stones that awaken an essence ability, found, traded and rewarded by rank and rarity.

### 2. Facts

- **What they do**: "An awakening stone that unlocks the power of consumption... Effect: Awakens an essence ability", requiring an unawakened essence ability (Book 1, Ch11 L17-20). The stone melts into the user's hand "with little fanfare" (Book 1, Ch22 L70).
- **Why they matter**: Rufus tells Jason he cannot "properly progress towards bronze rank until you awaken every essence ability you have. That's all twenty" (Book 1, Ch22 L68).
- **Naming and rarity**: stones are "unranked" and carry a rarity, such as the Awakening Stone of the Feast (common, Book 1, Ch11 L17), of Adventure (rare, Book 1, Ch22 L99), of the Stars (epic, Book 1, Ch4 L44) and of the Reaper (legendary, Book 2, Ch57 L31).
- **Affinity**: the Reaper stone "sharing affinity with the Reaper" is used in Jason's awakening ritual with the Reaper token (Book 2, Ch57 L31-33; Book 2, Ch63 L65), and it gives Humphrey, a human, a special attack (Book 2, Ch64 L72).
- **Divine awakening stones**: a god-made stone is "transcendent rank, epic" and bestows a specific aura power (Book 2, Ch13 L60).
- **Sources**: looting an awakening stone or essence can pay better than the contract to kill the monster (Book 1, Ch57 L21); the Adventure Society gives stones for exceptional service, "although rarely", and some open contracts offer them as rewards (Book 1, Ch64 L81); otherwise stones are bought through brokers, almost always at auction, at exorbitant prices (Book 1, Ch64 L82). Brokers in a small city hold the valuable stuff, like essences and stones, for big auctions (Book 1, Ch61 L36).
- **Family supply**: the Gellers traditionally supply their scions with enough stones to get started, while the rest must be earned (Book 1, Ch64 L80).
- **Restricted-essence trades**: Adventure Society representatives offer good trades for restricted essences, as when Sophie trades a death essence for two epic stones (Book 2, Ch65 L96).

### 3. Using this in a story

Awakening stones are the currency of early progression. Scarcity and who can afford a broker's auction drive many scenes in the delta.

Emir's cloud ship and palace, and Jason's own cloud houses, yachts and palaces made from his Cloud Flask.

### 2. Facts

- **Emir's cloud ship**: it arrives at the Greenstone dock and a walkway of cloud connects to the shore as a hole opens in its side (Book 1, Ch101 L64). Moored, it morphs over about ten minutes into a palace of clouds floating on the water (Book 1, Ch101 L103).
- **Emir's cloud palace**: floating just offshore on the north side of the Island, "fully exposed to the waves and currents, yet remained as immovable as solid ground", with wings and towers in blue, purple, orange and gold (Book 1, Ch108 L3). Walls, floors and ceilings glow with absorbed sunlight the palace stores and distributes, and the floors have a bouncy-castle spring (Book 2, Ch1 L3).
- **Team base**: Jason's team move into Emir's palace after the contest (Book 2, Ch8 L168).

### 3. Using this in a story

A cloud palace is both home and statement; Emir's flair is the model for how much a palace can show off.

## Consumables, Trial Rewards and Crafting Materials

### 1. Summary

Single-use and crafting items: the City of Fallen Echoes rewards, clockwork cores, reality cores and star seeds.

### 2. Facts

- **Lesser Miracle Potion**: "iron rank, legendary", "Salvation in a bottle", it fully restores health, mana and stamina but only works on normal and iron-rank people, and its lingering magic delays other recovery (Book 2, Ch44 L127-129).
- **Medallion of Escape**: "silver rank, uncommon", evacuates the holder from the astral space when their aura is projected into it, and only works inside the City of Fallen Echoes (Book 2, Ch44 L120-123).
- **Immortal Crest**: "iron rank, rare", lets a mystical tattooist draw out a soul crest and only works on an iron-rank essence user (Book 2, Ch58 L102-104).
- **Summoner's Die: Form**: "iron rank [growth], legendary", it randomly alters the form of an iron-rank summon (Book 2, Ch58 L53-56).
- **Clockwork Core**: "iron rank, rare", the core of an artificial monster that simplifies the materials and processes of a construct creature; clockwork cores are made by clockwork kings (Book 2, Ch25 L24-26, L35).
- **Star seeds**: great astral beings, not gods, "can send their followers star seeds"; once implanted and germinated the body transforms, "minor or major" (Book 2, Ch21 L26-28).

### 3. Using this in a story

These rewards are meant to be spent. A potion or medallion is a story decision, not an inventory slot.

## Desert Transport: Skimmers and Sand Barges

### 1. Summary

The vehicles that cross the desert around Greenstone: the sand skimmers and sand barges Emir Bahadir's people run, the carriages that move expeditions to relay stations, and the monstrous Ustei war barge. Emir's cloud ship is covered in Cloud_Constructs_and_Palaces.md.

### 2. Facts

- **Sand skimmers**: small desert vehicles that carry a party across the sand. Emir leaves skimmers waiting at Boko to carry the team into the desert (Book 1, Ch109 L21).
- **Pushed past their limits**: one of Emir's people specialises in magical tools and can periodically push the skimmers to speeds beyond their normal capability, so the whole vehicle vibrates (Book 1, Ch110 L4).
- **Their limits**: skimmers cannot go through an astral-space aperture, so the party continues on foot (Book 1, Ch110 L13).
- **Requisitioned skimmers**: expeditions requisition extra skimmers from the city and return them afterwards (Book 1, Ch113 L45, L52).
- **Sand barges**: larger vessels that move people and supplies overland. After Emir's ship sails south down the coast and into shore at an unremarkable patch of desert, his people unload three sand barges that carry the whole group inland to Sky Scar Lake (Book 2, Ch40 L80). The trip takes hours more, into the night (Book 2, Ch40 L81).
- **Expedition transport**: an expedition leaving Greenstone uses carriages to reach desert relay stations, then the captured Ustei barge and smaller barges supplied by the Magic Society (Book 1, Ch98 L4).
- **The Ustei war barge**: it is easily the size of a passenger liner, built on the skeletons of leviathan creatures, with three giant skulls on its flat-nosed bow, and the entire Ustei tribe lives in it (Book 1, Ch90 L14). None of Emir's barges is its size (Book 2, Ch40 L80).
- **Carriages on the Island**: adventurers travel to the Island in a train of carriages (Book 2, Ch32 L38).

### 3. Using this in a story

Desert travel in this world is a logistics problem solved by specialists. A skimmer carries a party across the sand but cannot enter an aperture, a barge carries a whole group and arrives after dark, and a tribe on a barge is a town on the move. Emir's people run most of it.

### 3. Using this in a story

- **Choosing**: for a first-time scene pick Era 1 or Era 3, where the rules of the world are still being taught. For a high-power story pick Era 7, where a single decision touches whole regions.

## Spoiler Management and Secrets

### 1. Summary

How to use the four spoiler levels, a table of the series' main secrets with the line where each first surfaces, and rules for who knows what.

### 2. Facts

**The four levels** (`../00_Project_Structure.md`, section 6)

**Main secrets and where they first surface**
- **Jason is an outworlder**: he tells Rufus that coming to this world is "a fresh start" (Book 1, Ch25 L43); Danielle later investigates him at Gabriel Remore's request (Book 1, Ch48 L101-102). Level 1.
- **Star seeds and the Builder**: an archbishop explains that great astral beings send followers star seeds (Book 2, Ch21 L26-28); Clive explains that great astral beings cannot act in physical reality directly and recruit others (Book 2, Ch21 L112). Level 1.
- **The Order of the Reaper**: Emir says the churches "scoured" it away (Book 2, Ch25 L73); Shade says it survives as a network of quiet allies and the trials are a lure (Book 2, Ch60 L120-122). Level 1.

**In-world belief versus fact**
- Emir's account of the Order of the Reaper (Book 2, Ch25 L73) is contradicted by Shade's account (Book 2, Ch44 L72-78); treat a character's story as belief unless a later line confirms it.
- A great astral being need not fear a god's retribution because gods cannot see into an astral space outside their world (Book 3, Ch62 L33).

### 3. Using this in a story

- **Default to the earliest level that covers the scene.** A level-1 scene uses only the level-0 and level-1 facts of the compendium.
- **Reveal in order.** When a character first learns a secret, use the line above as the earliest point; do not let an earlier-book character speak with the knowledge.
- **Keep levels in clearly marked sections** ("Later developments") when writing new documents.
- **Check a character's knowledge, not the reader's.** A narrator may know a level-3 fact while a level-1 character still believes the churches destroyed the Order.

## Spoiler-Safe Summary: Level 0, Public Setting

### 1. Read this before a session

This page uses only facts visible in the world from the first chapters. It reveals no plot event and no secret. Read it to run a scene in the setting without learning where the story goes.

### 2. The world

- **A magic-soaked world**: people absorb essences and gain abilities; four essences make an iron-rank essence user (Book 1, Ch15 L100; Book 1, Ch21 L65-66).
- **The rank ladder**: iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond (Book 1, Ch22 L58). Each rank suppresses lower ranks (Book 1, Ch19 L42).
- **Lifespan grows with rank**: bronze can pass a hundred years, silver can double that, and gold lasts centuries (Book 1, Ch22 L58).
- **Surges**: magic spikes and monsters pour out about every ten years (Book 1, Ch31 L71).
- **Animals**: heidels, horse-sized, scaled and two-headed, replace horses (Book 1, Ch17 L106).
- **Magic density varies by place**: Greenstone is very low, mid-iron (Book 3, Ch34 L60).
- **Money**: ranked people can live on spirit coins (Book 1, Ch15 L92); see ../../04_Peoples_and_Cultures/Economy_and_Money.md for denominations.

### 3. Safe starting points

- An adventurer's work is monsters, cores and the Adventure Society's ranks; see ../../03_Magic_and_Adventuring/00_Magic_Overview.md.
- A scene can run in any city with its guilds, shops and officials; ../NPC_Rosters_by_Era.md lists names by era (check each name's level).

### 4. Do not state

Any named secret, any death, the plans of gods or astral beings, or anything about other worlds. Move to level 1 only when the table has read Books 1-3.
