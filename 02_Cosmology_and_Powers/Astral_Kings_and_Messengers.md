## 1. Summary

Astral kings rule their own universes. Most are messengers' masters: the messengers are their servants, invaders of worlds, and carry the astral king's message. In Book 8 Jason discovers that he himself is an astral king, which makes him both a political danger among the messengers and an unusual ally to them.

## 2. Astral kings

- **What they are**: "what amounts to sentient miniature universes" (Dawn to Jason, Book 8, Ch67 L53); they forge "their very souls into physical universes, creating domains where their power was unassailable and all-but-unlimited" (Book 9, Ch95 L15). The wiki's added claims about immortality and control over whether inhabitants can die are not confirmed.
- **Prerequisites**: "you're an astral king because you have an astral kingdom... because you possess an astral throne and an astral gate" (Book 8, Ch65 L88); the "astral throne, astral gate and soul forge trifecta" (Book 10, Ch1 L52). The wiki's mention of transcendence is not confirmed. Jason's body is a gestalt of physical and spiritual (Book 9, Ch62 L30: a healer observes that he "was not a dual-entity comprised of body and soul, but a gestalt physical and spiritual being", like messengers).
- **Original astral kings** guided messengers when they came into being, as a parent guides a child; they were not themselves messengers. Boris explains (Book 11, Ch25 L51) that back then there were no limits on rank, because rank limits are a function of the brand, which did not exist. When messengers transcended and became astral kings, they instituted the brand and enslaved their own kind; war and rebellion followed, the free lost, and the defeated went into hiding. The Unorthodoxy descends from the free.
- **Most astral kings are messengers**: Jason is told (Book 9, Ch23 L44) that "the vast majority of astral kings are messengers".
- **Voices of the Will**: servants who carry the bestowed power of an astral king. Jason's familiars hold the source end of that power (Book 9, Ch62 L30-34), which shocks a healer: no silver-rank should possess it, and she realises Jason is a silver-rank astral king. Colin can awaken as a Voice of the Will after hibernation (Book 9, Ch69 L55-59).

## 3. Jason as an astral king (spoilers)

After the Astral Throne and Astral Gate appear (Book 8, Ch49 L105), Jason's spirit realm becomes an astral kingdom. The place-level description is in `06_Places/Astral_Spaces/Spirit_Vault_and_Astral_Kingdom.md`. His familiars gain bonded, eternal forms. He tells Arabelle that being an astral king puts him on a different path from anyone he cares about (Book 9, Ch41 L81).

## 4. Messengers

- **Nature**: gestalt beings whose body and soul are one thing; Jason explains (Book 9, Ch52 L60) that because auras are projections of the soul, their gestalt nature gives them aura abilities that body-soul dual beings do not have. A messenger seen in Book 9 "looked like a celestine with alabaster skin and long hair of spun gold... His eyes were solid gold orbs" (Book 9, Ch52 L5). Messengers can hide their wings, reduce their size and diminish their beauty (Book 13, Ch954 L4), and have hidden on Earth for millennia.
- **Belief and the instinct to kneel**: Book 10 has a chapter titled "The Instinct to Kneel" in which Hess confesses to feeling it (Book 10, Ch16 L48); Dawn tells Jason that messengers are messengers of astral kings, whose message is "Kneel", and that astral kings rule through them rather than invading in person (Book 8, Ch64 L72-80).
- **Rank**: the books confirm that rank limits are a function of the brand (Boris, Book 11, Ch25 L51), that Fal Vin Garath is a silver-rank messenger master and Jes Fin Kaal a gold-rank Voice of the Will (Book 9, Ch56 L5-8). A silver seal on messengers is mentioned in Book 9 (Vesta Carmis Zell "felt one of her seals disappear", a silver-rank seal, when Jason frees Tera; Book 9, Ch102 L66-67), and diamond messengers can begin to resist the brand (Book 11, Ch25 L53). Whether messengers are strictly capped at silver is not established.
- **Social order**: servants races (Benella and others) are subject to messengers; masters may treat them as they please so long as it does not impinge on other messengers (Book 9, Ch56 L5).
- **Methods**: Garth's isolation (duel) power (Book 11, Ch29 L31), aura masks, the world-taker worm and other apocalypse beasts (Book 10, Ch6 L11; Book 9, Ch62 L23), and the natural array's changes that devolve intelligence (Book 9, Ch104 L84). That messengers enslave essence users to dig for the array is from earlier notes and was not rechecked.
- **Resurrection**: does not work on messengers or anyone like them; their life is not just physical and is tenacious (Jason, Book 9, Ch65 L23).
- **Birth**: Jason says messengers are "budded, not born" and are "actually plants, which is kind of crazy" (Book 12, Ch67 L23).

## 5. The Unorthodoxy

Dissenting messengers who want to live without astral kings. Boris Ket Lundi tells Jason (Book 11, Ch24 L86) that he is Unorthodoxy and that his astral king's allies do not know; Book 12 confirms that Navise Den Rigal, a Voice of the Will, serves a minor astral king who is secretly one of the Unorthodoxy's rare astral kings, and that Boris was slipped into the service of Vesta Carmis Zell. Marek Nior Vargas leads a breach force against Yaresh in Book 9, has misgivings about the operation and the politics, and later brings his people into Jason's realm; whether Marek leads the Unorthodoxy is unchecked. Tera Jun Casta is a branded messenger whose brand Jason removes (Book 10, Ch42 L7: it was the brand of her astral king master, Vesta Carmis Zell, and it traumatises her).

## 6. Jamis Fran Muskar

The Council of Kings is, in Book 10 (Ch15 L3), "the closest thing the messengers had to central leadership"; a group of prime avatars, each belonging to a different member, watches the Builder's agents preparing to move a planet. Many astral kings stand outside it (Book 11, Ch24 L86). Jamis Fran Muskar is an astral king whose avatar attends the Book 10 scene; his avatar stands out because its wings have been completely absorbed into his body (Book 10, Ch15 L6), and in Book 12 he angrily accuses messengers of defying "the explicit instructions of the Council of Kings" (Book 12, Ch33 L51-55). In a scene with Vesta Carmis Zell he says that when a certain task is complete the price is paid and Zithis Carrow Vayel, the Builder, will have fulfilled his debt to them (Book 10, Ch15 L9). What the debt is remains unexplained in the text I checked.

## 7. Dangers and hooks

Messenger diplomacy, an enslaved population in a rainforest, a defecting messenger asking for sanctuary, an astral king's message. Hooks: a messenger envoy expects Jason to kneel; a Voice of the Will visits a human city.
