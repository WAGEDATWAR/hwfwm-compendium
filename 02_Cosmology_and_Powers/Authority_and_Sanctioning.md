## 1. Summary

Authority is what great astral beings and gods are made of, and the thing that lets them shape the cosmos. It cannot be taken by force and must be traded, it comes in a safe set state and a dangerous potential state, and it is guided by will. Sanctioning is how transcendent beings change one another: their authority is turned against them, or they change themselves with it. The books treat it as the cosmos's only real punishment, because an immortal cannot be killed.

## 2. How it works

- **What it is**: great astral beings are the most powerful entities in the cosmos, and "the nature of both themselves and their power was known as authority" (Book 8, Ch52 L9). Authority is "the fundamental power of the cosmos", and everything in physical reality is underpinned by it to some degree (Book 8, Ch57 L25).
- **Two states**: set-state authority has been put to a purpose, which makes it fixed, defined and safe ("clay turned into a bowl"); potential-state authority is the unworked clay, "except the clay is actually enriched uranium" (Book 8, Ch57 L25, L35).
- **Jason's domains**: his spirit domains are made of set-state authority, which he calls "the treehouse that I'm allowed to play in" (Book 8, Ch57 L25, L66). His spirit realm is also an aspect of his authority (Book 8, Ch57 L27). He thinks that if his domains broke down into potential-state authority it would kill him (Book 8, Ch57 L70).
- **Transformation zones**: they are "areas of set-state authority breaking down into potential-state authority", the domes being the world sealing them like scabs (Book 8, Ch57 L62).
- **It must be traded**: Jason says authority "has to be traded" and the Builder agrees (Book 8, Ch54 L18-19). The great astral beings' dealings are "striking bargains and making pacts", and pacts are about trading authority (Book 8, Ch52 L51, L88).
- **It cannot be taken**: when the diamond-ranked Shako touches the tablet holding Jason's authority he is thrown away with a sonic boom (Book 8, Ch52 L59). Jason says Shako could no more take it than "burn down the cosmos" (Book 8, Ch52 L70), and tells him "That isn't the kind of power you can just take" (Book 8, Ch52 L72). Shako has none of his own: "It is not my place" (Book 8, Ch52 L56-57).
- **Holding it is dangerous to a mortal**: the authority Jason holds feels like a cancer that would likely destroy him if he did not get rid of it soon, which he suspects is what burns through vessels so quickly (Book 8, Ch54 L15-16). Gods and great astral beings know at once when a mortal outside a spirit domain carries it (Book 8, Ch52 L11-12).
- **Shown to the holder**: it resonates with the great astral beings and gives Jason insight into how authority and the beings made of it work (Book 8, Ch54 L17).

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Will guides authority**: "Will is critical to the use of authority." Overcoming another's will to interfere with their authority is extremely difficult but not impossible, which is why gods have to team up to punish one of their number, and so do great astral beings (Book 11, Ch33 L140).
- **Affinity**: authority is flavoured by its holder. The Builder's is about forging reality and the World-Phoenix's about dimensional forces, so authority can be changed only so far (Book 11, Ch33 L155-157). Jason used authority taken from the Builder for the cloud flask and his astral throne, and authority taken from the World-Phoenix for his astral gate (Book 11, Ch33 L157).
- **Mortals and authority**: mortals use it through intrinsic-mandate magic, which Jason reaches through his familiar Gordon (Book 11, Ch33 L152-153).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Who can hold it**: Shako tells Dawn she is "on the cusp of claiming your own authority" (Book 8, Ch53 L24). She says she is now a hierophant, no longer among the servants but "my own agent" (Book 8, Ch53 L29).
- **Jason's special case**: he accepted a power the great astral beings gave him, meant to let him do what the World-Phoenix needed, with set-state authority only (Book 8, Ch57 L33-35).
- **Inside a spirit domain**: even gods cannot spy on Jason there, as if it were territory from which they are excluded (Book 8, Ch52 L11).
<!-- level:3 -->
- **Shade's authority**: Shade makes use of "authority accumulated over tens of thousands of years" for his self-sanction (Book 11, Ch68 L2206).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **The Builder's tablet, Book 8**: Jason breaks down the Builder's door and holds a sliver of authority as a tablet. He refuses to let Shako take it, offers it to the Builder in a trade, and gets the way into the fundamental realm opened once; the Builder takes the authority back (Book 8, Ch52 L58-70; Book 8, Ch54 L54, L60-61, L99).
- **The original Builder**: the other great astral beings sanctioned him for the twin-universe experiment by taking his authority and giving it to a mortal (Book 13, Ch1017 L11). Dawn says even hierophants are not told the secrets of the Builder's ascension and his predecessor's sanctioning, which are widely held to explain his erratic behaviour (Book 8, Ch19 L40).

<!-- level:3 -->
- **The new Builder's conditions**: when the other beings gave him "the sanctioned authority of original Builder", they set conditions, including the power to revert his mind to the state it was in at sixteen, as a messenger (Book 11, Ch68 L450-454). The Builder-and-nameless problem is described by Raythe and Velius (Book 11, Ch65 L61).
- **The Boundary becomes the World-Phoenix**: the Boundary "wanted to be more than it was" and sanctioned itself. The Cosmic Throne resisted, and the sundering freed the World-Phoenix from the pressure to be turned back (Book 11, Ch68 L1793-1795).
- **Shade sanctions himself**: Shade spends accumulated authority, and the window reads that he "has sanctioned himself" and become a Shadow of the Hegemon. Jason is then "in a state of self-sanction" and "has accepted external sanction", and the familiar contract becomes permanent (Book 11, Ch68 L2205-2218). The Reaper warns "Sanctioning can so easily go wrong" (Book 11, Ch68 L2220).
- **Hero's planned self-sanction**: Gwydion says Hero has decided to sanction himself, something he has considered for longer than any of them has lived (Book 11, Ch68 L707).
- **Purity**: his instability was coming to a head about five hundred years ago and he was likely to sanction himself, "essentially retiring himself and being reborn", until Knowledge persuaded him to leave a divine relic instead (Book 13, Ch1017 L15-16).
- **Undeath's avatar**: Jason sanctions the echo of Undeath's authority that drives an avatar. Gordon works the intrinsic-mandate magic, Jason guides it with will, and he reforges the god's authority with ghost fire into a divine weapon, at risk of Undeath taking root in his soul (Book 11, Ch33 L140-157; Book 11, Ch35 L43-48).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"Sanction" is not the affliction.** The holy affliction Sanction reduces healing and cannot be cleansed while Penance is on the target (Book 12, Ch67 L339). A character who knows the affliction thinks sanctioning is that; Jason says "what I'm talking about is a different kind of sanctioning. It's how entities like gods and great astral beings punish their own kind" (Book 11, Ch33 L9-13).
- **Sanctioning is not killing.** "You can't kill an immortal, but you can sanction them, which means to change them in some way" (Book 13, Ch1017 L11).
- **Sanctioning is not painting over.** Jason guesses it might be "taking someone and slapping a new person over the top"; Dawn tells him to stop watching heist movies (Book 8, Ch55 L31-32).
- **Authority can be ordered over.** Shako tells Jason "Hand it over, Asano" (Book 8, Ch52 L53), but the tablet throws him away, and the Builder concedes that authority must be traded (Book 8, Ch54 L19).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Book 8**: Jason holds authority for the first time and trades it back (Book 8, Ch54 L99).
- **Book 11**: he learns to use another's authority against it, and Shade's self-sanction makes the familiar bond permanent (Book 11, Ch35 L43-47; Book 11, Ch68 L2217-2218).
- **Book 13**: the limits are stated. Knowledge tells Jason that even as a great astral being he "will not have the authority to sanction a god. That is for other gods to do", and Jason answers that he said he would kill her (Book 13, Ch1024 L141-142). Dawn, as a new transcendent, "can't be killed, only sanctioned" (Book 13, Ch992 L63-64).

## 7. Using this in a story

Keep authority scarce, specific and expensive. A mortal who comes by a scrap of it is in danger from the holder and from every great astral being who notices; any deal over it is a trade, not a gift. A sanction should change its target in a way the story can show: a role lost, a mind reverted, a self remade. When a character says "sanction", check whether they mean the affliction or the act. Jason's confrontations with gods stay about will and trades until the rules, as stated in Book 13, are changed by someone with the standing to do so.
