## 1. Summary

The Unorthodoxy is the messenger resistance: the descendants of the free messengers who lost the old war, who live in hiding without the brand and the indoctrination. They are few, scattered and short of allies, but they have astral kings of their own and hidden birthing planets, and a secret way of drawing extra power at their rank. Boris Ket Lundi is their agent on Earth and Pallimustus.

## 2. How it works

- **Origin**: Boris says that originally astral kings guided newborn messengers to form their own marks, and there was no rank limit because limits are a function of the brand. When messengers who had transcended became astral kings they instituted the brand and enslaved their own kind. There was war, the free lost, and the defeated went into hiding. "In time, the leaders of the enemy became the Council of Kings. We became the Unorthodoxy" (Book 11, Ch25 L49-51).
- **The orthodox view**: messenger society teaches that the Unorthodoxy is a scant few in the hidden crevices of society. Boris says most of that is inaccurate (Book 11, Ch25 L47). Orthodox messengers hunt them diligently (Book 11, Ch42 L28), and an orthodox commander worries that talk of a traitor astral king is the thinking that leads to the Unorthodoxy and a purge (Book 12, Ch26 L27).
- **Numbers and hiding**: "the Unorthodoxy is scattered and lacking in allies. We hide because we lack the strength to fight, and nothing ever changes" (Book 10, Ch79 L100). They use different methods in each place so that losing one site does not expose them all (Book 11, Ch42 L28).
- **Birthing planets**: messengers are still allowed to find their own marks only on Unorthodoxy birthing planets (Book 11, Ch42 L24-26). Earth does not have enough magic for the birthing trees (Book 11, Ch42 L29-30).
- **Astral kings of their own**: Boris says the Unorthodoxy has astral kings, "slipping back into the general population" among too many to track (Book 11, Ch25 L56-57). One is the king of Navise Den Rigal, a Voice of the Will (Book 12, Ch26 L19).
- **The secret**: Unorthodoxy messengers have learned to tap their own potential, stalling at a rank in return for "overcharged mana" to enhance abilities. It is hidden from rank-and-file messengers and is "one of our key weapons" (Book 11, Ch40 L111-113).

## 3. Members and associates

- **Boris Ket Lundi**: a gold-rank agent kept below diamond so he can move without attention. He had a brand placed by one of their astral kings to deceive Vesta Carmis Zell (Book 11, Ch25 L53-55), says he was never put through messenger indoctrination (Book 10, Ch79 L102), and claims the Unorthodoxy founded the Cabal (Book 10, Ch79 L98). He spent millennia scouting hidden colonies (Book 13, Ch954 L6).
- **Marek Nior Vargas**: he hopes that Jason's freeing of messengers will turn "dead wood" into "raging inferno" (Book 9, Ch103 L114) and talks of suborning astral kings (Book 9, Ch103 L121). Jason supports the Unorthodoxy in principle but says Marek would turn his people into child soldiers (Book 11, Ch2 L38) and worries about "victory or death energy" (Book 11, Ch2 L75). Marek's people subordinate themselves to Boris (Book 11, Ch49 L71).
- **Erigo Fin Desca**: formerly Unorthodoxy, he says he has moved on and that there is little point fighting an oppressor that can turn you into a mindless drone (Book 10, Ch15 L14-15).
- **Navise Den Rigal**: a Voice of the Will whose king is secretly Unorthodoxy, and who slips Boris into Vesta Carmis Zell's service (Book 12, Ch26 L19).
- **Tera Jun Casta**: freed by Jason, she lives with Boris and his Unorthodoxy messengers despite her views on them (Book 11, Ch68 L2480), and is angered by messengers who want to join (Book 11, Ch40 L58).
- **Freed messengers**: the messengers in the Cardinas zone are not ready for the Unorthodoxy, since Jason's aura contradicts what they were taught (Book 11, Ch42 L8-9), and Boris's own silver-rankers are the difficulty (Book 11, Ch42 L11).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **Boris reveals himself**: he tells Jason's team he is with the Unorthodoxy, that the Cabal was founded by his people, and that his astral king saw what Vesta's plans would do to Pallimustus' membrane and sent him (Book 10, Ch79 L82, L98).
- **The Earth colony**: Boris scouted Earth as a site for hidden colonies, and the Council of Kings left the world alone because it was central to the scandal that got the original Builder sanctioned (Book 13, Ch954 L6-7). Jamis says he has agents there that the council cannot reach (Book 13, Ch1000 L36), and threatens to reveal the colony unless Jason trades (Book 13, Ch1000 L65-74).
- **Boris and the founder's familiar**: Boris worked with the Network founder's familiar to betray the founder (Book 13, Ch954 L9).
- **Refugees**: Jason arranges for freed messengers to be sheltered, as there are large and extremely secret Unorthodoxy enclaves on Earth (Book 11, Ch67 L18).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"The Unorthodoxy is a handful of malcontents."** Boris says it is far larger than taught, and Jali says if messengers learned how many there are, society would fracture (Book 11, Ch25 L47; Book 11, Ch44 L14).
- **"The Unorthodoxy is a rebellion."** Marek says it is far from one, since "you cannot rebel against those without whom you will die" (Book 9, Ch103 L117).
- **"The Unorthodoxy cares about Pallimustus."** Jason says the Unorthodoxy doesn't care about this world, and Boris replies, "No, we care about you" (Book 10, Ch79 L99-100).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Book 9**: Marek sees Jason's work as a way to show his people they can live without astral kings (Book 9, Ch103 L114-121).
- **Book 10**: Boris approaches Jason (Book 10, Ch79 L82-100) and Erigo, the Builder's new vessel, admits he was once part of it and has moved on (Book 10, Ch15 L14-15).
- **Book 11**: Boris explains the Unorthodoxy's history and astral kings (Book 11, Ch25 L47-57).
- **Book 12**: a Voice who serves a secret Unorthodoxy king hides her allegiance (Book 12, Ch26 L19).
- **Book 13**: the Earth colony becomes a bargaining chip with the Council of Kings (Book 13, Ch1000 L65-76).

## 7. Using this in a story

The Unorthodoxy is a movement that survives by being invisible. A story that needs messenger allies can use it, but each ally has a cost: secrecy, a brand to remove or a past they do not trust. The tension between a freed messenger who is ready and one who is not is more interesting than an alliance, and Boris is the best voice for the mix of cheerful bluster and cold calculation the books give him.
