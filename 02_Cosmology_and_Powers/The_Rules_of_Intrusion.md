## 1. Summary

The Rules (or Laws) of Intrusion govern who may invade a world and on what terms. They began as the rules for holy wars, where one faith invades the domain of another, and they also govern dimensional invasion. Since the Cosmic Throne was restored they are strict. Invaders need a pretext or an invitation, and until they meet the terms they are cut off from outside help.

## 2. How it works

- **What they cover**: "The Rules of Intrusion have always been in place. On my world, they mostly apply to holy wars, where one faith invades the domain of another. But, as it turns out, they also govern dimensional invasion" (Book 12, Ch63 L7). Jason describes them as a body of rules about "who can invade a world", including how one god can invade another's territory to conduct a holy war (Book 13, Ch1017 L44).
- **Closed worlds**: there are rules about intruding on worlds that have not declared themselves open to the cosmic community (Book 12, Ch60 L52). Earth was opened to the wider cosmos, which let outside forces in (Book 12, Ch60 L52-54).
- **Bending them**: the Jakaar pirate fleet makes its living on rules that "could be nudged" and others "pushed" (Book 12, Ch57 L13). The lower the rank, the less strenuous the rules, so a gold-rank crew can go places the admiral's main force cannot (Book 12, Ch57 L14), and a thin pretence is enough to satisfy the rules for a low-to-mid magic world (Book 12, Ch57 L21).
- **A pretext or an invitation**: the pirates are told that, with an invitation from the locals, they can "stroll right in, free and clear of the rules" (Book 12, Ch57 L62). The pirates' condition for being allowed on Earth is to remove Jason, and if they do not do so swiftly they will be in violation of the Rules (Book 12, Ch63 L5).
- **Cut off**: while the terms are unmet, the pirate crew is "cut off from the outside" (Book 12, Ch63 L45), and the admiral, through the rules of intrusion, can feel that Earth's universe "was still unavailable to him, even through gold-rank catspaws" (Book 13, Ch954 L40).
- **Cosmic law is stricter**: "There has been flexibility in the rules, going back to before the birth of our worlds, but that has changed. Recently. Cosmic law is much more rigid now" (Book 12, Ch63 L7). The throne's restoration put the rules back in place, and "contravening them is not easy" (Book 13, Ch1017 L44).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Hosts and guests**: an invitation, "either literally or metaphysically", means the guest is no longer an intruder, and the rights of host and guest carry metaphysical weight that sharply reduces the host's ability to harm them (Book 13, Ch1020 L100).
- **Gods need a holy war**: Knowledge cannot simply cross the bridge and take Earth. Earth has gods, and the faith behind them is real, so she has to conduct a holy war, take the world, and eradicate the temples of its closest thing to a resident deity (Book 13, Ch1017 L45).
- **Domains count as temples**: Jason's domains "function like the divine ground held by gods", so Knowledge needs her army to conquer the world and then his temples before she can take her place (Book 13, Ch1017 L47).
- **Avatars and half-transcendents**: half-transcendents use avatars "largely to avoid violating the rules of intrusion" (Book 13, Ch991 L34).
- **Outside influence**: a speaker explains that some people have lived somewhere long enough for it to be home, "but if they bring too much outside influence, they become outsiders in the eyes of the Laws of Intrusion" (Book 13, Ch987 L38).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **The pirates**: the Jakaar fleet's gold-rank crew enters Earth on the locals' invitation and is bound to the terms of the deal; Natala tells Jota that the rules leave them cut off until they remove Asano (Book 12, Ch63 L45).
- **Jota's reading**: he suspects the cult of the World-Phoenix arranged for the pirates to be Jason's first impression (Book 12, Ch64 L20).
- **The bridge**: Mr North warned Jason against anchoring the bridge inside one of his domains, because it would constitute an invitation. Jason therefore built the Earth anchor on the outback site, so that his territories remain bastions that Knowledge cannot reach until she has seized everything else (Book 13, Ch1020 L96-101).
- **Messengers on Earth**: Jason reads a messenger's threat as meaning that the Unorthodoxy's choice to hide on Earth means the laws of intrusion would not stop the Orthodoxy from attacking them, so either the Unorthodoxy leaves or the Orthodoxy is free to invade (Book 13, Ch1000 L72).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"Someone will arrest the intruders."** Lenora asks if the space police will come and arrest them (Book 12, Ch63 L6). Rufus answers that the Cosmic Throne enforces the rules, and that he does not know the consequences of breaking them (Book 12, Ch63 L9).
- **"A friendly bridge into Jason's domains would help."** Jason's Yumi suggests it, but a bridge into his domains would count as an invitation and give Knowledge access (Book 13, Ch1020 L99-100).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Before the repair**: the rules had flexibility (Book 12, Ch63 L7).
- **Book 12**: the restored throne makes them rigid, and the pirates are bound by their terms (Book 12, Ch63 L7, L45).
- **Book 13**: Jason uses the rules in his plan for the bridge and the defence of Earth (Book 13, Ch1017 L44-47; Book 13, Ch1020 L96-101).

## 7. Using this in a story

Make the rules a source of pressure rather than a referee. Invaders have to find or manufacture a pretext, a deal or an invitation, and every such deal has terms and a clock. A character who can honour the form of the rules while twisting their spirit, as the pirates' backers did, makes a good villain. A character who forgets that an invitation is an opening can lose a fortress.
