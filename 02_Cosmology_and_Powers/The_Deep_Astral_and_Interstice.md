## 1. Summary

The astral is the space between worlds, the source of all magic, and the home of the beings who govern the cosmos. Clive explains it to Jason as a bowl of dumpling soup in which worlds float. The deep astral has no geography, time or space of its own, and gold-rankers rarely travel in it without a patron. Interstice, the universe-city, is the capital of the part of it that anyone knows.

## 2. How it works

- **The dumpling soup**: Clive says the cosmos is "a bowl of dumpling soup". Each physical reality is a dumpling, and "the astral is the soup through which we are all the dumplings... floating" (Book 1, Ch69 L97). The astral is also the source of all magic, "pure, unadulterated; the most fundamental building blocks of reality" (Book 1, Ch69 L99).
- **Why worlds differ**: some worlds soak up a lot of the soup and others very little, which is why Pallimustus has essences, awakening stones, quintessence and monsters and Earth did not (Book 1, Ch69 L99-101).
- **Astral spaces**: "think of the soup kind of congealing around a dumpling", giving a magical dimension attached to a world (Book 1, Ch69 L103). Congealed magic can drift away, especially if someone pokes a hole in the world's side, and can touch another world to form a brief unstable link (Book 1, Ch69 L105-107).
- **What the deep astral is**: "just magic. Only magic." Physical substance cannot exist there, which is why Jason's body "ceased to exist" when he was pulled through it (Book 1, Ch69 L113-115). Only his soul, being magical, survived (Book 1, Ch69 L125).
- **Interstice**: a physical reality barely the size of a small sun that has existed longer than most universes. It is a single flat plane with no sun or stars, containing only the city world of Interstice (Book 3, Ch69 L219). It is "both the oldest and largest metropolis in existence" (Book 3, Ch69 L220).
- **How the city looks**: oceans with interlinked artificial islands charged by great waves, hollowed mountains, volcano foundry cities and intelligent species of every stripe in jungles of ziggurats (Book 3, Ch69 L220). It has no sun yet has days and no moon yet has tides, and some call it "the capital city of the cosmos" (Book 3, Ch69 L221).
- **Who runs it**: "There were administrators in Interstice, but no rulers". When the great astral beings have business in a physical reality, this is the one they use (Book 3, Ch69 L222).
- **Arrival**: travellers arrive by dimensional teleportation in portal squares in the submarine bowels of the city, where local authorities note arrivals and make checks; portalling into just any region is frowned upon and magically obstructed (Book 3, Ch69 L224-225).

## 3. Later developments, Books 5 to 9

<!-- level:2 -->
- **Dawn's description**: Interstice is "minute by the standards of a normal reality, but quite large by the standards of a city", more like a free-floating astral space, profoundly magical, and unique in that monsters do not manifest there. Many consider it the capital of the astral, "at least the portion of it that we know" (Book 5, Ch20 L67).
- **Borders and vessels**: the deep astral has no geography in any sense that works for physical reality. Astral spaces are borderlands where physical and astral blend, and the dimensional vessels powerful individuals use are mobile astral spaces (Book 6, Ch35 L39-40).
- **The wall**: Jason says that beyond the wall he sees in his spirit realm is the deep astral, shown as metaphor (Book 7, Ch1 L24). Every monster surge weakens the dimensional wall between a universe and the deep astral (Book 7, Ch36 L14). Until the bridge is complete, anything that tries to travel it is dumped into the deep astral (Book 7, Ch55 L16).
- **Regions**: a "functional metaphor" has regions where the influences of beings meet. The All-Devouring Eye's region, where the speaker and Gordon come from, abuts Legion's, where "life and annihilation are neighbours" (Book 8, Ch55 L92).
- **Who lives where**: Dawn says great astral beings are in charge of the cosmos and inhabit the deep astral, gods are like regional managers of universes, and astral kings are sentient miniature universes (Book 8, Ch67 L53).
- **Interstice and Jason**: once Jason reaches diamond rank, Shade can guide him to Interstice, and Haliastur will introduce him around (Book 9, Ch78 L25-27).
- **Power from it**: Jason's astral gate taps the deep astral, "the infinite plane of raw magic and dimensional forces" (Book 9, Ch95 L16, L33).

## 4. Later developments, Books 11 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **No time or space**: "The deep astral doesn't have time or space." Any perception of them is subjective for travellers in a pocket of reality such as a dimension ship. Each universe has its own space and time and serves as a waypoint for astral geography (Book 11, Ch63 L78-80).
- **Interstice's population**: only diamond-rankers may enter without invitation, and gold-rankers only as menial workers (Book 12, Ch57 L3). Residents come from the wider cosmos or from polities that span dimensions, such as the Radiant Sovereignty and the Constel Empire (Book 12, Ch57 L4).
- **The feeder universes**: the peripheral universes attached to Interstice exist to produce its future elites, with staggeringly rich ambient magic and advanced science and magic. The success rate of getting from the feeder programmes to a gold-rank posting in Interstice is infinitesimally small (Book 12, Ch57 L5-7).
- **A hard place for gold-rankers**: the cosmos is "unkind to gold-rankers". The deep astral is a place for diamond-rankers, astral entities and transcendents, and a gold-ranker needs a patron (Book 12, Ch57 L9-10). Jota thinks of universes as islands in an ocean (Book 12, Ch57 L20).
- **Who has been there**: Boris has been to Interstice, which Jason says is too much to explain right now (Book 12, Ch60 L70-71). Boris says the cult of the World-Phoenix finds people, "not unless you can get into Interstice" (Book 12, Ch60 L68).
- **Travel**: the deep astral is not visible in the normal sense, but its raw magic interacts with the ship's protective bubble, sometimes as arcadian landscapes and sometimes as a void with bizarre objects drifting past (Book 12, Ch58 L39). Crossing the bridge means crossing "the scathing unreality of the deep astral" (Book 12, Ch56 L123). Jason's astral kingdom has a private dock onto the deep astral, which once led to the Cosmic Throne (Book 12, Ch6 L3).
- **Its inhabitants**: the realm of the fully spiritual "has no ranks in power", only authority to act or its absence (Book 13, Ch981 L47). A transcendent like Dawn exists conceptually in the deep astral and has no real body (Book 13, Ch992 L63). Clive calls it "an infinite ocean of unadulterated power" (Book 13, Ch1008 L65).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"Jason's body changed."** Knowledge's account is that his body was changed, but Clive insists it simply stopped existing (Book 1, Ch69 L115-117).
- **"Interstice has rulers."** It has administrators only (Book 3, Ch69 L222).
- **"The deep astral is somewhere you can travel to."** It has no geography; distance is a metaphor and only becomes real where physical and astral meet (Book 6, Ch35 L39).

## 6. Using this in a story

Use the astral as the setting's ocean and its bureaucracy. Worlds are islands, ships are bubbles, and the cost of travel is time and rank. Interstice is a place of power in which even the powerful serve, and a character from the feeder universes is a good way to show how high the ceiling is and how few reach it.
