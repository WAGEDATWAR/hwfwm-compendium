## 1. Summary

A great astral being (GAB) is a cosmic-scale transcendent: an aspect of the cosmos that embodies a concept such as construction, finality of death, time, entropy or dimensional integrity. They are not gods and are not bound by gods. Clive describes them in Book 1 as similar to gods but belonging to no world: they exist between worlds in the deep astral, "gods to the gods, although you wouldn't catch any gods saying that". They do not rule worlds directly. They act through **vessels**: mortals chosen to host them, and **prime vessels**, who carry the fullest part of their power. GABs cannot interact with physical reality directly; they recruit others to act for them (Clive on the Builder, Book 2, Ch21 L112).

## 2. Key facts

- **Authority**: they generate it intrinsically. See `00_Cosmology_Overview.md`.
- **Mortal form**: great astral beings enjoy mortal bodies (Book 11, Ch68 L2526). A vessel can be possessed; Clive notes it is possible, if far from ethical (Book 3, Ch57 L4). Diamond-rank vessels of the great astral beings can withstand the strain and keep their selves, unlike the disposable vessels the Builder uses; eventually the soul can no longer cope and the vessel passes on the role, with no lingering effects and a stronger soul as a result (Book 3, Ch69 L1036-1040).
- **No fear of gods**: Clive says a GAB is one of the few beings that need not fear a god's retribution, since the gods of Pallimustus cannot see into an astral space that is not part of their world (Book 3, Ch62 L33).
- **Sanctioning**: see section 4.
- **Pacts**: after the Sundering, pacts between GABs restrict their direct meddling. Dawn tells Shako in Book 6 that by killing Jason he violated the compact between the Builder, the Reaper and the World-Phoenix (Book 6, Ch18 L79-80).

## 4. Sanctioning

Sanctioning is an extreme change to a transcendent being's nature through a shift of authority, usually punitive. Book 8 (Ch55 L25-31): Jason and Dawn reason that the original Builder was sanctioned, perhaps by "slapping a new person over the top", and Dawn says there are secrets about the Builder's ascension and his predecessor's sanctioning that even hierophants are not privy to (Book 8, Ch19 L40). Examples in the text: the Boundary sanctioned itself into the World-Phoenix (Book 11, Ch68 L1793-1795); Shade sanctioned himself into a Shadow of the Hegemon (Book 11, Ch68 L2209-2214); Hero decided to sanction himself (Book 11, Ch68 L707-709); Purity was likely about to sanction himself when Knowledge interfered, and the wiki says he expended his authority on a single artefact (Book 13, Ch1017 L14-16; wiki). "You don't kill gods, you sanction them." Only a god can sanction a god.

## 5. The Builder (Level 1; notes to 3)

- **Obsession**: "obsessed with physical reality while, by its very nature, being unable to co-exist with it", it is building a world of its own in the deep astral from stolen astral spaces; because an astral being cannot interact with physical reality directly, it "recruits people to carve off the astral spaces connected to their world, then it steps in and claims them" (Book 2, Ch21 L106-112).
- **Methods**: cults; **star seeds**, the means by which great astral beings "send their followers" power instead of essences and stones, which turn a heretic's remnant power into a means of exploding themselves (Book 2, Ch21 L26-30) and which are "transcendent-rank objects" not purged by an essence ability or simple ritual (Book 2, Ch21 L36); cultists and captives "exploded into crystal stars" (Book 2, Ch59 L113); constructs such as golems and clockwork animals; Clockwork Kings, gold-rank native creatures of the Builder's world, dragon-like and not monsters, whose cores make the cult's clockwork cores (Book 2, Ch25 L37-45).
- **Cult**: its cultists in Greenstone summoned Jason by accident while trying to summon a Clockwork King.
- **History**: the original Builder crafted twin universe seeds, Earth and Pallimustus; was sanctioned and replaced.
- **Book 8 settlement**: the Fundamental Realm Authority Token enters Jason's inventory in Book 8, and his ability to access the fundamental realm is lost (Book 8, Ch49 L89-91); he looted it from the destroyed artefacts (Book 8, Ch50 L94-95), and Dawn tells him he will need to give it back (Book 8, Ch51 L68-70). The separate item the World-Phoenix crafted in Book 6 is for establishing the link (Book 6, Ch18 L124). The deal that ends the Builder's invasion is not checked.

## 6. The World-Phoenix (Level 2/3)

- **Role**: guardian of dimensional integrity; gave Jason the World-Phoenix Token and Nirvanic Transfiguration.
- **Identity**: formerly the Boundary; resisted by the Cosmic Throne when changing, and freed by the Sundering. She speaks of the Boundary in her own words in Book 11 (Book 11, Ch68 L1793-1795).
- **Relationship to Jason**: sent Dawn; in the Rumble in the Jungle, the soul-space battleground where Jason's team stands against the nameless great astral beings (Book 11, Ch68 L171-175).
- **Prime vessel**: Helsveth (Book 11, Ch68 L2527-2530).

## 7. Hierophants

A hierophant is a former prime vessel who still serves a great astral being. Book 3 describes the World-Phoenix's cult: its head, the First Servant, was the great astral being's primary vessel, and former vessels end up as peak existences, even among their rank. Dawn is the World-Phoenix's hierophant; Soramir tells Jason (Book 7, Ch60 L27) that the Hierophant is allowed to intervene in the world a single time.

## 8. Dangers and hooks

GABs rarely appear in person. Their reach is felt through cults, vessels, relic wars and rules. Hooks: a cult of the Reaper, a Builder platform in the sky, a hierophant's request, a time-keeper's agent policing a link.
