## 1. Summary

The Council of Kings is the nearest thing the messengers have to central leadership: a loose body of the strongest astral kings, the heirs of the side that won the old war against free messengers. It guides but does not rule, many astral kings stand outside it, and its members are rivals who cooperate. Its de facto leader is Jamis Fran Muskar.

## 2. How it works

- **What it is**: "the closest thing the messengers had to central leadership", represented in Book 10 by a collection of prime avatars, each belonging to a different member (Book 10, Ch15 L3).
- **Membership**: it "ostensibly represents the strongest of the astral kings", with many others outside it, "even a few powerful eccentrics who have declined to be part of our little group" (Book 10, Ch15 L40). The council does not know how many astral kings stand outside it (Book 11, Ch24 L86).
- **Authority**: "The Council of Kings may guide us, but you do not rule us" (Book 12, Ch33 L55). Jamis says he is a member and that some consider him its leader, "although it has no such thing" (Book 12, Ch35 L11). Tera Jun Casta calls him "the closest thing we have to a true, singular leader" (Book 13, Ch999 L77).
- **Not absolute**: the council's power is not absolute, as what happened in Boko already shows, and controlling it is "a delicate affair" (Book 12, Ch35 L81).
- **Rivalry**: "Every person here is an enemy to everyone else in this room, yet we work together regardless" (Book 10, Ch15 L50).
- **Messenger structure**: astral kings rarely mix their forces, to protect the culture of hierarchy. Large operations are coordinated by meetings of Voices of the Will, the commanders of each king's local forces (Book 12, Ch26 L18).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Vesta Carmis Zell**: she sits in the Book 10 meeting, where Jamis tells her she is the only astral king in the room not focused on the larger goal and not some "personal project" (Book 10, Ch15 L30). In Book 9 she calls the council after Jason frees Tera (Book 9, Ch102 L69). By Book 13 she is "on the outside of the Council of Kings, particularly after her losses on Pallimustus" (Book 13, Ch999 L77).
- **Outside the council**: at a Book 12 meeting of Voices of the Will, the Voices belonging to council members are absent (Book 12, Ch26 L20). The council has instructed the forces not to provoke Jason (Book 12, Ch26 L29), and one Voice says none of their kings belong to the council (Book 12, Ch26 L30).
- **Hypocrisy**: Jamis says his people have a détente with the originals: "We avoid them, and those associated with them. They, in turn, do not interfere with us", and the arrangement keeps the messenger masses from seeing it (Book 13, Ch1000 L38). A few rebel astral kings fit within the council's ideological framework, so long as they do not highlight it (Book 13, Ch1000 L40).
- **Private agents**: Jamis has agents on Earth that the council cannot access (Book 13, Ch1000 L34-36).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **The Builder's planet-move**: prime avatars of council members watch the Builder's world engineers move a planet, and one of them wants to destroy Erigo Fin Desca as a traitor until Jamis stops him (Book 10, Ch15 L3, L16-19).
- **Jamis on Jason**: he tells Vesta he does not fear the rise of another original and will not annihilate every potential threat, since "our species would spend their entire lives roaming the cosmos, smiting silver- and bronze-rankers" (Book 10, Ch15 L43, L57).
- **Boko**: Jamis rebukes messenger commanders for defying "the explicit instructions of the Council of Kings" (Book 12, Ch33 L52, L65) and later meets Jason, saying he would offer him a seat on the council if he would take it (Book 12, Ch33 L71).
- **Jamis's offer**: empowered by the council, he offers the withdrawal of a significant number of occupying forces in return for Jason not campaigning against the messengers (Book 12, Ch35 L66). Jason points out that the territories are the ones now useless to their goals, and Jamis admits it (Book 12, Ch35 L68-70).
- **The squeeze on Earth**: the council saw no value in poking at the great astral beings and avoided Earth, which was central to the scandal that saw the original Builder sanctioned (Book 13, Ch954 L7).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"The council commands all messengers."** It does not, as Book 12 shows when kings and Voices defy it (Book 12, Ch33 L52-55).
- **"The council is united."** Its members are rivals who work together and fall out, as Vesta does (Book 10, Ch15 L30, L50).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Book 9**: Vesta convenes the council over Jason (Book 9, Ch102 L69).
- **Book 10**: the council watches the Builder's task, with Jamis restraining Vesta (Book 10, Ch15 L3-62).
- **Book 12**: the council has told its forces not to provoke Jason (Book 12, Ch26 L29), is defied at Boko (Book 12, Ch33 L52), and Jamis meets Jason (Book 12, Ch35).
- **Book 13**: Jamis reappears with a request and a threat, and the council's ignorance of the Unorthodoxy colony on Earth is part of what Jason trades on (Book 13, Ch1000 L36, L74).

## 7. Using this in a story

Use the council as an alliance of rivals. Any messenger action has to be read against the council's instructions and its members' private agendas. Jamis is the best face for it: polite, curious, amused, and capable of threats delivered as courtesy. A story that needs a messenger threat can pit one king against another before it pits any of them against Jason.
