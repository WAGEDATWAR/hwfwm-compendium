## 1. Summary

The cosmos of the books has layers. Physical worlds such as Earth and Pallimustus sit inside the **astral**, the source of all magic. Beyond the worlds lie the **deep astral**, where bodies cannot survive, and the cosmic community of transcendent beings: **gods**, **great astral beings** (GABs) and **astral kings**. Ordinary people interact with this layer only through gods and miracles. Most of the plot of the later books is about what the transcendent do to the worlds, and what the worlds can do back.

## 2. The shape of the cosmos

- **Dumpling soup** (Clive's model, Book 1, Ch69 L85-91): the cosmos is a bowl of dumpling soup. Each world is a dumpling; the astral is the soup in which all the worlds float and is the source of all magic. Some worlds soak up a great deal of it (Pallimustus) and others very little (Earth), which is why essences, awakening stones, quintessence and monsters appear on one and not the other. Where the soup congeals around a world it forms an astral space. Congealed magic can drift off like a tendril, especially if someone pokes a hole in the world's side, as Landemere Vane's summoning did; if the tendril touches another world a brief, unstable link forms. Jason was pulled through such a link across the deep astral, which is pure magic: his physical body ceased to exist because physical substance cannot exist there, and his soul, being magical rather than physical, was given a new body in the way a summoned familiar is given one.
- **The fundamental realm**: the reality underneath physical reality, which Jason's Book 5 interface describes as "a space of combined physical and astral nature"; the World-Phoenix calls it what Jason calls node space (Book 5, Ch40 L49-50; Book 6, Ch18 L124). Transformation zones are the source of reality cores (Book 6, Ch1 L20; Book 11, Ch68 L2257 shows the supply cut off when the zones stop appearing). In Book 11 a messenger says Jason "is a seed that the World-Phoenix planted"; whether reality cores act as batteries and whether universes are "seeds" are not established in the text I checked.
- **Interstice**: the universe-city at the centre of the cosmos. Only diamond-rankers may enter without invitation; gold-rankers such as Jota Withers are admitted only as menial workers (Book 12, Ch57 L3). Peripheral universes attached to it exist to produce future elites, raising their people to gold and ultimately diamond in feeder programmes whose success rate is tiny. Its population comes from the wider cosmos and from multi-dimensional polities such as the Radiant Sovereignty and the Constel Empire. See `06_Places/Astral_Spaces/00_Astral_Overview.md`.

## 3. Authority and will

The full account is in `Authority_and_Sanctioning.md`; the notes below are the short form.

- **Authority** is the fundamental power of great astral beings and gods: their flesh, blood and soul in the sense of what they are. Jason's Book 8 lecture distinguishes a *set* state (authority put to a purpose, "clay turned into a bowl", as in his spirit domains) from a *potential* state (raw, like enriched uranium) (Book 8, Ch57 L25-35). Transformation zones are set-state authority breaking down into potential-state authority. Clive teaches (Book 11, Ch33 L112) that authority is the highest order of magic: the magic gods use for miracles and GABs use to regulate the mechanics of the cosmos, such as birthing universes and governing the rules of life and death.
- **Will** is an active force developed through aura training. It guides authority.
- **Intrinsic-mandate magic** is high-order transcendent magic that shapes reality by decree; Gordon's orrery ritual is an example: Jason is told that Gordon can use intrinsic-mandate magic, which operates on the same scale as a great astral being (Book 10, Ch5 L80-84).
- **Akashic Speech** is the speech of the transcendent; Jason's avatar inherits it (Book 12, Ch10 L17-22).

## 4. The Cosmic Throne and the Sundering (Level 3)

Full accounts: `Cosmic_Throne_and_the_Sundering.md`, `The_System.md` and `The_Rules_of_Intrusion.md`. The notes below are the short form.

- The **Cosmic Throne** was the ultimate authority that regulated the cosmic forces and the great astral beings. The great astral beings **sundered** it to escape regulation, replacing it with pacts between themselves. (Raythe and Velius's account, Book 11, Ch64 L24-27; Jason's briefing in Book 13, Ch1017 L6-8.)
- Not all agreed. Book 11 (Ch64 L49; Ch65 L75): the Keeper, the All-Devouring Eye and the Word in the Silence "ever stood apart"; the Celestial Book and the Seeker of Songs were against the sundering from the start; the Reaper, Legion and the Whisper in Corners "have come around and will fight" (Raythe).
- The **Sundered Throne** still exists, even broken; Jason senses it as a thread that rivals the might of the great astral beings (Book 11, Ch1 L22-24). Some **avatars of doom** remain attached to it; they belong to the All-Devouring Eye and were loaned to the Throne (Book 11, Ch34 L97, L103).
- Raythe tells Jason (Book 11, Ch65 L55) that "only the nameless" great astral beings will stand with the World-Phoenix against him. Velius explains that the nameless "govern the functions of the cosmos. Life, death, time, matter" and are "unbound from the strictures of their duties" by the removal of the Throne's oversight (Book 11, Ch65 L57-58). Restoring the Throne requires a transcendence method plus a connection to it; Jason sits on it at the end of Book 11, and restrictions from the sundering unravel as he repairs it (for instance, fixed essence combinations stop being fixed).
- **After the restoration** the cosmic law is "much more rigid" (Book 12, Ch63 L7): the **Rules of Intrusion**, which in Pallimustus mostly govern holy wars between faiths, also govern dimensional invasion. Invaders on a world that has not declared itself open to the cosmic community must remove the person they entered to remove; if they fail they violate the rules. Kreegle's pirates and the messengers on Earth are bound this way.

## 5. Time and its keeper

The **Keeper of Moments** (or Sands) governs the flow of time between universes and polices attempts at time travel; Boris says (Book 11, Ch63 L98) that time travel is the worst of ideas on a cosmic scale and that the Keeper "comes down hard" on anyone who tries. Its prime vessel is Raythe, who is powerful enough, he says, to "trip over and transcend by accident" and has held a prime vessel position longer than anyone he knows. Shade tells Jason that, for the Keeper, "the past is inviolate" (Book 6, Ch14 L82). The deep astral has no time or space of its own (Book 11, Ch63 L76). The Builder's synchronisation of the timelines of Earth and Pallimustus is noted as something the Keeper may not have liked (Book 11, Ch63 L100).

## 6. What ordinary people see

Gods answer prayers and send miracles; great astral beings are rarely named outside scholars and cults, except the Reaper's followers and the Builder's cult; messengers appear as invaders; the astral shows itself in apertures, proto-astral spaces and transformation zones. See `06_Places/Astral_Spaces/` for the physical expressions.

## 7. Open questions

- Whether the "universe seeds" model is Clive's speculation or established.
- Exact content of the pacts that replaced the Throne.
- The Reaper's stance on the sundering: Book 11 shows the Reaper's vessel Velius saying the Reaper supported it while the Keeper was always against it, and Raythe later lists the Reaper among those who "have come around and will fight". The reading in this compendium is that the Reaper supported the sundering and has since changed sides; the book does not say more.
