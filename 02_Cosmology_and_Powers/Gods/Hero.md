## 1. Summary

Hero is the god of heroes, the only core deity without subordinate gods. He appears in the books almost only through the Cup of Heroes, a miracle that makes a dying hero a demigod at a terrible price. Hero is regretful about it, and by Book 11 plans to sanction himself to give mortals a gentler option.

## 2. Facts

- **Name and rank**: Jason notes "the god of heroes is called Hero. They really stick to that straightforwards naming convention" (Book 1, Ch45 L39). Hero is "the only core deity not to have subordinate gods" (Book 1, Ch48 L92).
- **Appearance**: a leonid larger than Gary's demigod form, with a divine aura (Book 11, Ch68 L310).

<!-- level:3 -->
- **The Cup of Heroes**: when Gary goes out to die against the undead, Hero appears and tells him his choice, not his life, was the sacrifice: "You believed that you came here to die. People keep thinking that what the gods want in sacrifice is their lives, but that is not the case" (Book 10, Ch86 L45). He conjures a goblet, a dewdrop falls into it, and Gary snatches it, saying "I know what it is" (Book 10, Ch86 L47-50). Hero calls his choice the reason both are there (Book 10, Ch86 L53).
- **What it does**: Gary drinks, and the window says he has absorbed divine power belonging to the god of heroes, which will leave his soul unless he is isolated from the god's influence, and reforges his body into a gestalt (Book 10, Ch86 L59-63). His hammer, fire and iron essences become Divine versions at Gold 0 and his forge confluence is replaced by a Demigod essence (Book 10, Ch86 L67-72).
- **The aftermath**: Hero acts "like a proud but sad father" (Book 10, Ch86 L52). Jason, learning of it, speaks in a flat voice, and Farrah warns him not to do anything crazy (Book 10, Ch89 L26-40).
- **A gift to Jason**: afterwards Hero tells Jason he can only push the limits of his influence a little, enough to give him a gift as the Healer did: a Genesis Command: Source, a transcendent orb with "the authority to link power" (Book 10, Ch89 L77-83). Hero says "It is the best I can do" and is gone (Book 10, Ch89 L83).
- **On Purity**: Hero guesses Purity turned himself into an artefact because the continental war made Purity seem a dark god, and says that is why he himself is wary of changing his own nature (Book 10, Ch89 L70-75).
- **How he sees himself**: "I am the god of heroes. I, of all, understand that actions, not power, are what makes one worthy of respect. And all I do is kill heroes" (Book 11, Ch68 L326, L334).
- **His planned self-sanction**: Gwydion Remore tells Gary Hero has decided to sanction himself, as he has considered for longer than any of them has been alive. It will not be as drastic as Purity's. He wants to add "another miracle option. Something less powerful than the Cup of Heroes, with less of a price", by making the relics of heroes who drank the cup usable again, "if only for a short time", so that "the stories of heroes past live on in heroes of the future" (Book 11, Ch68 L707-709). Gary is one of the reasons (Book 11, Ch68 L711).
- **Hero's church**: Gwydion Remore is a priest of Hero (Book 11, Ch60 L24). The church has collected the relics of dead demigods for millennia (Book 11, Ch68 L709).

## 3. Using this in a story

Hero is a god of tragedy: the god who is asked to choose between dying and becoming something that dies slowly. His church is respectful, and its priests tend to carry guilt. A story that wants a god's help in a desperate hour can use him, but only for a heroic sacrifice, and he will say plainly that it will cost the hero. His self-sanction is a hook for a changed Hero, a new miracle and a church that has to adapt.
