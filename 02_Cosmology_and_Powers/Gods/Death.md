## 1. Summary

The god of Death on Pallimustus governs the passage of souls into the astral, and is distinct from the Reaper, the great astral being who is "very big on the finality of death". The church certifies deaths and returns, opposes Undeath, and is dryly protective of the order of things. The deity changes guise, and the books speak of both a goddess and a lord.

## 2. Facts

- **Domain**: the Reaper is "very big on the finality of death. Some consider it the true god of death, as all our god of death governs is the passage of the soul into the astral" (Book 2, Ch25 L67). Where there is no god of death to guide the soul into the astral, it drifts into the Reaper's grasp on its own (Book 3, Ch69 L189).

## Books 4 to 9

- **The god and the Reaper work in balance**: the Goddess of Death and the Reaper "each have their areas of authority and they work with an ebb and flow" (Book 7, Ch60 L100). The Reaper's cult may not trespass on the local god's domain (Book 4, Ch3 L30).
- **Precautions against dying**: Death keeps a close eye on resurrections. A priest tells Jason that the goddess knew "each time you had fallen and each time you had returned" the moment he entered her sphere, and has asked that he be more careful (Book 7, Ch9 L123, L125).
- **Certification**: the Church of Death confirms that returned adventurers are who they claim, and its paperwork helps with registration (Book 7, Ch7 L64; Book 7, Ch11 L83; Book 7, Ch17 L105).
- **Alignment with a cult**: the Church of Death is "closely aligned" with a cult that works with it, doing public-facing things while the cult is the hidden hand (Book 9, Ch27 L87).

## 3. Later developments (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Three guises**: in Book 10 Death appears to Jason as "a stocky, middle-aged woman with a colourful dress" with the warmth of a matronly tavern keeper, instead of the stern-faced man who normally appears in that region (Book 10, Ch82 L62, L65). To Miriam Vance he appears as a pale male elf in grey Yaresh clothing with a voice "cold and hollow as an empty tomb" (Book 10, Ch85 L12). In Book 12 she is a woman with plain features, pale skin and a grey dress with a faded blue flower pattern (Book 12, Ch8 L4).
- **The miracle**: Jason says prayer isn't his thing, and Death calls his prayer "very mediocre". She says a miracle needs one of her servants or a grand sacrifice, and she has no interest in the sacrifice of lives (Book 10, Ch82 L61-72). She declines his two resurrections as "not enough" (Book 10, Ch82 L77-78). The miracle that follows burns the undead to ash with ghost fire (Book 10, Ch85 L5).
- **The pact**: Jason makes a pact with the Pallimustus goddess of death, enacted by her guiding his soul to change itself, with no access to it (Book 10, Ch83 L64, L69). He gains power to diminish undeath energy, which makes his aura anathema to the priests of Undeath (Book 11, Ch28 L16). Death's ghost fire is the model for the weapon he forges (Book 11, Ch35 L45-46).
- **A talk with Miriam**: Death tells Miriam to stand, "You still command this battle" (Book 10, Ch85 L15), and explains how Disguise moved slowly to take over Purity (Book 10, Ch85 L33).
- **Giving back**: in Book 12 Death returns the resurrection power Jason had traded, saying holding it back would be "petty... unbecoming at our level" and thanking him for stymieing Undeath (Book 12, Ch8 L8-13). She says the gods will pass with their planet, "yet you will remain" (Book 12, Ch8 L15).
- **Limits**: Death's remit "does not reach beyond this planet, which itself will have its time" (Book 10, Ch82 L76).

## 4. Using this in a story

Death is patient, fair and unimpressed. She is tired of people who will not stay dead and relieved when someone else deals with Undeath. A scene with her should feel like a meeting with a good-natured official whose job is also the end of everything. Her church is a bureaucracy of certificates, funerals and quiet help against the undead.
