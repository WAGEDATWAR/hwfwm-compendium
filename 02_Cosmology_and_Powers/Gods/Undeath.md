## 1. Summary

Undeath is the god of the unliving, the dark counterpart of Death. His church lies dormant for generations and strikes when it has saved up the god's chances to intervene. In Book 10 it nearly destroys the Cardinas expedition, and in Book 11 Jason sanctions the echo of Undeath's authority left in an avatar. Undeath is a menacing but brisk god who compromises with other gods, including the high priestess of Destruction whose grandson serves in his church.

## 2. Facts

- **Early notice**: Gary says "Undeath is bad" (Book 1, Ch20 L21). A character recalls a zombie plague with a Temple of Undeath at its heart, whose high priest was the local mayor (Book 7, Ch9 L97-99).
- **How the church acts**: Hana Shavar says the Church of Undeath "does not act frequently. We don't give them the chance", so it lies dormant for generations and strikes when it gets one. "It's like their god has been saving up his chances to intervene in the world. Expect to see some dark miracles" (Book 10, Ch78 L38).
- **Garth and Jameela**: Garth, a zemravore, leads the church's priests in Cardinas (Book 11, Ch8 L20), and with Jameela commands the undead (Book 10, Ch88 L70-73). Garth's tactic: "The harder-fought the battle, the more we have to work with when it's done", and every battle that does not crush them makes the church stronger (Book 10, Ch88 L73).
- **Claiming territory**: Jason sees the god seeping into the ground and making it his domain, marked by purple light (Book 10, Ch85 L63; Book 10, Ch87 L19). Death says "Undeath has been liberally spending his influence in this place" (Book 10, Ch85 L70).
- **Appearance**: in Cardinas Undeath appears as a living man in simple black robes with empty sockets containing a crimson spark, and a deep voice "like gravel being dumped into a silo" (Book 10, Ch88 L74-76).
- **His manner**: he accepts that Death and Hero's interventions at the critical moment need a response, tells Garth he will not punish him for following instructions, and complains that his priest Jeff's grandmother is a high priestess of Destruction: "When dealing with other gods, compromises must be made, even by me" (Book 10, Ch88 L77-85). He says "we are forced into another compromise" over animating a vessel that will hold only a fraction of his power (Book 10, Ch88 L89-91).
- **The avatar**: he allows an avatar to be drawn into the transformation zone to aid his priests, and it fights Jason and loses (Book 11, Ch65 L43). It is "just power and the echo of intent", with no active will behind it, since the god's will is locked out (Book 11, Ch33 L99). Jason sanctions its authority and turns it into a divine weapon (Book 11, Ch35 L42-47; see `../Authority_and_Sanctioning.md`).
- **Jason's reaction**: his aura becomes anathema to the priests of Undeath (Book 11, Ch28 L16).
- **A visit in Greenstone**: the god leaves when told to by Jason, and when Jason calls him "Steve" the others whisper (Book 11, Ch62 L28, L39). The text says "The god of Undeath's name is Undeath" (Book 11, Ch62 L77).
- **Hidden strongholds**: he is among the gods who keep only hidden strongholds (Book 10, Ch85 L40).
- **Jason's view**: Jason calls Undeath "a complete prick" in a conversation about the gods, noting that some others have been kind to him (Book 12, Ch7 L14).

## 3. Using this in a story

Use Undeath as the patient storm. His church is a threat that arrives once in a generation and has been preparing. His own manner is more businesslike than evil, and he does not rant. A villain in his church believes the cost of every battle is an investment, and that is a more frightening mindset than bloodlust.
