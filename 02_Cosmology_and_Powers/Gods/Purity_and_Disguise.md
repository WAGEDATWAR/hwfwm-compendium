## 1. Summary

Purity is the god of cleanliness, with both gentle and extreme aspects; his church is rich, strict and prominent in Greenstone. For centuries the god in the Church of Purity was not Purity but Disguise, who masqueraded in his place with Knowledge's help. The church's collusion with the cult of the Builder, Disguise's role in the Order of Redeeming Light, and the real Purity's relic are the revelations behind the Book 3, 8 and 10 plots.

## 2. Facts

- **The church**: Anisa Lasalle is a priestess of the God of Purity (Book 1, Ch20 L80), and a priest of Purity in clean robes attends the Adventure Society inquiry team (Book 2, Ch17 L31). The temple has church militants who guard its interests (Book 2, Ch37 L105).
- **Collusion with the Builder cult**: when the church's involvement with the cult comes to light, a number of its key members vanish on "previously scheduled sabbaticals", including the archbishop Nicolas Hendren, spelt Hedron in that line (Book 3, Ch1 L106). The people from the church are detained under the authority of the ecumenical council (Book 3, Ch5 L67).
- **Hendren**: the archbishop calls out, "You think afflictions can take down a priest of Purity?" in the fight (Book 3, Ch59 L45).

## 3. Later developments, Books 4 to 9

<!-- level:2 -->
- **A bargain with the Builder**: Dawn says "the god Purity has struck a bargain with the Builder": Purity lays the groundwork and the Builder helps him cleanse the world of what he has come to see as unclean, taking the abundant astral spaces and leaving Purity to rebuild civilisation in his image (Book 4, Ch68 L139). Clergy of the Order say their "so-called God of Purity handed our world to a being that would taint our very souls" (Book 7, Ch16 L53).
- **Purification ability**: converts of the Builder cult have a purifying ability that comes ultimately from the god, passed along a chain from the god to a purified clockwork king, a purified core, and the converted (Book 8, Ch10 L24).
- **Carlos's theory**: the priest of the Healer argues that Purity has been replaced by one of the deception gods, most likely Deceit or Disguise (Book 8, Ch48 L14). Disguise is "the lord of illusion, manipulating assumptions and walking unnoticed in plain sight", and "if the god of disguises chooses to take on a disguise, even Knowledge or Truth cannot reveal it" (Book 8, Ch48 L16, L22). The gods have rules governing how they relate (Book 8, Ch48 L22-26).
- **The ecumenical council**: it was convened by the churches to judge the church's behaviour, and found it had been operating well outside its dictates for some time (Book 8, Ch48 L32).
- **Unanswered**: Emir says people everywhere are trying to learn exactly when and why the original Purity was sanctioned. The churches either haven't been told or aren't saying, and the diamond-rank community has been digging into it (Book 9, Ch51 L15-17).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **The Order of Redeeming Light**: Carlos and Arabelle conclude that the Flames of Redemption are an extreme modification of lesser vampirism, and that part of it comes from Disguise, "the god who was pretending to be Purity", with the order founded within the church only after Disguise took on Purity's mantle (Book 10, Ch39 L45-46). The Flames instil an absolute faith in Purity as a safety mechanism against deprogramming (Book 10, Ch39 L55-56).
- **Death on Disguise**: Death confirms that Disguise moved slowly, shifting the clergy over generations towards the extreme aspects of Purity's remit, and rejected the priests who knew the original (Book 10, Ch85 L33-35). She says the gods were frustrated but could do nothing, since "the hidden gods operate differently" (Book 10, Ch85 L40).
- **Hero on Purity**: he guesses that the god of purity has positive and negative aspects, and that a war over a human empire's concept of purity put him in danger of being seen as a dark god. The Order of Redeeming Light's seed was planted then, before Disguise (Book 10, Ch89 L71-73).
- **The "fake god"**: a character refers to a deal made "with the fake god of Purity" (Book 12, Ch32 L74).
- **Jason's account**: Purity was in conflict within himself, as he tends to be, about five hundred years ago. He was likely to sanction himself, retiring and being reborn, but Knowledge persuaded him to leave a divine relic instead. She hid it until about a quarter of a century ago, and she aided Disguise in masquerading as Purity in return for his help (Book 13, Ch1017 L15-18).

## 5. Using this in a story

The Church of Purity is the best example in the books of a faith that can be sincere and betrayed at once. Its people believe, and their god is a fraud, so a scene with them should respect their sincerity. For a villain, Disguise is a patient, careful trickster who builds a long con out of a real faith. The real Purity is an absent god whose relic is still waiting to be found.
