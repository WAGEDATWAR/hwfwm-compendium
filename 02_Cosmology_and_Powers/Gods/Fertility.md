## 1. Summary

Fertility is the god of growth and birth, and his church is far more practical than its murals suggest. It grows children in vats, bridges races so that mixed couples can have children, supplies food in surges, and in Book 12 is the thing the brighthearts most need to rebuild their people. Its temples are covered in murals of people being fertile, which Jason notes with some alarm.

## 2. Facts

- **The murals**: Jason avoids pairing with "the god of fertility? The one with all the provocative murals?" (Book 1, Ch82 L73), and Farrah describes the temples as covered "in murals of people... being fertile" (Book 10, Ch8 L41).

## 3. Later developments, Books 6 to 9

<!-- level:2 -->
- **Priestesses**: a priestess of Fertility is among those Humphrey's team meets (Book 6, Ch47 L910).
- **Food in a surge**: the Temple of Fertility keeps secure stations in outlying areas with infrastructure to grow large amounts of food quickly in a small space, critical to stopping fortress towns starving during a surge (Book 7, Ch21 L3-5). Jason leaves its main doors with his storage space loaded with supplies (Book 7, Ch21 L3).
- **Children without a partner**: the church can let people have a child without ever touching one another, growing the child in a jar, which is how most aristocrats do it (Book 7, Ch39 L74). A speaker says there are Gellers everywhere and that one would have to ask the Church of Fertility about that (Book 7, Ch45 L106).
- **Mixed-race births**: one person says the church allowed an elf and a human to have children and purged his father's "impurity" to produce him (Book 8, Ch17 L12), and describes another birth made possible with the church's help (Book 8, Ch20 L18).
- **Records**: a church record-keeper finds how the church helped Melody Jain have a child, which her order's overseers never knew (Book 9, Ch27 L26).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 12 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **A messenger of the god**: a priestess in the full robes of the Church of Fertility, with young acolytes, arrives at Jason's door saying "My god told me that Jason Asano was in here" (Book 10, Ch1 L88-89).
- **Feeding Yaresh**: after the messenger attack the Fertility church supplies all the food for the refugees and its priestess runs Jason's relief building, which he turns into a multi-storey food court (Book 10, Ch8 L37).
- **In vats**: a character was "cracked out of the vat" by the church before he learned to walk (Book 11, Ch52 L54), and a speaker says he might have to recruit a priest of Fertility and one of Knowledge for a complicated plan (Book 11, Ch46 L29). He is advised to prioritise meeting the church of Fertility (Book 11, Ch63 L14).
- **Velius's cover**: Velius, a prime vessel of the Reaper, works on Earth as a fertility doctor, in a role normally filled by the church of Fertility, knowing all its magic that does not need divine influence (Book 11, Ch68 L776-778).
- **The brighthearts**: the church supplies the thing the brighthearts need most, children. In only a few years the streets are teeming with them (Book 12, Ch6 L27). The brighthearts' leader says excluding the Church of Fertility from the main city is especially contentious, since "we were brought to the brink of extinction, and they are vital to rebuilding our population", and Jason offers to withdraw his dominion over parts of the city so the temples can be built (Book 12, Ch7 L46-48).
<!-- /level -->

## 5. Using this in a story

Fertility is the unromantic god of life: logistics, food and births, with a surface of ancient frankness. His church solves problems that other churches cannot, so it is rich in leverage. Use its people to supply, to rebuild and to quietly keep records, and keep its murals as a running joke that the characters take less seriously than visitors do.
