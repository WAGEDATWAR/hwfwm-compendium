## 1. Summary

Pain and Discord belong to the "hidden" gods, the gods people pray to for what they want to avoid. The books mention them only in passing. This file records every thing the text says, so that a story using them knows how much is open.

## 2. Facts

- **The hidden gods**: Carlos says many gods fall into two camps: those whose temples are anywhere there are people, and "the gods whose priests dwell in the hidden places. Gods who you pray to not because of something you want, but something you want to avoid. Pain, Discord, Deceit" (Book 8, Ch48 L51). These are the "dark gods" who "will do very well" if the Purity deception becomes public, and Carlos does not like "the idea of my actions serving the God of Discord" (Book 8, Ch48 L47).
- **Pain, a god or a goddess**: a soldier thinks of Pain as a god, but Kerr answers, "Where I come from, they know that Pain is a woman", and refers to "the Goddess of Pain's firstborn daughter" (Book 7, Ch25 L113-115). Gods are gender-fluid in the books (Book 1, Ch77 L8).
- **A byword for fear**: Trenchant says that against an affliction skirmisher he would "run like the God of Pain was chasing me" (Book 7, Ch44 L23).
- **Discord**: it is called out as "in particular" the god the deception benefits (Book 8, Ch48 L53).

## 3. Not in the text

The text gives no scene with either god, no named priest, no temple and no description of their clergy or appearance. The wiki's list names them as evil or hidden gods (wiki).

## 4. Using this in a story

Both gods work best as names that characters fear to say. If a story needs a church of Pain or Discord, it should be hidden, small and rarely seen, matching Destruction and Undeath, with priests who live where the text puts them: out of sight.
