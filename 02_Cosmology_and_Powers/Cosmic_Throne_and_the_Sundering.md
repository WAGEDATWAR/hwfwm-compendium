## 1. Summary

The Cosmic Throne was the ultimate authority that regulated the great astral beings. They broke it long ago, replaced it with a web of pacts, and in time regretted it. Jason is asked to repair it during his transition to astral king, and to leave it empty. He does so at the end of Book 11, which sets a new status quo for the cosmos and, as a side effect, makes his interface the System.

## 2. How it works

- **What it was**: "the power that regulated cosmic forces, keeping the great astral beings adherent to their respective purposes" (Book 11, Ch64 L24). Transcendent entities have effectively infinite power, and authority is "the right for a transcendent being to employ their infinite power in a specific way", which keeps the cosmos in balance; the throne was "the ultimate authority" (Book 11, Ch64 L24-25).
- **A neutral arbiter**: the throne must be left empty, "a neutral arbiter without will, only purpose", as it was before the sundering (Book 11, Ch65 L8-9). Raythe and Velius want someone who will claim the ultimate authority and then give it up without ever exercising it (Book 11, Ch65 L6).
- **The rules it enforces**: "The rules of the cosmos are enforced by something called the Cosmic Throne. It was damaged long ago, but the throne has been restored and the rules are more dangerous to bend, let alone break" (Book 12, Ch63 L9). Knowledge says the rules "have been put back in place. By you, no less" (Book 13, Ch1024 L141).
- **Gods are bound by it**: gods are bound to their worlds and last only as long as the planet they are attached to; Knowledge's crossing to Earth was only possible through the circumstances of the Builder's twin universes, which "would never have happened while the Cosmic Throne was intact" (Book 13, Ch1021 L69-71).

## 3. Variations and exceptions

- **Those who stood aside**: the great astral beings agreed to pacts instead, but not all took part. The Keeper of Moments opposed the sundering from the beginning and refused to participate (Book 11, Ch64 L47), and the Keeper, the All-Devouring Eye and the Word in the Silence "have ever stood apart from the rest, even before the sundering" (Book 11, Ch64 L49).
- **The nameless**: the original pacts did not include the nameless great astral beings, whom the named ones treated "as nobility sees servants" (Book 11, Ch65 L59). Without the throne's oversight they are "unbound from the strictures of their duties" (Book 11, Ch65 L58) and are unhappy at its restoration, but will not hunt Jason (Book 12, Ch1 L26).
- **Servants cannot do it**: "No servant of the great astral beings can be used. The pacts prevent it" (Book 11, Ch64 L83), so Dawn and other minions cannot repair the throne.
- **The chosen candidate**: the repair needs someone in one of a short subset of transcendental processes, including becoming an original, with a pre-existing connection to the Sundered Throne (Book 11, Ch64 L85-89). The great astral beings believe the All-Devouring Eye created Gordon specifically for this outcome (Book 11, Ch64 L91).

## 4. Examples from the books

- **The sundering and its cost**: after the throne was sundered some regretted it and others "revelled in their newfound freedom", including the Builder, who had always been bound by strict rules and began to experiment (Book 13, Ch1017 L9). The twin universes of Earth and Pallimustus were "a step too far", the sort of thing the throne had prevented, and the others sanctioned the Builder (Book 13, Ch1017 L9-11).
- **The pacts and why they failed**: the old Builder was sanctioned and the new one brought into the pacts together with the nameless, but this addendum "was not an effective curb on the behaviour of these late additions" (Book 11, Ch65 L61). The nameless cause many lesser problems that "fester in the dark", cascading "towards infinite anarchy" (Book 11, Ch65 L62-64).
- **The offer**: Raythe and Velius ask Jason to repair the throne while he is transcending, and the Reaper speaks through Velius to say that many of those who took part in the sundering now think revisiting it would be prudent (Book 11, Ch64 L58-66).
- **The risks**: connecting to the throne exposes his soul to intrusion, and his soul becomes a liminal space that others can enter while he is connected (Book 11, Ch65 L20, L28). The World-Phoenix, which prefers things as they are, will be his antagonist (Book 11, Ch65 L34-36).
- **Restrictions released**: after the sundering the Celestial Book locked off essence combinations, so that each had only one possible confluence; Jason's repair starts to unravel the restrictions (Book 11, Ch68 L1574-1580).
- **The World-Phoenix**: before the sundering the Boundary sanctioned itself into the World-Phoenix and the throne resisted, "a constant fight to not be turned back into what I was" (Book 11, Ch68 L1793-1795). When the throne is restored it takes the World-Phoenix's current state as the new baseline, so the pressure ends (Book 12, Ch60 L16).

## 5. Common misconceptions in-world

- **"The throne is a weapon."** It is meant to be empty; its danger is the ultimate authority, "a power that no one and nothing should have" (Book 11, Ch65 L3).
- **"Restoring it undoes the sundering."** It cannot: "The throne could never be restored to what it was. Part of you was imprinted upon it" (Book 12, Ch1 L34).
- **"Jason will rule the cosmos."** He is asked to leave it empty (Book 11, Ch65 L8). He mocks the idea in a threat to the Reaper that he will use the Cosmic Throne to give everyone in the universe ten free resurrections (Book 11, Ch68 L2409).

## 6. Changes over time

- **Book 11, the repair**: the throne room stands at the end of a hallway in a castle at the boundary of reality. The throne is split as if cleaved by an axe, the gap filled with blue and orange light, and Gordon's orbs shine and sing (Book 11, Ch68 L2522-2524). The great astral beings gather in their own prime vessels, including Raythe and Helsveth (Book 11, Ch68 L2526-2528). Jason says he is not going to make a fundamental change to the cosmos "right?" and every person with essences or their potential sees "Don't Panic. Everything is going to be fine. Welcome to the System." (Book 11, Ch68 L2533-2540).
- **Book 12, the aftermath**: his mortal aspect barely endures the task and he falls off the throne (Book 12, Ch1 L3, L22). The restoration set "a new status quo based on things as they were at the moment of restoration" (Book 12, Ch1 L28). The strictures the beings placed on the cosmos after the sundering are now being released (Book 12, Ch1 L30).
- **Book 13, the rules back in force**: Knowledge notes that the rules are in place again and that Jason, even as a great astral being, will not have the authority to sanction a god, which only gods can do (Book 13, Ch1024 L141).

## 7. Using this in a story

Treat the throne as the cosmos's fixed constitution: it limits what even great astral beings may do, and breaking the rules has consequences nobody in the books can name. Because it is empty, no one can bargain with it, and the way to get around the rules is a loophole, a pact or a trade. A story in which a character wants to "use the throne" is a story about a temptation Jason is expected to refuse.
