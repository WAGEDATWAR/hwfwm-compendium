## 1. Summary

The Builder is the great astral being of construction, whose work is forging universes. There have been two. The first built the twin universes of Earth and Pallimustus and was sanctioned for it. The second, the Builder of the present, is a mortal messenger who was raised to the office and whose authority is held on conditions. He is obsessed with physical reality, runs the cults that hunt astral spaces, and is building a universe of his own.

## 2. Facts

- **Obsession and method**: "obsessed with physical reality while, by its very nature, being unable to co-exist with it", the Builder recruits people to carve off the astral spaces connected to their world, then steps in and claims them (Book 2, Ch21 L106-112). He gathers followers rather than crossing the World-Phoenix (Book 2, Ch25 L69).
- **Tools**: star seeds, tokens, cults, constructs and clockwork animals (Book 2, Ch21 L26-36; Book 2, Ch25 L37-45).
- **The three-way meeting**: the Builder meets the Reaper and the World-Phoenix, grandly says "I will be worshipped by an entire universe... A god beyond gods" (Book 3, Ch69 L261), and is told that "we are the rules" (Book 3, Ch69 L258). The Reaper's terms bar him from interfering with Jason's birth world or producing new star seeds, tokens or vessels (Book 3, Ch69 L288-292). In return the World-Phoenix will offer Jason a power at the cost of no further resurrections (Book 3, Ch69 L295).
- **Observers**: the Builder's observer constructs accompany a group at a test, and Shade reads this as the Builder testing them (Book 3, Ch63 L45).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **The first Builder**: experimenting once the throne was sundered, he made the twin universes, one rich in magic and one poor. It was "a step too far", and the other great astral beings took his power and authority and gave it to a mortal (Book 13, Ch1017 L9-11).
- **The second Builder**: "His name is Zithis Carrow Vayel. He was a sixteen-year-old diamond-ranker when the great astral beings recruited him to replace the old Builder" (Book 10, Ch26 L55).
- **A new prime vessel**: Erigo Fin Desca, a messenger heavily modified by the Builder, with silver wings and amber glass eyes, is the new prime vessel (Book 10, Ch15 L5). He leads the move of a planet with the world engineers (Book 10, Ch15 L3-4).
- **The conditions**: the Builder says that when he was ascended, "they took precautions to control me": they can revert his mind to the state it was in at sixteen, brash, impetuous and arrogant, though he keeps his memories (Book 11, Ch68 L450-454). He believes they intend to restore his predecessor and move his authority back (Book 11, Ch68 L460).
- **His own kingdom**: he is building his own universe, "not from a seed but something different, belonging to only me", so that when the others move against him he has a refuge. He wants to be "the greatest of astral kings" (Book 11, Ch68 L460-464).
- **Why he fights**: he fights to restore the throne because the great astral beings could only do this to him by being unbound from their purposes (Book 11, Ch68 L468). Jason calls him a worse person than Thadwick Mercer, whom the Builder used as a vessel (Book 11, Ch68 L447, L467).
- **A weak replacement**: of the new Builder, Jason says "it turns out that he's... not great" (Book 13, Ch1017 L27).
<!-- /level -->

## 5. Using this in a story

The Builder is a great power with a mortal's resentments. He is arrogant, impatient and bound by pacts, and what he cannot do directly he does through cults, constructs and vessels. His plans have an expiry date because the other beings may revert his mind, and a story can use that fear.
