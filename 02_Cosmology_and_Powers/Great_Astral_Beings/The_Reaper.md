## 1. Summary

The Reaper is the great astral being of the finality of death. It is distinct from the god of Death of Pallimustus, who only guides souls into the astral. Cold, dry and a lover of rules, the Reaper is the mediator among the great astral beings, the father of the shadows that serve as familiars, and in Book 11 the reluctant opponent of Jason's plan to restore the throne. Its vessel is Velius.

## 2. Facts

- **Domain**: "The Reaper is very big on the finality of death. Some consider it the true god of death, as all our god of death governs is the passage of the soul into the astral" (Book 2, Ch25 L67).
- **Items and orders**: the Way of the Reaper is a set of skill books (Book 1, Ch50 L40), and the Order of the Reaper's teachings are collected in its legacy (Book 2, Ch60 L15). The Order of the Reaper used ancient knowledge of how to anchor such realms, "provided by the Reaper itself" (Book 2, Ch44 L91). Reaper awakening stones give mostly aggressive utility and conjuration powers (Book 2, Ch67 L45).
- **Speaking through Velius**: when the Reaper takes over its vessel, the warm voice becomes "cold and bleak" (Book 3, Ch69 L251). It tells the Builder that Jason "carried the World-Phoenix's token. Those pacts are older than you and I will not violate them" (Book 3, Ch69 L254).
- **The rules**: "We are the rules. To deny them is to deny ourselves" (Book 3, Ch69 L258). It arranges the compromise between the Builder and the World-Phoenix and makes its own concession, because it fears being made a farce if Jason is resurrected over and over (Book 3, Ch69 L284-304).
- **Shade**: "Of all my children, Shade has ever followed his own path", the Reaper says, with a brief fatherly smile (Book 3, Ch69 L305-306).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **The resurrection**: Jason says "The Reaper resurrected her, not me. The World-Phoenix convinced him because she was worried I'd go nuts and let the whole planet die" (Book 10, Ch57 L60).
- **The throne**: through Velius the Reaper says many of those who took part in the sundering now think revisiting it would be prudent (Book 11, Ch64 L66), and later fights Jason in his soul as one of the opponents (Book 11, Ch68 L2430). Its servant Velius is a fertility doctor on Earth in the aftermath (Book 11, Ch68 L776-778).
- **A joke**: Jason calls everyone to hear that the Reaper made a joke, and the Celestial Book asks whether it was any good (Book 11, Ch68 L1329-1335).
- **A walk with the World-Phoenix**: afterwards the Reaper and the World-Phoenix walk an empty road for two years (Book 11, Ch68 L2244). The World-Phoenix accuses it of raising its vessels "like pets instead of using them like tools", and it answers that she got sad when her boy left home (Book 11, Ch68 L2328-2329).
- **Sanctioning**: when Shade sanctions himself, the Reaper warns that "Sanctioning can so easily go wrong" (Book 11, Ch68 L2220).
- **Bending a rule**: Jason says the Reaper decided to "bend his own rules, one last time, before the rules were locked in once more", allowing Gary to reincarnate near people who knew him (Book 13, Ch963 L57).
- **Its children**: Shade and Umber are the Reaper's children and brothers who drifted apart (Book 13, Ch981 L37, L62-64).

## 5. Using this in a story

The Reaper is a stern parent with a dry streak. It follows its own rules strictly, bends them rarely and thinks in eons. It has no interest in grand speeches. If a story needs a great astral being to say no, or to set limits, the Reaper is the one.
