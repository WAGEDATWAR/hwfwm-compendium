## 1. Summary

The Celestial Book is the great astral being of the understanding of magic, venerated by researchers and ritualists. It gives blessings, is friendlier than the Reaper and more cheerful than most of its peers, and in the Book 11 battle it is the one great astral being who enjoys being mortal. It locked essence combinations after the sundering.

## 2. Facts

- **Veneration**: "I venerate the Celestial Book," Clive says, describing a blessing that triggered a racial gift evolution, changing the human special-attack affinity to a spell affinity, like the elves (Book 1, Ch85 L22). It is "fairly common for those of us heavily involved in magical theory" (Book 2, Ch21 L94).
- **Not a god of books**: Jason first asks if it is "the god of books, or something" (Book 1, Ch85 L26). It is named among the great astral beings interested in physical realities (Book 2, Ch21 L92).
- **Approachable**: its stones are described as "a lot more approachable than the Reaper" (Book 2, Ch56 L94).
- **A word on proportionality**: the World-Phoenix reminds the Builder, "The Celestial Book wanted me to remind you about proportionality... The ritualist is one of his", meaning Clive, and the book will not tolerate a gold-ranker sent to kill the boy out of spite (Book 3, Ch69 L278).

## 3. Later developments, Books 9 to 11

<!-- level:2 -->
- **A network**: those who venerate the Celestial Book "maintain a loose network, with ties to the Magic Society and the Church of Knowledge", and its symbol is commonly recognised (Book 9, Ch65 L28-30). A man on Jason's world venerates it (Book 9, Ch27 L21).

- **The sundering and combinations**: the Celestial Book and the Seeker of Songs "were both against the sundering from the start" (Book 11, Ch65 L75). After the sundering the Celestial Book locked off essence combinations, so that each combination has only one confluence; the restrictions unravel as Jason repairs the throne (Book 11, Ch68 L1580). A great working of authority for healing would also have involved the Celestial Book (Book 11, Ch33 L112).
- **In the jungle**: in a mortal body the Celestial Book "merrily plundered each new food cart" (Book 11, Ch68 L443), asks if the Reaper's joke was any good (Book 11, Ch68 L1333), and finds the battle "a petty squabble. It's kind of fun" (Book 11, Ch68 L174). It is delighted at the surprise of the gathering (Book 11, Ch68 L178), although it later gives Jason accusatory looks (Book 11, Ch68 L260).
- **Its warning**: it tells Jason that if he loses, his mind will be destroyed and he will be a new person with no memory (Book 11, Ch68 L227). Later it says "all of our judgements are compromised" by the vessels Jason gave them (Book 11, Ch68 L2166), and that the battle is done and he lost (Book 11, Ch68 L2158).

## 4. Using this in a story

The Celestial Book is the friendly face of the cosmos. It likes discoveries and cleverness, and it reacts to mortal pleasures with the delight of a student on a trip. A story that needs a patron for a researcher, or a great astral being who is on the player's side without being a tool, can use it. Its history of locking off essence combinations is a hook for anyone investigating why combinations are fixed.
