## 1. Summary

The World-Phoenix is the great astral being of dimensional integrity: it keeps universes from tearing, and it set Jason's journey in motion. It prefers to plant seeds rather than act, has a sense of humour, and fought Jason in his own soul in Book 11. Its prime vessels are called First Servant or First Sister, and Dawn, a former vessel, is its hierophant.

## 2. Facts

- **The mark on Jason**: his first status screens carry a symbol of a fire bird, and an ability labelled "Authority of the World Phoenix" (Book 1, Ch1 L86). Jason says the World-Phoenix gave him "a gift as his soul passed through the astral", and that he is an outworlder through "happenstance" (Book 3, Ch69 L266-267).
- **Its role**: "the integrity of dimensions is mine. It is only with my permission that you can conduct your little game" (Book 3, Ch69 L272). The Builder will not cross it (Book 2, Ch25 L69).
- **The meeting of three**: the World-Phoenix attends in a celestine vessel with alabaster skin and ruby hair, "a teasing smile and an amused twinkle in her red, gemstone eyes" (Book 3, Ch69 L262). She calls Jason "a pleasantly effective little seedling" (Book 3, Ch69 L276), and negotiates with the Reaper over terms that forbid the Builder from interfering with Jason's world (Book 3, Ch69 L288-295).

## 3. Later developments, Books 4 to 9

<!-- level:2 -->
- **How it acts**: "The World-Phoenix does not like to act directly. It prefers to set things in motion that will ultimately achieve the end it desires" (Book 4, Ch3 L66). Jason receives "the blessing of the World-Phoenix" (Book 4, Ch2 L39).
- **The cult**: its head is the First Servant, the ultimate arbiter before the World-Phoenix itself (Book 3, Ch69 L962). Dawn says she cannot sneak past the World-Phoenix (Book 5, Ch20 L52), and that it and she as its representative have always sought to sanction interference with the physical-astral boundary (Book 5, Ch66 L36).
- **Avoiding confrontation**: the World-Phoenix is "notoriously averse to direct confrontation" (Book 6, Ch35 L42). It crafts a bridge for Jason as compensation, Dawn says, "a sliver of hope in his darkest hour" (Book 6, Ch18 L65).
- **A tool that fails**: Dawn says the World-Phoenix "doesn't care about me or my wellbeing because it can't. What it can recognise is when a tool... is not functioning the way it should" (Book 8, Ch19 L54).
- **Speaking through Dawn**: at the table in Book 8, the World-Phoenix speaks through Dawn, "a lot more sultry than I expected", and thanks Jason for reminding Dawn of her mortality (Book 8, Ch54 L24-29). It grins and the vessel's eyes return to normal (Book 8, Ch54 L112).
- **Former servant**: Dawn is no longer First Sister, and her successor is Helsveth (Book 7, Ch63 L111; Book 9, Ch78 L28).

## 4. Later developments, Books 10 to 13 (spoilers)

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Not a friend in the soul**: Jason says the World-Phoenix "had to know what she was doing to me" (Book 10, Ch5 L100). In Book 11 Raythe says it prefers the current state of affairs and will be his antagonist in the battle for the throne (Book 11, Ch65 L34-36).
- **The battle**: the World-Phoenix leads a horde of monsters in Jason's soul, asks him why he struggles (Book 11, Ch68 L244, L1755), and says his will is in tatters in a matter-of-fact voice (Book 11, Ch68 L2082). When the great astral beings are given mortal vessels, it realises "he is trying to infect us with mortal morality" (Book 11, Ch68 L2175).
- **The Boundary**: she explains that before the sundering she was the Boundary, who sanctioned herself because she wanted to be more than her role (Book 11, Ch68 L1793-1795).
- **After**: for two years the World-Phoenix and the Reaper walk an empty road together, with one pause when she says "I miss food" (Book 11, Ch68 L2244-2248). When Jason arrives, she is surprised, and he talks about the Rumble in the Jungle (Book 11, Ch68 L2339-2342). The restored throne takes her current state as its baseline (Book 12, Ch60 L16).
- **In Book 12 and 13**: the cult of the World-Phoenix opens the universe to gold-rankers at Jason's request (Book 12, Ch60 L19-20), and the World-Phoenix leaves Jason a message through Helsveth, who has fought him before (Book 13, Ch1024 L90).

## 5. Using this in a story

The World-Phoenix is the patient gardener of the cosmos: it plants seeds, trims branches and tolerates a great deal so long as the dimensions hold. It speaks in a teasing, almost friendly way, and will use a friend against a friend without malice. A scene with it should leave the character feeling both looked after and managed.
