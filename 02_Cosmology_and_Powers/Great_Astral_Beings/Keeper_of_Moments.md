## 1. Summary

The Keeper of Moments, also called the Keeper of the Sands, is the great astral being that governs time. It opposed the sundering from the beginning and refused to join the pacts, and it polices attempts at time travel. Its prime vessel is Raythe, who in Book 13 bargains with Jason over what he may try to do with time.

## 2. Facts

- **Domain**: "Even the Keeper of Moments, the great astral being that governs time, cannot [affect the past]" (Book 6, Ch14 L80).

<!-- level:3 -->
- **Policing time**: it "comes down hard" on anyone who comes close to time travel, "in terms of bad ideas, even on a cosmic scale, time travel is the worst" (Book 11, Ch63 L98).
- **Names**: a speaker confesses "a soft spot for the name 'Keeper of the Sands'. I like the hourglass imagery", and Jason says he once heard the master called "the Underclock" (Book 11, Ch22 L21-22).
- **Vessel**: Lady Raythe is the prime vessel of the Keeper of Moments (Book 11, Ch64 L9). She says her master "opposed the sundering from the beginning and refused to participate in any of it", which gives her freedoms others lack (Book 11, Ch64 L47). The Keeper stands apart with the All-Devouring Eye and the Word in the Silence (Book 11, Ch64 L49).
- **Interventions**: Raythe's master took an interest in Jason (Book 11, Ch22 L29-32), and its prime vessel killed Mah Go Schaat to protect him (Book 11, Ch42 L87). Velius warns that the Keeper and the All-Devouring Eye waging war in Jason's soul would be bad even if they were on his side (Book 11, Ch65 L47).
- **Why the link was left**: Clive reasons that the Builder's synchronising of two universes' timelines can't have pleased the Keeper (Book 11, Ch63 L100).
- **Raythe on time**: she says her relationship with time is not linear. Time is "a series of interconnected points" and "any given node... can only be directly changed by those within it" (Book 13, Ch991 L7-11). She asks Jason not to attempt time travel or time looping, saying that section of the time pattern is too damaged to tell whether he would succeed or cause a cascading paradox (Book 13, Ch991 L60-62). She did not kill him earlier because eliminating the one who would repair the throne would be a massive disruption, and Dawn talked her out of it (Book 13, Ch991 L66-68).
- **An offer**: the Keeper's rules hold sway even in Jason's realm. Raythe offers to find him when no one else can (Book 13, Ch991 L84-86).

## 3. Using this in a story

The Keeper is the quiet auditor of causality. Do not make it the one who rewrites the past. It prevents others from doing so. A story with time travel will bring in Raythe or her kind, and she will be polite, patient and immovable.
